Monotonicity, Extrema and Convexity

Chapter 11 · Monotonicity, Extrema and Convexity

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by concept and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

In every exercise start from the domain and construct a sign table for $f'$ (for monotonicity and extrema) or for $f''$ (for convexity and inflection points). The checking is always done by yourselves: a test value in each interval confirms the sign, and a comparison of values confirms the type of the extremum. You do not need a solutions manual.

A. Monotonicity and critical points

First the domain, then the roots and the points where $f'$ does not exist, and finally the sign table.

11E.1 BASIC

Find the critical points and the intervals of monotonicity of $f(x)=x^{3}-12x+5$.

11E.2 INTERMEDIATE

For the function $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{x-1}$ determine the domain, the critical points and the intervals of monotonicity.

Hint: $x=1$ is not a critical point, because it does not belong to the domain; the denominator of $f'$ keeps a constant sign.

11E.3 ADVANCED

Show that $f(x)=2x+\sin x$ is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$ and conclude that the equation $2x+\sin x=1$ has exactly one real solution.

Hint: monotonicity gives uniqueness; for the existence use Bolzano’s theorem on a suitable closed interval.

B. Local extrema

Every critical point is tested either by the first derivative test or by the second derivative test.

11E.4 BASIC

Find the local extrema of $\displaystyle f(x)=x^{2}+\frac{16}{x}$, $x\neq 0$, and verify their type by the second derivative test. What happens on the branch $x<0$?

11E.5 BASIC

Find the local extremum of $f(x)=x^{4}-4x+1$ and determine its type.

11E.6 INTERMEDIATE

For $\displaystyle f(x)=\sin x+\tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x$ on $[0,2\pi]$ find all the critical points and determine which of them are local extrema.

Hint: write $\cos 2x=2\cos^{2}x-1$ and factor $f'$ with respect to $\cos x$. One of the critical points gives a double root.

C. Global extrema on a closed interval

On a closed and bounded interval compare the values at the critical points and at the two endpoints.

11E.7 BASIC

Find the global maximum and the global minimum of $f(x)=x^{3}-3x$ on $[-2,3]$.

Careful: the global minimum may be attained at more than one point.

11E.8 INTERMEDIATE

Find the global extrema of $f(x)=x+2\cos x$ on $[0,\pi]$ and give their exact values.

11E.9 INTERMEDIATE

Find the global extrema of $f(x)=(x-1)^{2/3}$ on $[0,9]$.

Hint: there is a point of the interval at which $f$ is not differentiable; this too is a candidate.

D. Optimization problems

Figure, variable, constraint, function of one variable, domain — and only then differentiation.

11E.10 BASIC

A rectangular plot of area $800\ \mathrm{m^2}$ is to be fenced. One side borders a river and requires no fence. Find the dimensions that minimize the total length of the fence and compute this length.

11E.11 INTERMEDIATE

From a square sheet of cardboard of side $12$ cm, four equal squares of side $x$ are cut from the corners and the sides are folded up, so as to form an open box. (α) Express the volume $V(x)$. (β) Find the domain of $V$. (γ) Find the $x$ that gives the maximum volume and compute it.

11E.12 ADVANCED

Find the points of the parabola $y=x^{2}$ that are at minimum distance from the point $\bigl(0,\tfrac{3}{2}\bigr)$ and compute the minimum distance.

Hint: minimize the square of the distance and set $u=x^{2}\geq 0$.

E. Convexity and concavity

The sign of $f''$ determines the position of the curve relative to its tangent lines.

11E.13 BASIC

Find the intervals of convexity and concavity of $f(x)=\mathrm{e}^{x}-x^{2}$.

11E.14 BASIC

Study the convexity and the concavity of $f(x)=\tan x$ on $\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$.

11E.15 INTERMEDIATE

Show that $f(x)=\sqrt{1+x^{2}}$ is convex on all of $\mathbb{R}$.

Hint: after simplification, $f''$ is written as a fraction with constant numerator.

F. Inflection points

It is not enough that $f''=0$: a change of sign of $f''$ on either side of the point is required.

11E.16 BASIC

Find the inflection points and the intervals of convexity and concavity of $f(x)=x+\cos x$ on $[0,2\pi]$.

11E.17 INTERMEDIATE

Show that $(0,0)$ is an inflection point of $f(x)=\sqrt[3]{x}$, although $f$ is not differentiable at $0$. What is the position of the tangent there?

11E.18 INTERMEDIATE

Determine the constants $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$ so that $f(x)=x^{3}+ax^{2}+bx$ has $(1,3)$ as an inflection point. Then verify that it is indeed an inflection point.

G. Taylor polynomials and error estimation

The polynomial gives the approximation; the Lagrange remainder gives the guarantee.

11E.19 BASIC

Find the Taylor polynomial $P_{2}(x)$ of $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{x}$ about $a=1$ and use it to approximate $\dfrac{1}{1.1}$.

11E.20 INTERMEDIATE

Find the Taylor polynomial $P_{2}(x)$ of $f(x)=\sqrt[3]{x}$ about $a=8$ and approximate $\sqrt[3]{9}$.

11E.21 ADVANCED

It is required to approximate $f(x)=\mathrm{e}^{x}$ on $[0,1]$ by a Maclaurin polynomial $P_{n}(x)$, so that the error is less than $10^{-3}$ for every $x\in[0,1]$. Find the least degree $n$ that guarantees this accuracy.

Hint: first bound $\mathrm{e}^{\xi}$ for $\xi\in[0,1]$ by a simple integer and solve the inequality for $(n+1)!$.

H. Mixed and composite problems

Here monotonicity, extrema and convexity are combined — often with parameters or with a physical interpretation.

11E.22 INTERMEDIATE

Determine the constants $a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}$ so that $f(x)=ax^{3}+bx^{2}+cx+d$ has a local maximum at the point $(-1,6)$ and a local minimum at the point $(1,2)$.

Hint: the four conditions are $f(-1)=6$, $f(1)=2$, $f'(-1)=0$, $f'(1)=0$.

11E.23 ADVANCED

Prove that $\displaystyle x-\frac{x^{2}}{2}<\ln(1+x)<x$ for every $x>0$.

Hint: study separately the monotonicity of the auxiliary functions $g(x)=x-\ln(1+x)$ and $h(x)=\ln(1+x)-x+\dfrac{x^{2}}{2}$, observing that $g(0)=h(0)=0$.

11E.24 ADVANCED

Beam. The elastic curve of a simply supported beam of length $L$ under a uniformly distributed load $q$ is given by $\displaystyle y(x)=-\frac{q}{24EI}\bigl(x^{4}-2Lx^{3}+L^{3}x\bigr)$, $0\leq x\leq L$, with $y$ measured upwards. (α) Show that the elastic curve is convex on all of $(0,L)$, and hence has no inflection point. (β) Find the position of the maximum deflection. (γ) Using the relation $EI\,y''=M$ find the position and the value of the maximum bending moment and comment on its sign.

Hint: in (β) verify that $x=L/2$ makes $y'$ vanish and justify this by the symmetry of the problem.

Exercises for the section

11E.25

Examine whether Rolle’s theorem holds for $f(x)=x^3-4x$ on $[-2,2]$ and find all the values of $c$.

11E.26

Mean value theorem (Lagrange): For $f(x)=x^3-x$ on $[-1,1]$, (α) check the hypotheses, (β) find all the $c\in(-1,1)$ such that $f'(c) = \dfrac{f(1)-f(-1)}{1-(-1)}$.

11E.27

For $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$ on $[1,4]$: apply the MVT and find $c$.

11E.28

Inequality: Prove by the MVT that $\sqrt{x+1}<1+\dfrac{x}{2}$ for $x>0$. (Hint: apply the MVT to $f(x)=\sqrt{x+1}$ on $[0,x]$.)

11E.29

Find all the local extrema of $f(x)=x^4-4x^3+4x^2$, using the first or the second derivative test.

11E.30

Monotonicity and extrema: For $f(x)=x\mathrm{e}^{-x}$ on $\mathbb{R}$: (α) find $f'(x)$, (β) determine the intervals of monotonicity, (γ) find the local maximum.

11E.31

Find the global extrema of $f(x)=\sin x+\cos x$ on $[0,2\pi]$.

11E.32

Prove that $\ln x \leq x-1$ for every $x>0$, by studying the monotonicity of a suitable auxiliary function.

11E.33

Inequality: For $f(x)=\ln x - x + 1$: (α) compute $f'(x)$ and the critical points, (β) determine the intervals of monotonicity, (γ) find the absolute maximum, (δ) conclude that $f(x)\leq0$ for every $x>0$.

11E.34

$f(x)=x^4-8x^2+7$: study of monotonicity, extrema, convexity and inflection points; sketch.

11E.35

Study the function $f(x)=2x^3+3x^2-12x+1$ completely: critical points, intervals of monotonicity, local extrema, convexity/concavity and inflection point.

11E.36

Study $f(x)=\dfrac{x}{x^2+1}$ completely: parity, monotonicity, extrema, convexity and sketch.

11E.37

Find the intervals of convexity/concavity and the inflection points of $f(x)=x^3-6x^2+9x+1$.

11E.38

Find the intervals of convexity/concavity and the inflection points of $f(x)=x^4-6x^2+5$.

11E.39

Convexity and inflection points: For $f(x)=2x^3-9x^2+12x$: (α) compute $f''(x)$, (β) find the intervals of convexity and concavity, (γ) determine the inflection point.

11E.40

Application: The elastic curve of a beam is given (in suitable units) by $y(x)=2x^3-3x^2$, $0\le x\le 2$. The sign of $y''$ determines the direction of the curvature (the sign of the bending moment). Find the point at which the direction of the curvature changes.

11E.41

Taylor and approximation: Expand $f(x)=\ln(1+x)$ about $a=0$ up to $n=4$. Approximate $\ln(1.5)$ (set $x=0.5$). What error bound does the Lagrange remainder formula give?

11E.42

Fourth-order Maclaurin polynomial of $f(x)=(1+x)^{1/2}$. Approximate $\sqrt{1.2}$ and compare with the exact value.

11E.43

Find the Taylor polynomial $P_3(x)$ of $f(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-x}$ about $a=0$ and estimate the error $|R_3(x)|$ for $x\in[0,\,1]$ by the Lagrange remainder formula.

11E.44

Third-order Taylor polynomial of $\sin x$ about $x_0=\pi/6$. Approximate $\sin(34^{\circ})$.

11E.45

Find the Taylor polynomial $P_2(x)$ of $f(x)=\arctan x$ about $a=1$: $P_2(x) = f(1) + f'(1)(x-1) + \dfrac{f''(1)}{2!}(x-1)^2$.

11E.46

A limit with Taylor: Compute $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\tan x-x}{x^3}$ using the expansion $\tan x = x + \dfrac{x^3}{3} + \ldots$

11E.53

(For engineers) Optimal cross-section. From a cylindrical log of radius $R$ a beam of rectangular cross-section of width $b$ and height $h$ is cut, with $b^2+h^2=4R^2$.
