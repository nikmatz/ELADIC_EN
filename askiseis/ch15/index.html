The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

Chapter 15 · The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by section and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

Every definite integral is evaluated with the Newton–Leibniz formula: you find one antiderivative and subtract its values at the endpoints. Always check the sign and the size of the result geometrically: if the integrand is positive on the interval, the integral must be positive and comparable with the area of a rectangle enclosing it. In a substitution always change the limits of integration as well. You do not need an answer key.

A. The first part of the fundamental theorem

Differentiation undoes integration: you do not evaluate the integral, you differentiate it.

15E.1 BASIC

Find $F'(x)$ in each of the following cases:

(α) $\displaystyle F(x)=\int_1^{x}\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\,dt$    (β) $\displaystyle F(x)=\int_0^{x}\cos(t^{3})\,dt$    (γ) $\displaystyle F(x)=\int_x^{2}e^{t^{2}}\,dt$

Hint: in (γ) the variable is in the lower limit; swap the limits first.

15E.2 INTERMEDIATE

Consider the function $\displaystyle F(x)=\int_0^{x}\frac{t-1}{t^{2}+1}\,dt$, $x\in\mathbb{R}$. Find $F'(x)$, prove that $F$ has a global minimum at $x=1$ and compute $F''(0)$.

Hint: the sign of $F'$ is determined solely by the numerator.

15E.3 ADVANCED

Evaluate the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1}{x^{3}}\int_0^{x}t\sin t\,dt$.

Hint: this is an indeterminate form $0/0$; combine L'Hôpital's rule (Ch. 12) with the first part of the fundamental theorem.

B. The Newton–Leibniz formula

One antiderivative, two values, one subtraction — you only have to watch the points where the sign changes.

15E.4 BASIC

Evaluate:

(α) $\displaystyle\int_1^{2}\left(x^{3}-\frac{1}{x^{2}}\right)dx$    (β) $\displaystyle\int_0^{1}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}$    (γ) $\displaystyle\int_0^{3}\frac{dx}{9+x^{2}}$

Hint: in (β) and (γ) use the basic-table formulas with parameter $a$.

15E.5 INTERMEDIATE

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2}\bigl|x^{2}-1\bigr|\,dx$.

Hint: first determine the sign of $x^{2}-1$ and split the interval at its zeros.

15E.6 INTERMEDIATE

Find all values of the parameter $a>0$ for which $\displaystyle\int_0^{a}(4-2x)\,dx=3$.

Hint: the computation leads to a quadratic equation in $a$; check which roots are admissible.

C. Substitution in a definite integral

When you change the variable you change the limits too; then there is no need to return to the original variable.

15E.7 BASIC

$\displaystyle\int_0^{1}x\sqrt{1+x^{2}}\,dx$

Hint: set $u=1+x^{2}$ and transform the limits $0$ and $1$.

15E.8 BASIC

$\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^{3}x\,\cos x\,dx$

15E.9 INTERMEDIATE

$\displaystyle\int_0^{4}\frac{dx}{1+\sqrt{x}}$

Hint: with $u=\sqrt{x}$ a rational expression of the form $\dfrac{2u}{1+u}$ appears; carry out the division before integrating.

D. Integration by parts in a definite integral

The term $\bigl[uv\bigr]_a^b$ is evaluated immediately at the endpoints; no constant of integration is carried along.

15E.10 BASIC

$\displaystyle\int_0^{1}x\,e^{-x}\,dx$

15E.11 INTERMEDIATE

$\displaystyle\int_1^{e}x^{2}\ln x\,dx$

Hint: choose the logarithmic factor as $u$.

15E.12 ADVANCED

$\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}e^{x}\sin x\,dx$

Hint: after two applications of the formula the original integral reappears; treat the relation as an equation in it, without forgetting the boundary terms.

E. The Leibniz rule with variable limits

When the limits are functions of $x$, the fundamental theorem is combined with the chain rule.

15E.13 BASIC

Find $F'(x)$:

(α) $\displaystyle F(x)=\int_0^{e^{x}}\ln(1+t)\,dt$    (β) $\displaystyle F(x)=\int_{x^{2}}^{4}\ln\!\left(1+t^{2}\right)dt$

15E.14 INTERMEDIATE

For the function $\displaystyle F(x)=\int_{x}^{2x}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1+t^{4}}}$ find $F'(x)$ and compute the value $F'(0)$.

Hint: both limits are variable; write the integral as a difference of two integrals with a constant lower limit.

15E.15 ADVANCED

Let $\displaystyle F(x)=\int_{x}^{x+1}t^{2}\,dt$. Find $F'(x)$ in two ways: (α) by applying the Leibniz rule and (β) by first evaluating the integral explicitly. Confirm that the two results agree and determine the global minimum of $F$ on $\mathbb{R}$.

F. Area between curves

First find the points of intersection, then which curve lies on top on each subinterval.

15E.16 BASIC

Find the area of the region enclosed by the curves $y=x^{2}$ and $y=\sqrt{x}$.

15E.17 INTERMEDIATE

Find the area of the region enclosed by the parabola $y=x^{2}-4x+3$ and the $x$-axis.

Hint: on the interval between the roots the parabola lies below the axis; area is a positive quantity.

15E.18 ADVANCED

Find the area of the region enclosed by the curves $y=|x|$ and $y=2-x^{2}$.

Hint: exploit the symmetry about the $y$-axis and work only for $x\ge 0$.

G. Applications

The integral of a rate of change gives the total accumulated quantity.

15E.19 BASIC

A body moves along a straight line with velocity $v(t)=4t-t^{2}$ (in m/s) for $0\le t\le 3$ (in s). Compute the displacement of the body over this interval and its average velocity.

Hint: check first that $v$ keeps a constant sign on $[0,3]$.

15E.20 BASIC

Find the average value of the function $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{x}$ on the interval $[1,e^{2}]$ and determine the point $c\in(1,e^{2})$ at which $f$ attains this average value, as guaranteed by the Mean Value Theorem for Integrals.

15E.21 INTERMEDIATE

The instantaneous power consumed by an installation is given by $P(t)=100+40\sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{12}\right)$ (in kW), where $t$ is the time in hours with $0\le t\le 24$. Compute the total energy consumed over a twenty-four-hour period, as well as the average power.

Hint: the sinusoidal variation completes a whole number of periods on $[0,24]$.

H. Mixed and composite problems

Often symmetry or a simple inequality reaches the answer faster than direct computation.

15E.22 INTERMEDIATE

Let $f$ be continuous on $[-a,a]$. Prove that if $f$ is even, then $\displaystyle\int_{-a}^{a}f(x)\,dx=2\int_{0}^{a}f(x)\,dx$, while if it is odd, then $\displaystyle\int_{-a}^{a}f(x)\,dx=0$. Apply the conclusion to $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x^{3}\cos x}{1+x^{2}}\,dx$.

Hint: split the integral at $0$ and in the first piece set $x=-u$.

15E.23 INTERMEDIATE

Without evaluating the integrals, prove that:

(α) $\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\le\int_0^{1}\frac{dx}{1+x^{2}}\le 1$    (β) $\displaystyle\int_0^{1}e^{-x^{2}}dx\ \ge\ \int_0^{1}e^{-x}\,dx$

Hint: in (β) compare $x^{2}$ and $x$ for $0\le x\le 1$ and use the monotonicity of the exponential.

15E.24 ADVANCED

$\displaystyle\int_0^{1}x\arctan x\,dx$

Hint: integrate by parts with $u=\arctan x$; then write $\dfrac{x^{2}}{1+x^{2}}=1-\dfrac{1}{1+x^{2}}$.
