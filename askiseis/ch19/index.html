Sequences and Infinite Series

Chapter 19 · Sequences and Infinite Series

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by topic and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

In every question about convergence state explicitly which test you are applying and check that its hypotheses are satisfied. You can verify your results numerically: compute enough partial sums $S_N$ or enough terms $a_n$ and compare with your conclusion. If the ratio or the root test gives $L=1$, it does not decide: look for another tool.

A. Limits of sequences

Isolate the dominant term and choose the right tool: division, the conjugate expression or the squeeze theorem.

19E.1 BASIC

Compute $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sqrt{4n^{2}+n}}{n+3}$.

19E.2 BASIC

Compute $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{3^{n}-2^{n}}{3^{n}+2^{n}}$.

Hint: divide numerator and denominator by the fastest growing quantity.

19E.3 INTERMEDIATE

Show by the squeeze theorem that the sequence $a_n=\sqrt[n]{2^{n}+3^{n}}$ converges and find its limit.

Hint: bound the expression $2^{n}+3^{n}$ between $3^{n}$ and $2\cdot 3^{n}$.

B. Monotonicity and bounds

The Monotone Convergence Theorem guarantees the existence of a limit without computing it.

19E.4 BASIC

Examine whether the sequence $a_n=\dfrac{n}{n^{2}+1}$ is monotone and bounded and conclude whether it converges.

Hint: study the sign of the difference $a_n-a_{n+1}$.

19E.5 INTERMEDIATE

Consider the sequence $\displaystyle a_n=\frac{1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdots(2n-1)}{2\cdot 4\cdot 6\cdots(2n)}$. Show that it is strictly decreasing and bounded below and conclude that it converges.

Hint: compute the ratio $a_{n+1}/a_n$; you do not need to find the limit.

19E.6 ADVANCED

Let $H_n=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}$ and $c_n=H_n-\ln n$. Show that $(c_n)$ is decreasing and bounded below by $0$, and conclude that it converges.

Hint: write $\ln(n+1)-\ln n=\displaystyle\int_{n}^{n+1}\frac{dx}{x}$ and compare the integral with the values of $1/x$ at the endpoints of the interval. The limit is the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

C. Recursive sequences

First a bound and monotonicity by induction, then the fixed-point equation for the limit.

19E.7 BASIC

Let $a_1=1$ and $a_{n+1}=\sqrt{3a_n}$ be given. Show by induction that $a_n<3$ for every $n$, that the sequence is increasing, and find its limit.

19E.8 INTERMEDIATE

Let $a_1=0$ and $a_{n+1}=\dfrac{1}{2-a_n}$ be given. Show that $0\le a_n<1$ for every $n$, that the sequence is increasing, and compute its limit.

Hint: for the monotonicity show that $a_{n+1}-a_n=\dfrac{(1-a_n)^{2}}{2-a_n}$.

19E.9 INTERMEDIATE

Let $x_1=1$ and $x_{n+1}=1+\dfrac{1}{x_n}$ be given. Show that $1\le x_n\le 2$ for every $n\ge 2$ and that, if the sequence converges to a limit $L>0$, then $L^{2}=L+1$. Determine the value of $L$.

Hint: the terms do not form a monotone sequence; the convergence is assumed and you are not asked to prove it.

D. Geometric and telescoping series

The only two families for which the sum is computed in closed form by means of $S_N$.

19E.10 BASIC

Examine the convergence and compute the sum of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n}+3^{n}}{6^{n}}$.

Hint: split it into two geometric series.

19E.11 BASIC

Compute the sum $\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{3^{n}}$, after first determining its first term and its ratio.

Careful with the starting index: the series does not begin at $n=0$.

19E.12 INTERMEDIATE

Show that the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2n+1}{n^{2}(n+1)^{2}}$ is telescoping, find the partial sum $S_N$ in closed form and compute its sum.

Hint: examine the difference $\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}-\dfrac{1}{(n+1)^{2}}$.

E. The comparison and integral tests

Choose a reference series (a $p$-series or a geometric one) with a general term of the same order of magnitude.

19E.13 BASIC

Using the comparison test examine the convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}+n}$.

19E.14 INTERMEDIATE

Using the limit comparison test examine the convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt{n}}{n}$.

Hint: multiply by the conjugate expression and locate the appropriate $p$-series of reference.

19E.15 INTERMEDIATE

Using the integral test examine the convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\,e^{-n^{2}}$.

Hint: first check that $f(x)=x e^{-x^{2}}$ is decreasing on $[1,+\infty)$ and then compute the improper integral by a substitution.

F. The ratio and root tests

Factorials and $n$-th powers: the ratio test for the first, the root test for the second.

19E.16 BASIC

Using the ratio test examine the convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{2}}{3^{n}}$.

19E.17 INTERMEDIATE

Using the root test examine the convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{n^{2}}{(\ln n)^{n}}$.

Hint: you will need the limit $\sqrt[n]{n}\to 1$.

19E.18 ADVANCED

Examine by the ratio test the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{n}}{3^{n}\,n!}$.

Hint: the ratio of successive terms reduces to the expression $\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n}$; the conclusion is decided by comparing $e$ with $3$, so justify explicitly whether the test decides.

G. Alternating series and absolute convergence

Two questions each time: does the series converge and does the series of absolute values converge?

19E.19 BASIC

Examine whether the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{3n-1}$ converges and decide whether the convergence is absolute or conditional.

19E.20 INTERMEDIATE

Examine whether the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,\frac{\sqrt{n}}{n+1}$ converges and decide whether the convergence is absolute or conditional.

Hint: for the monotonicity of $b_n=\dfrac{\sqrt{n}}{n+1}$ study the function $f(x)=\dfrac{\sqrt{x}}{x+1}$ on $[1,+\infty)$.

19E.21 ADVANCED

The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\bigl(\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt{n}\bigr)$ is given. (α) Examine its convergence and decide whether it is absolute or conditional. (β) Find the least number of terms $N$ for which the partial sum $S_N$ approximates the sum with an error less than $0.05$.

Hint: write $\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt{n}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt{n}}$ and use the error bound $|S-S_N|\le b_{N+1}$ of the Leibniz test.

H. Mixed and composite problems

Here the tool is not given: first identify the structure and then choose a test.

19E.22 INTERMEDIATE

Compute the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ln\!\left(n^{2}+1\right)}{\ln\!\left(n^{3}+n\right)}$.

Hint: write $\ln(n^{2}+1)=2\ln n+\ln\!\left(1+n^{-2}\right)$ and similarly for the denominator.

19E.23 ADVANCED

Examine the convergence of the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sqrt[n]{2}-1\right)$.

Hint: write $\sqrt[n]{2}=e^{(\ln 2)/n}$ and compare in the limit with the harmonic series, using the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}=1$.

19E.24 ADVANCED

Let $a_n\ge 0$ for every $n$ and suppose that the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n$ converges. Prove that the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{a_n}}{n}$ also converges.

Hint: from the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality, $\sqrt{xy}\le\frac{1}{2}(x+y)$ for $x,y\ge 0$; apply it with $x=a_n$ and $y=1/n^{2}$ and use the comparison test.

19E.31

Composite Problems

19E.35

$\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{n(n+2)}$: find the exact sum (telescoping, by means of partial fractions).

19E.36

Examine whether the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n\ln n}$ converges absolutely, conditionally, or diverges.

19E.37

19E.38

19E.39

Compute the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{n+2}{n-1}\right)^{n}$ (hint: write it as $\left(1+\tfrac{3}{n-1}\right)^{n}$).

19E.40

Examine the convergence and find the sum, where it exists:

  1. $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^n$
  2. $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty(-1)^n 2^n$
  3. $\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^\infty\frac{1}{n^2-1}$

19E.41

Apply the ratio test:

  1. $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^2}$
  2. $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n n}{2^n}$
  3. $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{n^{100}}{n!}$

19E.42

Examine the convergence:

  1. $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{n}{n^2+1}$ (integral test)
  2. $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{n^2+3n+2}$
  3. $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\sin^2 n}{n^2}$
  4. $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\sqrt{n}}{n^2+1}$

19E.43

A recursive sequence: $a_1=2$, $a_{n+1}=\dfrac{a_n+6}{2}$. Show that $a_n\to6$.

19E.44

The alternating series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{\sqrt{n}}$: convergence and estimation of the error so that $|\text{error}|<0.01$.

19E.45

Examine the monotonicity and the boundedness of the sequence $a_n=\dfrac{3n-1}{n+2}$ and compute $\lim_{n\to\infty}a_n$.

19E.46

Show that the sequence $a_n=\dfrac{2^{n}}{n!}$ converges to $0$, by examining the ratio $a_{n+1}/a_n$ of successive terms.

19E.47

Apply the ratio test to the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(n!)^2}{(2n)!}$. Does it converge or diverge?

19E.48

Apply the Leibniz test to the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}\,n}{n^2+1}$ and decide whether the convergence is absolute or conditional.

Composite Problems

19E.53

Show that for every $p>0$ the ratio test, applied to the series $\sum 1/n^p$, gives $L=1$. What do you conclude about the limits of applicability of the test? By which test can the question of convergence finally be answered?

19E.54

Application: The amplitude of oscillation of a structure decreases by $15\%$ in each cycle, starting from $A_0=20\,\mathrm{mm}$. (α) Find the sum of all the maximum amplitudes, $\sum_{n\ge0}A_n$. (β) How many cycles are required for the amplitude to drop below $1\,\mathrm{mm}$?
