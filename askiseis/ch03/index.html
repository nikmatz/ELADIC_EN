Linear Systems

Chapter 3 · Linear Systems

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by section of the chapter and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

Every result is checked by you: substitute the solution you found into all the original equations. When the solution is parametric, the verification must hold for every value of the parameter. The factorization $A=LU$ is checked with a single matrix multiplication, the rank by the number of pivots and the consistency by the Rouché–Capelli criterion, that is, by comparing $\mathrm{rank}(A)$ and $\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{A})$. You do not need a solutions manual. Throughout, $\tilde{A}=[A\,|\,\mathbf{b}]$ denotes the augmented matrix of the system.

A. Gaussian elimination and echelon form

The algorithm is always the same: reduction to echelon form, identification of the pivots, back substitution.

3E.1 BASIC

Solve by Gaussian elimination and back substitution the system $\begin{cases}x+y+2z=9\\ 2x+4y-3z=1\\ 3x+6y-5z=0\end{cases}$ and verify your solution in all three original equations.

3E.2 BASIC

Bring the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2&-1&3\\2&4&1&0\\3&6&0&3\end{bmatrix}$ to reduced row echelon form by elementary row operations. Determine the pivots, the columns in which they lie and the rank $\mathrm{rank}(A)$.

3E.3 INTERMEDIATE

Consider the system $\begin{cases}x_1+2x_2+x_3-x_4=2\\ 2x_1+4x_2+3x_3+x_4=7\\ -x_1-2x_2+x_3+5x_4=2\end{cases}$ (α) Reduce the augmented matrix to reduced row echelon form. (β) Determine the basic and the free variables. (γ) Write the general solution in the form $\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_p+c\,\mathbf{v}$.

Hint: the number of free variables follows from the difference “number of unknowns minus rank” before you even write down the solution.

B. Geometric interpretation

Each equation is a line in $\mathbb{R}^2$ or a plane in $\mathbb{R}^3$: the solution set is their intersection.

3E.4 BASIC

Examine the relative position of the planes $x+2y-z=1$ and $2x+4y-2z=5$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Does the corresponding system have a solution? Justify your answer both geometrically and by comparing $\mathrm{rank}(A)$ and $\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{A})$.

3E.5 BASIC

Consider the three lines $x+y=3$, $\;x-y=1$ and $\;2x+y=5$ of the plane. Regarding them as a $3\times 2$system, show that they have a common point and determine it. What is then $\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{A})$ and why can it not be larger?

3E.6 INTERMEDIATE

Consider the planes $x+y+z=1$, $\;x+2y+3z=2$ and $\;2x+3y+4z=3$. Show that the three planes have infinitely many common points, describe their intersection geometrically and give a parametric description of it in the form $\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_0+t\,\mathbf{v}$.

Hint: observe the relation of the third equation to the first two.

C. Consistency and parameters

Before you solve, decide whether and how many solutions there are, by comparing the two ranks with the number of unknowns.

3E.7 BASIC

For the system $\begin{cases}x+y+z=2\\ x+2y+3z=5\\ 2x+3y+4z=7\end{cases}$ compute $\mathrm{rank}(A)$ and $\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{A})$, decide by the Rouché–Capelli criterion on the number of solutions and write the general solution.

3E.8 INTERMEDIATE

Consider the system $\begin{cases}x+y+z=1\\ x+2y+4z=k\\ x+4y+10z=k^{2}\end{cases}$ with $k\in\mathbb{R}$. (α) Show that the coefficient matrix has rank $2$, independently of the value of $k$. (β) Find all the values of $k$ for which the system is consistent. (γ) For each of these values write the general solution in vector form.

Hint: after the elimination, the last row gives a quadratic condition in $k$ that factors.

3E.9 ADVANCED

Discuss fully, with respect to the parameter $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$, the system $\begin{cases}\lambda x+y+z=1\\ x+\lambda y+z=\lambda\\ x+y+\lambda z=\lambda^{2}\end{cases}$ (α) Compute the determinant of the coefficient matrix and factor it. (β) Determine the values of $\lambda$ for which there is a unique solution. (γ) For each critical value of $\lambda$ examine with the Rouché–Capelli criterion whether the system has infinitely many solutions or is inconsistent and, where solutions exist, write the general solution.

Hint: adding the three equations side by side gives a relation containing the factor $\lambda+2$.

D. Homogeneous systems

A homogeneous system always has the zero solution: the only question is whether it has others.

3E.10 BASIC

Solve the homogeneous system $\begin{cases}x+2y-3z=0\\ 2x+5y-4z=0\\ 3x+7y-7z=0\end{cases}$, write the general solution as a multiple of a vector of $\mathbb{R}^3$ and determine the dimension of $\ker(A)$.

3E.11 INTERMEDIATE

Consider the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2&1&3\\2&4&3&8\\1&2&2&5\end{bmatrix}$. (α) Find its reduced row echelon form and $\mathrm{rank}(A)$. (β) Find a basis of the solution space of $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}$. (γ) Verify the rank–nullity theorem $\dim\ker(A)+\mathrm{rank}(A)=4$.

3E.12 INTERMEDIATE

Let $A\in\mathcal{M}_{3\times 3}(\mathbb{R})$ with columns $\mathbf{c}_1,\mathbf{c}_2,\mathbf{c}_3$ for which $\mathbf{c}_3=2\mathbf{c}_1-\mathbf{c}_2$. (α) Find explicitly a nonzero vector $\mathbf{x}$ with $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}$. (β) Conclude that $\det A=0$ and that $\mathrm{rank}(A)\le 2$. (γ) Construct a specific numerical example of such a matrix and verify the above.

Hint: the product $A\mathbf{x}$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$ with coefficients the coordinates of $\mathbf{x}$.

E. Cramer's rule

It applies only to square systems with $\det A\neq 0$ and it gives each unknown separately.

3E.13 BASIC

Solve by Cramer's rule the system $\begin{cases}3x+2y=16\\ x-4y=-4\end{cases}$, after first checking that the rule applies.

3E.14 BASIC

For the system $\begin{cases}x+y+z=6\\ 2x-y+3z=9\\ x+2y-z=2\end{cases}$ compute only the unknown $z$ by Cramer's rule, without solving the whole system.

3E.15 INTERMEDIATE

Consider the system $\begin{cases}x+y=1\\ x+ay=a\end{cases}$ with $a\in\mathbb{R}$. (α) For which values of $a$ does Cramer's rule apply? (β) Apply it and show that the solution does not depend at all on $a$. (γ) Examine separately the value of $a$ that is excluded and describe geometrically what happens then with the two lines.

F. The $LU$ factorization

The multipliers of Gaussian elimination are not thrown away: they make up $L$, while the echelon matrix is $U$.

3E.16 BASIC

Find the factorization $A=LU$ (without row interchanges) of the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1&-1\\4&5&-3\\-2&5&-2\end{bmatrix}$ and verify the result by carrying out the multiplication $LU$.

3E.17 INTERMEDIATE

For the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2&1\\3&8&1\\0&4&1\end{bmatrix}$ find the factors $L$ and $U$ and, using the same factorization, solve by forward and back substitution the systems $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}_1$ and $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}_2$ with $\mathbf{b}_1=(4,12,5)^{\top}$ and $\mathbf{b}_2=(3,7,1)^{\top}$. How many times did you carry out the elimination and how many times would you have carried it out with plain Gaussian elimination?

3E.18 ADVANCED

Show that the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$ does not admit a factorization $A=LU$ with $L$ unit lower triangular and $U$ upper triangular. Then find a permutation matrix $P$ such that $PA$ does admit such a factorization, and write it down. Finally, state in general which condition on the pivots guarantees the existence of the $LU$ without row interchanges.

Hint: write $L$ and $U$ with unknown entries, carry out the product and compare entry by entry, starting from the entry in position $(1,1)$.

G. Applications: networks, trusses, interpolation

Conservation laws and interpolation conditions always produce linear systems.

3E.19 INTERMEDIATE

In a square of one-way streets, the junctions $A,B,\Gamma,\Delta$ are connected cyclically by flows (vehicles per hour) $x_1$ from $A$ to $B$, $x_2$ from $B$ to $\Gamma$, $x_3$ from $\Gamma$ to $\Delta$ and $x_4$ from $\Delta$ to $A$. From side streets there are inflows and outflows: at $A$ there is an inflow of $100$ and an outflow of $50$; at $B$ there is an inflow of $80$ and an outflow of $30$; at $\Gamma$ there is an inflow of $20$ and an outflow of $90$; at $\Delta$ there is an inflow of $40$ and an outflow of $70$. (α) Write the conservation principle (inflow $=$ outflow) at each junction and form the system $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$. (β) Compute $\mathrm{rank}(A)$ and $\mathrm{rank}(\tilde{A})$ and explain why the system has infinitely many solutions. (γ) Express the general solution with parameter the flow $x_4$. (δ) If all the flows must be nonnegative, what is the smallest admissible value of $x_4$?

3E.20 INTERMEDIATE

Find the plane with equation $z=a+bx+cy$ that passes through the points $(1,1,3)$, $(2,0,4)$ and $(0,3,2)$. (α) Form the linear system $3\times 3$ with unknowns $a,b,c$. (β) Compute the determinant of the coefficient matrix and explain why the plane is unique. (γ) Solve by Gaussian elimination and verify that all three points satisfy the equation you found.

Hint: each point gives one equation, linear in the unknown coefficients $a,b,c$ and not in the coordinates.

3E.21 ADVANCED

In a plane truss three members carry forces $F_1,F_2,F_3$ (in kN). The equilibrium equations of the joints, with the geometry of member $1$ corresponding to a right triangle with sides $3,4,5$, are $\begin{cases}\tfrac{3}{5}F_1+F_2=10\\ \tfrac{4}{5}F_1+F_3=12\\ F_2-F_3=-4\end{cases}$ (α) Multiply suitably so that the coefficients become integers and solve by Gaussian elimination. (β) Show that the determinant of the coefficient matrix is nonzero and explain what this means for whether the truss is statically determinate. (γ) Interpret physically the sign of each force, given that a positive value corresponds to tension and a negative one to compression.

H. Mixed and composite problems

Here several tools of the chapter are combined: rank, determinant, consistency, homogeneous system.

3E.22 INTERMEDIATE

We are given $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2&3\\2&5&7\\3&7&11\end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{b}=(6,14,21)^{\top}$. (α) Reduce $A$ to echelon form and compute $\det A$ as the product of the pivots. (β) Solve $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ using the same reduction. (γ) Explain why the answer to (α) guarantees in advance that the solution of (β) is unique.

3E.23 ADVANCED

Let $A\in\mathcal{M}_{m\times n}(\mathbb{R})$ and $\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^m$. Suppose that the system $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ has two different solutions $\mathbf{x}_1\neq\mathbf{x}_2$. (α) Show that for every $t\in\mathbb{R}$ the vector $\mathbf{x}_1+t(\mathbf{x}_2-\mathbf{x}_1)$ is also a solution. (β) Conclude that the system has infinitely many solutions and hence that a linear system can never have exactly two solutions. (γ) State this conclusion in terms of $\mathrm{rank}(A)$ and of the number $n$ of unknowns.

Hint: in (α) compute $A\big(\mathbf{x}_1+t(\mathbf{x}_2-\mathbf{x}_1)\big)$ using the linearity of matrix multiplication.

3E.24 ADVANCED

Consider, for $a\in\mathbb{R}$, the system $A_a\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ with $A_a=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&-1\\2&3&a\\1&a&3\end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{b}=(1,2,1)^{\top}$. (α) Compute $\det A_a$, factor it and find the values of $a$ for which it vanishes. (β) For which values of $a$ does the system have a unique solution? (γ) For each of the critical values of $a$ apply the Rouché–Capelli criterion and, where the system is consistent, write the general solution in vector form. (δ) Observe that the homogeneous system $A_a\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}$ has nonzero solutions exactly for the critical values, and explain the relation of these solutions to the general solution of part (γ).

3E.52

System with parameters. Consider the system \[ \begin{cases} x + 2y + z = 1 \\ 2x + 5y + 2z = 2 \\ x + 4y + \lambda z = \mu \end{cases} \]

  3. For $\lambda=1$ and the value of $\mu$ that gives infinitely many solutions, write the general solution.
