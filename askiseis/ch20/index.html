Power Series and Taylor Series

Chapter 20 · Power Series and Taylor Series

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by topic and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

For every power series you should always make three things clear: the center, the radius of convergence and the behavior at the two endpoints, which are examined separately. Every expansion you find you can check numerically, by substituting a small value of $x$ and comparing the partial sum with the value of the function.

A. Radius and interval of convergence

The ratio test gives the radius; the endpoints always require a separate check.

20E.1 BASIC

Find the radius and the interval of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{3^{n}\sqrt{n}}$, examining both endpoints separately.

Hint: at the endpoints you obtain a $p$-series and an alternating series.

20E.2 BASIC

Find the radius and the interval of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}\,2^{n}x^{n}}{n}$ and make clear at which endpoint the convergence is conditional and not absolute.

20E.3 INTERMEDIATE

For the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(3x-1)^{n}}{n^{2}}$ determine the center, the radius and the set of convergence with respect to $x$.

Hint: first write $3x-1=3\left(x-\tfrac13\right)$; the radius with respect to $x$ does not coincide with the radius with respect to the quantity $3x-1$.

B. Taylor and Maclaurin expansions

Instead of successive differentiations, start wherever possible from a known series.

20E.4 BASIC

Find the Maclaurin series of $f(x)=x^{2}\sin x$ in general-term form, write the first three nonzero terms and give the radius of convergence.

20E.5 BASIC

Find the first four nonzero terms of the Maclaurin series of $f(x)=\ln(1+2x)$, as well as the radius of convergence and the behavior at the endpoints.

Hint: substitute $u=2x$ in the already known series of $\ln(1+u)$.

20E.6 INTERMEDIATE

Find the Taylor expansion of $f(x)=e^{-x}$ about $a=2$ in general-term form and justify why its radius of convergence is infinite.

C. Operations with power series

Substitution and multiplication of series save you from computing endless derivatives.

20E.7 BASIC

By a suitable substitution in a known series, find the Maclaurin series of $f(x)=\cos\!\left(x^{2}\right)$ and its radius of convergence.

20E.8 INTERMEDIATE

By multiplying the Maclaurin series of $e^{x}$ and $\cos x$, find all the terms up to and including $x^{3}$ of the expansion of $g(x)=e^{x}\cos x$.

Hint: the coefficient of $x^{k}$ in the product is obtained by summing all the products of coefficients whose indices add up to $k$.

20E.9 INTERMEDIATE

Find the terms up to and including $x^{3}$ of the Maclaurin series of $\displaystyle h(x)=\frac{\sin x}{1-x}$ and justify why the radius of convergence is $R=1$.

Hint: write the factor $\frac{1}{1-x}$ as a geometric series and multiply by the Cauchy product.

D. Differentiation and integration of power series

Inside the open interval of convergence, term-by-term differentiation and integration are allowed and preserve the radius.

20E.10 BASIC

By expanding $\dfrac{1}{1+t^{3}}$ in a geometric series and integrating term by term, express $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\frac{dt}{1+t^{3}}$ as a power series and give its radius of convergence.

Hint: watch the sign — the denominator is $1+t^{3}$, so the expansion is alternating.

20E.11 INTERMEDIATE

Identify the sum $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n\,3^{n}}$ as the value of a known power series at a suitable point and compute it in closed form. First check that the point belongs to the interval of convergence.

20E.12 ADVANCED

Consider the function $\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2n+1}}{2n+1}$ for $|x|<1$. (α) Show, by differentiating term by term, that $f'(x)=\dfrac{1}{1-x^{2}}$. (β) By integration and the condition $f(0)=0$, find a closed form for $f$. (γ) Examine the behavior of the series at the endpoints $x=\pm1$.

Hint: in (β) use a decomposition into partial fractions — note that here the denominator is $1-x^{2}$ and not $1+x^{2}$.

E. The Lagrange remainder and error estimation

An approximation without an error bound is not an engineering result.

20E.13 BASIC

Bound by the Lagrange remainder the error of the approximation $e^{x}\approx 1+x+\dfrac{x^{2}}{2}$ for $0\le x\le \tfrac12$.

Hint: the exponential is increasing, so $e^{\xi}$ is bounded by its value at the right endpoint.

20E.14 INTERMEDIATE

Show that for every $x\in[0,1]$ we have $\displaystyle\left|\cos x-\left(1-\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\frac{x^{4}}{24}\right)\right|\le\frac{1}{720}$, using the Lagrange remainder.

Hint: all the derivatives of the cosine are bounded in absolute value by one.

20E.15 ADVANCED

Let $f(x)=\ln(1+x)$. (α) Write the Lagrange remainder $R_n(x)$ of the expansion about $0$, first expressing $f^{(n+1)}$. (β) Show that for $0\le x\le1$ we have $|R_n(x)|\le\dfrac{1}{n+1}$. (γ) Conclude that the Maclaurin series converges to $\ln(1+x)$ on all of $[0,1]$.

Hint: in (β) use the fact that $\xi$ lies between $0$ and $x$, so that $1+\xi\ge1$.

F. Linearization in engineering

Every “small-quantity formula” of engineering consists of the first terms of a Taylor expansion.

20E.16 BASIC

The thermal expansion of a rod is described exactly by $L(T)=L_{0}e^{\alpha(T-T_{0})}$. Expand in a series with respect to $u=\alpha(T-T_{0})$ and show that the linearization gives the usual formula $L\approx L_{0}\left[1+\alpha(T-T_{0})\right]$. Estimate the relative error of the linearization when $u=10^{-2}$.

20E.17 INTERMEDIATE

The exact period of a simple pendulum with amplitude $\theta_{0}$ is given by $T=T_{0}\left[1+\tfrac14\sin^{2}\tfrac{\theta_{0}}{2}+\cdots\right]$, where $T_{0}=2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$. By replacing the sine with its expansion, show that for small amplitudes $T\approx T_{0}\left(1+\tfrac{\theta_{0}^{2}}{16}\right)$ and compute the relative increase of the period for $\theta_{0}=20^{\circ}$.

Hint: first convert the angle into radians.

20E.18 INTERMEDIATE

The curvature of the elastic curve of a beam is $\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{y''}{\left(1+(y')^{2}\right)^{3/2}}$. Using the binomial series show that for small slopes $\kappa\approx y''\left(1-\tfrac{3}{2}(y')^{2}\right)$ and explain why the linear theory of beams adopts the further simplification $\kappa\approx y''$.

Hint: set $u=(y')^{2}$ and expand $(1+u)^{-3/2}$.

G. Fourier series

The symmetry of the function makes half of the coefficients vanish in advance.

20E.19 BASIC

For the $2\pi$-periodic function with $f(x)=x$ for $-\pi<x<\pi$, justify from the symmetry that $a_{n}=0$ for every $n$ and compute the coefficients $b_{n}$.

Hint: the integral $\int_{0}^{\pi}x\sin(nx)\,dx$ is computed by parts.

20E.20 INTERMEDIATE

Consider the $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x)=|x|$, $-\pi\le x\le\pi$. (α) Explain why $b_{n}=0$ for every $n$ and compute $a_{0}$ and $a_{n}$. (β) By setting $x=0$ in the expansion, find the sum of $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{2}}$.

Hint: in (β) $x=0$ is a point of continuity, so the Dirichlet conditions guarantee equality.

20E.21 ADVANCED

A periodic square pulse of period $2L$ is defined by $f(x)=1$ for $|x|<c$ and $f(x)=0$ for $c<|x|<L$, with $0<c<L$. (α) Explain why $b_{n}=0$. (β) Compute $a_{0}$ and $a_{n}$ as functions of $c$ and $L$. (γ) To which value does the Fourier series converge at the point $x=c$?

Hint: in (γ) refer to the statement of the Dirichlet conditions at a point of discontinuity.

H. Mixed / composite problems

Here the method is not given: first identify which known series is hidden behind the expression.

20E.22 INTERMEDIATE

Compute the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\tan x-\sin x}{x^{3}}$ by replacing each function with its Maclaurin expansion.

Hint: we have $\tan u=u+\tfrac{u^{3}}{3}+\cdots$; the terms up to and including $x^{3}$ suffice.

20E.23 ADVANCED

Find the first three nonzero terms of the Maclaurin series of $f(x)=\ln(\cos x)$ and use them to compute the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\ln(\cos x)}{x^{2}}$.

Hint: write $\cos x=1-u$ with $u=\tfrac{x^{2}}{2}-\tfrac{x^{4}}{24}+\cdots$ and expand $\ln(1-u)$, keeping all the terms that contribute up to the order you need.

20E.24 ADVANCED

Consider the function $\displaystyle y(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}x^{2n}}{4^{n}\,(n!)^{2}}$. (α) Show by the ratio test that the radius of convergence is infinite. (β) By differentiating term by term and shifting the index suitably, verify that $y$ satisfies the equation $x\,y''+y'+x\,y=0$.

Hint: in (β) collect all the terms with the same power of $x$ and require that every coefficient vanish.
