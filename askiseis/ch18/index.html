Improper Integrals

Chapter 18 · Improper Integrals

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by type of singularity and by convergence test and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

Before every computation locate exactly where the singularity lies: an infinite limit of integration, an unbounded integrand, or both. Then write the improper integral as a limit of definite integrals over bounded intervals, where the function is continuous, and answer the question of convergence first; the numerical value has meaning only if the limit exists.

A. Infinite limits of integration

Replace the infinite limit by a finite $b$ and examine whether the limit exists as $b\to+\infty$.

18E.1 BASIC

Examine the convergence and compute the value of $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}e^{-3x}\,dx$.

18E.2 BASIC

Examine the convergence and compute the value of $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}\frac{dx}{(x+1)^2}$.

18E.3 INTERMEDIATE

Examine the convergence and compute the value of $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dx}{x^2+2x+2}$.

Hint: first complete the square in the denominator and split the integral into two improper integrals, each with only one infinite limit.

18E.4 INTERMEDIATE

Show that $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}\cos x\,dx$ diverges, although the integrand is bounded on all of $[0,+\infty)$.

Hint: compute $\displaystyle\int_0^{b}\cos x\,dx$ as a function of $b$ and examine its behavior for $b\to+\infty$. Which property of the limit is violated?

B. Unbounded integrands

Locate the point at which the function blows up and approach it by a limit from the interior of the interval.

18E.5 BASIC

Examine the convergence and compute the value of $\displaystyle\int_0^{4}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{4-x}}$.

Hint: the singularity is at the upper limit.

18E.6 BASIC

Examine the convergence and compute the value of $\displaystyle\int_0^{1}\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx$.

18E.7 INTERMEDIATE

Examine the convergence and compute the value of $\displaystyle\int_0^{1}\ln x\,dx$.

Hint: find an antiderivative by integration by parts and examine separately the limit of the boundary term as the lower limit tends to $0^{+}$.

18E.8 ADVANCED

Examine the convergence and, if it converges, compute the value of $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{8}\frac{dx}{\sqrt[3]{x^2}}$.

Hint: the singularity is interior. Split at $0$ and require that both improper integrals converge separately; the integrand is defined for negative $x$ as well.

C. The $p$-integrals

The test for $p$-integrals answers the question of convergence without any computation — it is enough to see which limit creates the problem.

18E.9 BASIC

Classify as convergent or divergent, without computing any value: (α) $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty}\frac{dx}{x^{5/4}}$, (β) $\displaystyle\int_0^{1}\frac{dx}{x^{5/4}}$, (γ) $\displaystyle\int_0^{1}\frac{dx}{x^{4/5}}$.

Hint: in each case first identify whether it is an integral of Type I or of Type II; the condition on $p$ is reversed.

18E.10 INTERMEDIATE

Show that $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}\frac{dx}{x^{p}}$ diverges for every value of $p$.

Hint: split at $1$ and write the convergence condition imposed separately by each of the two pieces. Can the two conditions hold simultaneously?

18E.11 ADVANCED

Examine the convergence and compute the value of $\displaystyle\int_0^{1/2}\frac{dx}{x\,(\ln x)^2}$.

Hint: the singularity is at $0^{+}$ and lies in the borderline case $p=1$, where the power of $x$ does not by itself decide. Try the substitution $u=\ln x$ and note where the limits go.

D. The comparison test

Find a simpler function that bounds the integrand from above (for convergence) or from below (for divergence) over the whole interval.

18E.12 BASIC

Show that $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty}\frac{dx}{x^3+x}$ converges, without computing it.

Hint: for $x\ge 1$ we have $x^3+x>x^3$.

18E.13 INTERMEDIATE

Show that $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty}\frac{2+\sin x}{x^2}\,dx$ converges.

Hint: bound the numerator and confirm that the integrand is nonnegative, so that the comparison test applies.

18E.14 INTERMEDIATE

Show that $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty}\frac{dx}{x+\ln x}$ diverges.

Hint: for $x\ge 1$ we have $0\le\ln x\le x$; bound the denominator from above and compare with an integral of type $p$ with $p=1$.

E. The limit comparison test

Identify the dominant behavior of the integrand near the singularity and compare it in the limit with a suitable power.

18E.15 BASIC

Examine the convergence of $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty}\frac{x+1}{x^3+2}\,dx$ by the limit comparison test.

Hint: keep the highest-degree terms of the numerator and the denominator in order to guess the comparison function.

18E.16 INTERMEDIATE

Examine the convergence of $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty}\frac{x^2+3}{x^3+x+1}\,dx$ and justify fully the choice of the comparison function.

Hint: first confirm that the denominator does not vanish for $x\ge 1$, so that the integral is improper only because of the infinite limit.

18E.17 INTERMEDIATE

Examine the convergence of $\displaystyle\int_0^{1}\frac{dx}{e^{x}-1}$.

Hint: the singularity is at $0^{+}$. Compute $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}\frac{e^{x}-1}{x}$ and compare in the limit with an integral of type $p$ on $(0,1]$.

F. Absolute convergence

If the integral of the absolute value converges, then the original one converges too — and indeed absolutely.

18E.18 BASIC

Show that $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}e^{-x}\cos(2x)\,dx$ converges absolutely, without computing its value.

Hint: bound $|\cos(2x)|$ and compare with an exponential integral.

18E.19 INTERMEDIATE

Show that $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x^{3/2}}\,dx$ converges absolutely and explain why the comparison test cannot be applied directly to the integrand itself.

Hint: the comparison test requires nonnegative functions; what sign does $\sin x$ have on $[1,+\infty)$?

G. The gamma function and the Laplace transform

The $\Gamma$ and the Laplace transform are improper integrals that depend on a parameter; the convergence must be stated together with the result.

18E.20 BASIC

Express by means of the $\Gamma$ function and compute $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}x^{4}e^{-x}\,dx$.

Hint: identify the exponent with the form $t^{x-1}$ of the definition and use the relation $\Gamma(n+1)=n!$.

18E.21 INTERMEDIATE

With the substitution $t=x^2$, express $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}e^{-x^2}\,dx$ by means of the $\Gamma$ function and, accepting that $\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}$, find its value.

Hint: from $t=x^2$ we get $x=\sqrt{t}$ and $dx=\frac{1}{2}t^{-1/2}\,dt$; the new integral is improper at both limits, so justify its convergence.

18E.22 ADVANCED

Compute from the definition the Laplace transform $\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\{\cos(\omega t)\}=\int_0^{+\infty}e^{-st}\cos(\omega t)\,dt$, where $\omega\in\mathbb{R}$ is a constant, determining for which values of $s$ the integral converges.

Hint: two successive integrations by parts bring back the original integral; treat it as an equation. Justify separately the vanishing of the boundary terms.

H. Mixed — composite singularities

Here there is more than one singularity: split correctly before every computation.

18E.23 ADVANCED

Examine the convergence and compute the value of $\displaystyle\int_1^{+\infty}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{x^2-1}}$.

Hint: the integral is improper at both limits; split it at an interior point, e.g. at $x=2$. For the antiderivative try the trigonometric substitution $x=\sec\theta$ and watch the new limits.

18E.24 ADVANCED

Examine the convergence and compute the value of $\displaystyle\int_0^{+\infty}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}\,(1+x)}$.

Hint: before any substitution, examine separately the behavior of the integrand near $0^{+}$ and for $x\to+\infty$ by means of integrals of type $p$. Then try $u=\sqrt{x}$.
