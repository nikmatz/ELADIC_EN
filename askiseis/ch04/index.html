Linear Transformations

Chapter 4 · Linear Transformations

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by section of the chapter and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

Unless stated otherwise, vectors are written as columns and matrices are taken relative to the standard basis. Every result is checked by you: the matrix of a transformation is checked by applying it to the basis vectors, the kernel and the image by the rank–nullity theorem, the inverse by composition and the geometric character by a rough sketch. You do not need a solutions manual.

A. Testing for linearity

Linearity is proved for arbitrary vectors, while it is refuted by a single counterexample.

4E.1 BASIC

Examine which of the following maps are linear transformations:
(α) $T(x,y)=(3x-2y,\ y)$,   (β) $T(x,y)=(x,\ |y|)$,   (γ) $T(x,y,z)=(x-z,\ 0,\ 2y)$,   (δ) $T(x,y)=(x+2,\ y-1)$.

Hint: start from the image of the zero vector, it is the fastest test.

4E.2 INTERMEDIATE

In the vector space $M_{2\times2}(\mathbb{R})$ of $2\times2$ matrices consider the maps $T(A)=\mathrm{tr}(A)$ and $S(A)=\det(A)$, with values in $\mathbb{R}$. Show that $T$ is linear, while $S$ is not.

Hint: for $S$ examine homogeneity, that is, the relation between $S(cA)$ and $c\,S(A)$.

4E.3 ADVANCED

Let $T:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m$ be linear. Prove by induction on $k$ that $\displaystyle T\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_i\mathbf{v}_i\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_i\,T(\mathbf{v}_i)$ for all $c_1,\dots,c_k\in\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbf{v}_1,\dots,\mathbf{v}_k\in\mathbb{R}^n$. Conclude that a linear transformation is completely determined by its values on the vectors of a basis.

B. The matrix of a linear transformation

The columns of the matrix are nothing other than the images of the basis vectors of the domain.

4E.4 BASIC

Find the matrix of $T:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^3$ with $T(x,y)=(2x-y,\ x+3y,\ y)$ relative to the standard bases, state its dimensions and compute $T(1,-2)$.

4E.5 BASIC

The transformation $T:\mathbb{R}^4\to\mathbb{R}^2$ is defined by $T(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=(x_1-x_3,\ x_2+2x_4)$. Write its matrix relative to the standard bases and compute $T(1,0,1,2)$.

4E.6 INTERMEDIATE

Let $D:\mathcal{P}_2\to\mathcal{P}_1$ be the differentiation map, $D(p)=p'$. (α) Show that $D$ is linear. (β) Find its matrix relative to the bases $\{1,x,x^2\}$ of $\mathcal{P}_2$ and $\{1,x\}$ of $\mathcal{P}_1$. (γ) Use the matrix to compute the derivative of $p(x)=4-3x+2x^2$ and verify the result by direct differentiation.

C. Kernel and image

The kernel is the solution set of a homogeneous system, while the image is spanned by the columns of the matrix.

4E.7 BASIC

For $T:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^2$ with $T(x,y,z)=(x+y+z,\ x+y+z)$ find a basis of $\ker T$, a basis of $\mathrm{Im}\,T$ and verify the rank–nullity theorem.

4E.8 INTERMEDIATE

Let $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&1\\1&0&-1\end{bmatrix}$ and $T(\mathbf{x})=A\mathbf{x}$. Find a basis of $\ker T$, a basis of $\mathrm{Im}\,T$ and the rank of $A$. Is $T$ one-to-one?

Hint: observe the relation of the third row to the first two before you begin the elimination.

4E.9 INTERMEDIATE

Let $T:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m$ be linear and $\mathbf{v}_1,\dots,\mathbf{v}_k\in\mathbb{R}^n$. Show that if $T(\mathbf{v}_1),\dots,T(\mathbf{v}_k)$ are linearly independent, then so are $\mathbf{v}_1,\dots,\mathbf{v}_k$. Does the converse hold?

Hint: for the converse look for a counterexample with a transformation that has a nontrivial kernel.

4E.10 ADVANCED

A linear transformation $T:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^3$ is sought with $\ker T=\mathrm{span}\{(1,1,1)\}$ and $\mathrm{Im}\,T$ the plane $x+y+z=0$. (α) Check by the rank–nullity theorem that the two requirements are compatible. (β) Construct explicitly a matrix $A$ that satisfies them and justify the choice of its columns. (γ) Is $T$ onto $\mathbb{R}^3$?

D. Composition of transformations

Composition corresponds to a product of matrices, with the order of the factors reversed relative to the order of application.

4E.11 BASIC

Let $T:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^2$ have matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-1\\2&1&0\end{bmatrix}$ and $S:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ matrix $B=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$. Find the matrix of $S\circ T$ and explain why the composition $T\circ S$ is not defined.

4E.12 INTERMEDIATE

The transformation $T:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^3$ is defined by $T(\mathbf{e}_1)=\mathbf{e}_2$, $T(\mathbf{e}_2)=\mathbf{e}_3$ and $T(\mathbf{e}_3)=\mathbf{e}_1$. (α) Write the matrix $C$ of $T$. (β) Compute $C^2$ and $C^3$. (γ) Find the smallest positive integer $k$ with $C^k=I$ and interpret the result.

4E.13 ADVANCED

Let $T:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ be linear with $T\circ T=0$ and $T\neq 0$. (α) Show that $\mathrm{Im}\,T\subseteq\ker T$. (β) Using the rank–nullity theorem show that $\mathrm{Im}\,T=\ker T$. (γ) Construct a $2\times2$ matrix with this property.

E. Geometric transformations of the plane

Every $2\times2$ matrix tells a geometric story: rotation, reflection, scaling, projection or shear.

4E.14 BASIC

Describe geometrically the transformation $T(\mathbf{x})=A\mathbf{x}$ for:
(α) $A=\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}$,   (β) $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&3\end{bmatrix}$,   (γ) $A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{bmatrix}$.

Hint: follow the images of $\mathbf{e}_1$ and $\mathbf{e}_2$ and draw them.

4E.15 BASIC

Write the matrix of the counterclockwise rotation by $45^\circ$ and compute the images of the vectors $(\sqrt{2},0)^{T}$ and $(0,\sqrt{2})^{T}$.

4E.16 INTERMEDIATE

A figure in the plane is first rotated by $90^\circ$ and then scaled by a factor $2$ along the $x$ axis and $\tfrac{1}{2}$ along the $y$ axis. (α) Find the overall matrix. (β) Compute its determinant and deduce what happens to the area of the figure.

Hint: pay attention to the order of the factors in the product of the matrices.

F. Isomorphism and change of basis

The same map changes matrix when the basis changes, and the relation connecting the two matrices is similarity.

4E.17 BASIC

Show that $T:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^3$ with $T(x,y,z)=(x+y,\ y+z,\ z)$ is an isomorphism and find the matrix of the inverse transformation.

4E.18 INTERMEDIATE

Show that the set $\mathcal{B}=\{(1,1,0),\,(0,1,1),\,(1,0,1)\}$ is a basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$ and find the coordinates $[\mathbf{v}]_{\mathcal{B}}$ of $\mathbf{v}=(2,4,2)^{T}$.

Hint: for the first part a single determinant suffices.

4E.19 INTERMEDIATE

The transformation $T:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ has, relative to the standard basis, the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}4&-2\\1&1\end{bmatrix}$. For the basis $\mathcal{B}=\{(1,1),\,(2,1)\}$ compute the matrix $[T]_{\mathcal{B}}=P^{-1}AP$, where $P$ is the matrix whose columns are the vectors of $\mathcal{B}$. Comment on the form that results and on what it means geometrically for the two directions of $\mathcal{B}$.

G. Applications to computer graphics

In homogeneous coordinates translation too becomes a multiplication by a $3\times3$ matrix.

4E.20 BASIC

Write in homogeneous coordinates the $3\times3$ matrices (α) of the translation by $(3,-2)$ and (β) of the scaling by a factor $2$ along both axes. Apply each of them separately to the point $(1,4)$.

4E.21 INTERMEDIATE

Consider the reflection in the $y$ axis followed by a translation by $(4,0)$. (α) Write the overall $3\times3$ matrix in homogeneous coordinates. (β) Apply it to the triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(0,2)$. (γ) Show that the overall transformation is a reflection in a vertical line and determine it.

Hint: write the formula $(x,y)\mapsto(\ldots,\ldots)$ given by the overall matrix and look for the fixed points.

H. Mixed and composite problems

Here matrix, kernel, image, determinant and geometry are combined in the same exercise.

4E.22 INTERMEDIATE

The linear transformation $T:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ maps the unit square with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(1,1)$, $(0,1)$ onto the parallelogram with vertices $(0,0)$, $(2,1)$, $(3,3)$, $(1,2)$ respectively. (α) Find the matrix of $T$. (β) Compute the area of the image. (γ) Is $T$ invertible?

4E.23 ADVANCED

Let $A=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}1&\sqrt{3}\\ \sqrt{3}&-1\end{bmatrix}$. (α) Show that $A^{2}=I$ and that $\det A=-1$. (β) Determine all vectors $\mathbf{x}$ with $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}$. (γ) Determine all vectors $\mathbf{x}$ with $A\mathbf{x}=-\mathbf{x}$ and interpret the transformation geometrically.

Hint: in parts (β) and (γ) write the required relation as a homogeneous linear system $(A\mp I)\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}$.

4E.24 ADVANCED

(α) Show that there is no linear transformation $T:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^3$ with $\ker T=\mathrm{Im}\,T$. (β) Show that in $\mathbb{R}^4$ such a transformation does exist, by constructing its matrix explicitly and verifying both requirements.

Hint: what does the rank–nullity theorem say about the dimension of the domain, if the two subspaces coincide?

Exercises for the section

4E.25

Find $\ker(T)$ and $\mathrm{Im}(T)$ for $T\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}x-y\\2x-2y\end{bmatrix}$.

4E.26

If $T: \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ and $\dim(\ker T)=2$, find $\dim(\mathrm{Im}\,T)$.

4E.27

Let $T(x,y,z)=(x+y,\, 2z)$. Find $\dim(\ker T)$, $\dim(\mathrm{Im}\,T)$ and verify the rank–nullity theorem.

4E.28

Show that the transformation $T(x,y)=(x+y, 2x-y)$ is linear. Find its representation matrix relative to the standard basis.

4E.29

For the transformation $T:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ with $T(1,0)=(2,3)$ and $T(0,1)=(-1,4)$, compute $T(3,2)$ and the representation matrix.

4E.30

If $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}$, describe geometrically $T(\mathbf{x})=A\mathbf{x}$.

4E.31

Find the matrix of $T_2 \circ T_1$ for $A_1=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{bmatrix}$, $A_2=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\3&1\end{bmatrix}$.

4E.32

Find the matrix of the composition: first a rotation by $90°$, then a reflection in the line $y=x$.

4E.33

If $T_1$ has matrix $\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{bmatrix}$ and $T_2$ has matrix $\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$, find the matrix of the composition $T_2 \circ T_1$ and compare it with that of $T_1\circ T_2$.

4E.34

Apply a rotation by $30°$ to the vector $\mathbf{v}=(2,0)^T$.

4E.35

Show that the projection $P=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$ satisfies $P^2=P$ and find its eigenvectors.

4E.36

Find the matrix of the reflection in the line $y=-x$ and check that its square is the identity matrix.

4E.37

Show that $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $T(x,y)=(x+y, x-y)$ is an isomorphism.

4E.38

If $\mathbf{b}_1=\begin{bmatrix}2\\1\end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf{b}_2=\begin{bmatrix}1\\3\end{bmatrix}$, find the change-of-basis matrix $P$ and $P^{-1}$.

4E.39

For $A=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$ (a rotation by $90°$), find the matrix relative to the basis $\mathcal{B}=\{(1,1),(1,-1)\}/\sqrt{2}$.

4E.40

Let $\mathcal{B}=\{(1,2),(3,5)\}$ be a basis of $\R^2$. Find the coordinates $[\mathbf{v}]_{\mathcal{B}}$ of $\mathbf{v}=(4,9)^T$ and explain why the map $\mathbf{v}\mapsto[\mathbf{v}]_{\mathcal{B}}$ is an isomorphism.

4E.41

The transformation $T:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ has $[T]_{\mathcal{B}}=\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}$ relative to the basis $\mathcal{B}=\{(1,1),(1,-1)\}$. Find the matrix $A$ of $T$ relative to the standard basis (the reverse direction: $A=P[T]_{\mathcal{B}}P^{-1}$).

4E.42

A transformation $T$ satisfies $T(1,0)=(3,1)$ and $T(1,1)=(4,3)$. (α) Find its matrix relative to the standard basis. (β) Is it invertible? (γ) Compute $T^{-1}(1,1)$.

4E.43

Write the $3\times3$ matrix (homogeneous coordinates) for a rotation by an angle $\theta=30°$ about the origin. Apply it to the point $(\sqrt{3},1)^T$.

4E.44

Write the matrix of an affine transformation in homogeneous coordinates: a translation by $(2,3)$ and then a scaling by $(2,1)$.

4E.45

Rotate the point $(1,0)$ by $45°$ about the point $(1,1)$ (translation, rotation, inverse translation).

Composite Problems

If $T: \mathbb{R}^5\to\mathbb{R}^3$ and $\mathrm{rank}(T)=2$, what is $\dim(\ker T)$? Give an example of such a $T$.

Composite Problems

4E.52

Let $T:V\to W$ be linear. Show that $T$ is one-to-one if and only if $\ker T=\{\mathbf{0}\}$ and that then $T$ maps every linearly independent set of vectors of $V$ to a linearly independent set of $W$.
