Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

Chapter 5 · Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by section and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

Every result is checked by you: if you have found a pair $(\lambda,\mathbf{v})$ with $\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{0}$, it is enough to compute the product $A\mathbf{v}$ and to compare it with $\lambda\mathbf{v}$. A second check for every matrix: the sum of the eigenvalues must equal the trace and their product the determinant. You do not need a solutions manual.

A. Definition and checking of an eigenvector

A vector is an eigenvector when the matrix does not change its direction, only its length and possibly its orientation.

5E.1 BASIC

Let the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&6\\5&2\end{bmatrix}$ be given. Examine which of the vectors $\mathbf{v}_1=\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf{v}_2=\begin{bmatrix}6\\-5\end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf{v}_3=\begin{bmatrix}1\\-1\end{bmatrix}$ are eigenvectors of $A$ and, for those that are, determine the corresponding eigenvalue.

Hint: it is enough to check whether $A\mathbf{v}$ is a multiple of $\mathbf{v}$· no determinant is needed.

5E.2 BASIC

Using the criterion $\det(A-\lambda I)=0$ examine which of the numbers $\lambda=1$, $\lambda=5$, $\lambda=-2$ are eigenvalues of $A=\begin{bmatrix}4&3\\1&2\end{bmatrix}$. For those that are, find a corresponding eigenvector.

5E.3 INTERMEDIATE

Let $A$ be an $n\times n$ matrix in which the sum of the entries of every row is the same, equal to $s$. Prove that the number $s$ is an eigenvalue of $A$ and find a corresponding eigenvector. Apply the conclusion to the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1&1\\0&3&1\\1&1&2\end{bmatrix}$.

Hint: try the vector all of whose coordinates are equal to $1$ and carry out the multiplication row by row.

B. The characteristic polynomial

The eigenvalues are the roots of $p(\lambda)=\det(A-\lambda I)$· for $2\times2$ matrices the trace and the determinant are enough to write it down.

5E.4 BASIC

For the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}6&-2\\-2&9\end{bmatrix}$ find the characteristic polynomial and its eigenvalues. Verify your result by checking that the sum of the eigenvalues equals $\mathrm{tr}(A)$ and their product $\det(A)$.

5E.5 INTERMEDIATE

Find the characteristic polynomial and all the eigenvalues of $A=\begin{bmatrix}2&0&1\\0&3&0\\1&0&2\end{bmatrix}$, noting which eigenvalue appears as a multiple root. Verify with the trace and the determinant.

Hint: the second column has two zeros· expand the determinant along it.

5E.6 INTERMEDIATE

An invertible matrix $A$ of size $2\times2$ has characteristic polynomial $p(\lambda)=\lambda^{2}-6\lambda+8$. Without determining $A$, compute the trace and the determinant of each of the matrices $A$, $A^{2}$ and $A^{-1}$.

Hint: first find the eigenvalues of $A$ and recall how they change when we raise the matrix to a power or invert it.

C. Computing eigenvectors

For each eigenvalue $\lambda$, the eigenspace $E_\lambda$ is the kernel of $A-\lambda I$: you solve a homogeneous system.

5E.7 BASIC

Find the eigenvalues of $A=\begin{bmatrix}7&-4\\6&-3\end{bmatrix}$ and a basis of each eigenspace. Verify the relation $A\mathbf{v}=\lambda\mathbf{v}$ for every eigenpair you have found.

5E.8 INTERMEDIATE

For the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}3&-1&0\\-1&3&0\\0&0&2\end{bmatrix}$ find the characteristic polynomial, the eigenvalues, a basis of each eigenspace and the dimension of each.

Hint: the zeros in the third row and the third column allow an expansion of the determinant along the last column.

5E.9 ADVANCED

Let the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}4&1\\2&3\end{bmatrix}$ and the vector $\mathbf{x}=\begin{bmatrix}5\\-1\end{bmatrix}$ be given. Find the eigenpairs of $A$, write $\mathbf{x}$ as a linear combination of eigenvectors and compute $A^{5}\mathbf{x}$ without raising the matrix to the fifth power.

Hint: on an eigenvector the action of $A^{k}$ reduces to multiplication by a number.

D. Geometric interpretation

An eigenvector means an invariant direction: think about the geometry first, then verify by computation.

5E.10 BASIC

The transformation of space that reflects every point in the plane $xy$ has matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&-1\end{bmatrix}$. Find its eigenvalues geometrically, without computing a determinant, describe the corresponding eigenspaces and give their dimension. Then verify your conclusions algebraically.

5E.11 BASIC

Let the matrix $R=\dfrac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix}-3&4\\4&3\end{bmatrix}$ be given. Compute $\mathrm{tr}(R)$ and $\det(R)$, find its eigenvalues and eigendirections and explain why $R$ represents a reflection in a line through the origin. Determine that line.

Hint: in a reflection, which direction stays fixed and which one is reversed?

5E.12 INTERMEDIATE

The rotation of space by an angle $\theta$ about the axis $z$ has matrix $R=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta&0\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix}$. Prove that the number $1$ is always an eigenvalue of $R$ and interpret the corresponding eigenspace geometrically. For which values of $\theta$ does $R$ have other real eigenvalues as well?

Hint: which points of space remain fixed under the rotation?

E. Algebraic and geometric multiplicity

The $m_a$ is measured in the characteristic polynomial, the $m_g$ in the dimension of the eigenspace· always $1\le m_g\le m_a$.

5E.13 BASIC

A matrix $A$ of size $4\times4$ has characteristic polynomial $p(\lambda)=(\lambda-2)^{3}(\lambda+1)$. List the eigenvalues with their algebraic multiplicities and determine which values the geometric multiplicity of each one can take. Justify your answer.

5E.14 INTERMEDIATE

For the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}4&0&1\\2&3&2\\1&0&4\end{bmatrix}$ find the characteristic polynomial, all the eigenvalues and, for each one, $m_a$ and $m_g$. Give a basis of each eigenspace.

Hint: the second column has two zeros· expand along it.

5E.15 INTERMEDIATE

Prove that for every eigenvalue $\lambda$ of a matrix $A$ of size $n\times n$ we have $m_g(\lambda)=n-\mathrm{rank}(A-\lambda I)$. Using this relation, find $m_a$ and $m_g$ of the eigenvalue $\lambda=2$ of $A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1&1\\0&2&0\\0&0&2\end{bmatrix}$ without solving the homogeneous system.

Hint: apply the rank–nullity theorem to the matrix $A-\lambda I$.

F. Complex eigenvalues

When the discriminant is negative, the eigenvalues appear in conjugate pairs and always conceal a rotation.

5E.16 BASIC

For the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}3&-2\\2&3\end{bmatrix}$ find the characteristic polynomial and its eigenvalues. Explain why there is no real eigendirection and describe the action of $A$ on the plane as a combination of a rotation and a scaling, giving the scaling factor and the angle of rotation.

5E.17 INTERMEDIATE

Let $A$ be a real $2\times2$ matrix with complex eigenvalues $\lambda=a\pm bi$, where $b\neq0$. Prove that $\mathrm{tr}(A)=2a$ and $\det(A)=a^{2}+b^{2}>0$, and conclude that such a matrix is always invertible. Verify the above for $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&-5\\2&3\end{bmatrix}$, after computing its eigenvalues.

5E.18 ADVANCED

Let $A$ be a real $2\times2$ matrix with $\mathrm{tr}(A)=t$ and $\det(A)=d$. Prove that $A$ has non-real eigenvalues if and only if $t^{2}<4d$. Then determine for which values of the parameter $k$ the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&k\\-1&3\end{bmatrix}$ has non-real eigenvalues, for which it has a double real eigenvalue and for which two distinct real ones.

Hint: write the characteristic polynomial with the help of $t$, $d$ and study its discriminant.

G. Applications: vibrations and stresses

The symmetric matrices of engineering — inertia, stress, stiffness — have real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigendirections.

5E.19 BASIC

The inertia tensor of a plane body with respect to the axes $x$, $y$ is $J=\begin{bmatrix}5&-2\\-2&8\end{bmatrix}$ (in $\mathrm{kg\cdot m^{2}}$). Find the principal moments of inertia and the principal axes, and verify that the two principal directions are orthogonal.

Hint: the principal moments are the eigenvalues, the principal axes the corresponding eigendirections.

5E.20 INTERMEDIATE

The stress tensor at a point of a structural member is $\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\begin{bmatrix}8&3\\3&0\end{bmatrix}$ (in MPa). Compute the principal stresses and the principal axes. Which principal stress corresponds to tension and which to compression, and how do you deduce this from their sign?

5E.21 ADVANCED

The study of the small oscillations of a system of two equal masses, connected by three identical springs, leads to the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}-2&1\\1&-2\end{bmatrix}$ (in suitable units), where the natural frequencies are given by the relation $\omega=\sqrt{-\lambda}$. Find the eigenvalues and the eigenvectors of $A$, compute the two natural frequencies and describe the mode of oscillation that corresponds to each eigenvector.

Hint: examine whether the two masses move in the same direction or in opposite directions in each eigendirection.

H. Mixed and composite problems

Here the definition, the characteristic polynomial and matrix algebra are combined· the method is not given.

5E.22 INTERMEDIATE

Let the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}2&k\\1&3\end{bmatrix}$ with $k\in\mathbb{R}$ be given. Determine for which values of $k$ the matrix $A$ has (α) two distinct real eigenvalues, (β) one double real eigenvalue, (γ) non-real eigenvalues and (δ) $0$ as an eigenvalue. In case (δ), what do you conclude about the invertibility of $A$?

5E.23 ADVANCED

Prove, by carrying out the operations directly, that every $A=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}$ matrix satisfies its own characteristic polynomial, that is, $\displaystyle A^{2}-\mathrm{tr}(A)\,A+\det(A)\,I=O.$ Then, assuming $\det(A)\neq0$, express $A^{-1}$ as a linear combination of $A$ and $I$, and apply the formula you have found to $A=\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\2&4\end{bmatrix}$.

Hint: for the second part multiply the identity by $A^{-1}$.

5E.24 ADVANCED

Let $A$ be a matrix of size $n\times n$ with $A^{2}=I$. (α) Prove that every eigenvalue of $A$ equals $1$ or $-1$. (β) Prove that every $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ can be written as $\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w}$ with $A\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}$ and $A\mathbf{w}=-\mathbf{w}$. (γ) Verify (α) and (β) for $A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{x}=\begin{bmatrix}5\\1\end{bmatrix}$.

Hint: for (β) try $\mathbf{u}=\tfrac12(\mathbf{x}+A\mathbf{x})$ and compute $A\mathbf{u}$.

Exercises for the section

5E.25

It is given that $\mathbf{u}_1=\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{u}_2=\begin{bmatrix}2\\1\end{bmatrix}$ are eigenvectors of $B=\begin{bmatrix}3&-2\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$. Compute $B\mathbf{u}_1$, $B\mathbf{u}_2$ and find the corresponding eigenvalues.

5E.26

Examine whether $\mathbf{v}_1=\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{v}_2=\begin{bmatrix}2\\-1\end{bmatrix}$ are eigenvectors of $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&4\\2&3\end{bmatrix}$ and find the corresponding eigenvalues.

5E.27

Using the criterion $\det(A-\lambda I)=0$ examine which of the numbers $\lambda=2$, $\lambda=4$, $\lambda=-1$ are eigenvalues of $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&2\end{bmatrix}$.

5E.28

If $\lambda_1=2$, $\lambda_2=-1$ are the eigenvalues of a $2\times2$ matrix $A$, compute $\mathrm{tr}(A)$ and $\det(A)$. What is the geometric meaning of the product of the eigenvalues?

5E.29

A matrix $3\times3$ has trace $8$ and determinant $12$, with eigenvalues $\lambda_1=1$ and $\lambda_2=3$. Find $\lambda_3$.

5E.30

For which values of $k$ does the matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}k&1\\1&k\end{bmatrix}$ have $0$ as an eigenvalue? What does this mean for its invertibility?

5E.31

Proof: Show that if $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$, then $\lambda^2$ is an eigenvalue of $A^2$. Generalize the result for $A^n$.

5E.32

Find the eigenvalues of $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&-2\\2&1\end{bmatrix}$. Are they real? Which property of the matrix guarantees real eigenvalues?

5E.33

Find the eigenvalues of the rotation matrix $R=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{bmatrix}$. For which values of $\theta$ are they real?

5E.34

Find the eigenvalues of the upper triangular matrix $D=\begin{bmatrix}2&1&0\\0&3&0\\0&0&-1\end{bmatrix}$. What holds in general for the eigenvalues of triangular matrices?

5E.35

Find a basis of the eigenspace $E_2$ of $A=\begin{bmatrix}2&0&0\\0&2&0\\1&1&3\end{bmatrix}$ and its dimension.

5E.36

Is $A=\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\4&-2\end{bmatrix}$ invertible? Justify your answer on the basis of its eigenvalues.

5E.37

Geometric interpretation: Explain geometrically why the rotation by $90^\circ$, $R=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$, has no real eigendirections and confirm that the equation $\det(R-\lambda I)=0$ has no real roots.

5E.38

For the projection onto the line $y=x$, $P=\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}$, find the eigenvalues and the eigendirections geometrically and verify them by computation.

5E.39

For the reflection in the $x$ axis, $R=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}$, explain geometrically why the eigenvalues are $1$ and $-1$ and which directions remain invariant.

5E.40

For $A=\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\0&3\end{bmatrix}$ draw the image of the unit circle. How are the semi-axes of the resulting ellipse related to the eigenvalues of $A$?

5E.41

The identity matrix $I_2$ has eigenvalue $\lambda=1$. Show that every nonzero vector of $\mathbb{R}^2$ is an eigenvector of it and interpret this geometrically. The same for $cI_2$.

5E.42

For the matrix $C=\begin{bmatrix}2&1&0\\0&2&0\\0&0&3\end{bmatrix}$ find all the eigenvalues with their multiplicities $m_a$, $m_g$ and verify that $1\le m_g\le m_a$.

5E.43

For the shear $S=\begin{bmatrix}1&k\\0&1\end{bmatrix}$, $k\neq0$, find $m_a$ and $m_g$ of its unique eigenvalue.

5E.44

For $A=\begin{bmatrix}3&1&0\\0&3&1\\0&0&3\end{bmatrix}$ find $m_a$ and $m_g$ of the eigenvalue $\lambda=3$.

5E.45

Show that the nilpotent matrix $N=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix}$ has a unique eigenvalue $\lambda=0$ with $m_a=2$ and $m_g=1$.

5E.46

Proof: Show that if a $n\times n$ matrix has $n$ distinct eigenvalues, then for every eigenvalue $m_a=m_g=1$ holds. Hint: use the relation $1\le m_g\le m_a$.

5E.47

Examine whether the symmetric matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\-1&2\end{bmatrix}$ is positive definite. What kind of conic section corresponds to the quadratic form $Q(x,y)=\mathbf{x}^\top A\mathbf{x}=1$?

5E.48

Characterize the form $Q(x,y)=3x^2-4xy+3y^2$: is it positive definite? Find its principal axes.

5E.49

Decompose $A=\begin{bmatrix}4&2\\2&1\end{bmatrix}$ into the form $A=Q\Lambda Q^\top$ with $Q$ orthogonal. What is the geometric meaning of the zero eigenvalue?

5E.50

If $A$ is a symmetric $3\times3$ matrix with eigenvalues $\lambda_1=1$, $\lambda_2=2$, $\lambda_3=3$, find $\det(A)$, $\mathrm{tr}(A)$ and $\det(A-I)$.

Composite Problems

5E.51

Prove that if $A^2=A$ (idempotent matrix), then its eigenvalues are $0$ or $1$. Give an example of a $2\times2$ idempotent matrix with both $0$ and $1$ as eigenvalues.

5E.52

Find the eigenvalues and the eigenvectors of the non-symmetric matrix $A=\begin{bmatrix}5&-1\\3&1\end{bmatrix}$ and check whether they are orthogonal. Then repeat for the symmetric $B=\begin{bmatrix}6&2\\2&3\end{bmatrix}$. What do you conclude about the relation between symmetry and orthogonality of eigenvectors?

5E.53

Let $A=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\b&a\end{bmatrix}$ with $b\neq0$. Show that its eigenvalues are $a+b$ and $a-b$, with eigenvectors $(1,1)^\top$ and $(1,-1)^\top$ independent of the values of $a$, $b$.
