Functions of One Real Variable

Chapter 8 · Functions of One Real Variable

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by topic and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

Everything asked here is dealt with exclusively by algebraic means and with the definitions of the chapter: monotonicity is always proved by comparing the values $f(x_1)$ and $f(x_2)$ for $x_1\lt x_2$, never with a derivative. Every result is checked independently: the domain by testing values at the ends of the restrictions, the range by solving $y=f(x)$ for $x$, the inverse by the verification $f^{-1}(f(x))=x$. You do not need a solutions manual.

A. Domain and range

Every restriction generates the domain; solving for $x$ reveals the range.

8E.1 BASIC

Find the natural (largest) domain, justifying each restriction: (α) $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{\sqrt{x+3}}{x-2}$,   (β) $\displaystyle g(x)=\ln(4-x)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$,   (γ) $h(x)=\arcsin(2x-1)$.

8E.2 BASIC

Find the range, justifying your answer analytically: (α) $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{x^{2}+1}$,   (β) $g(x)=3-2\cos x$,   (γ) $h(x)=\sqrt{x-1}+2$.

Hint: in each case first locate the range of the inner expression ($x^{2}+1$, $\cos x$, $\sqrt{x-1}$).

8E.3 INTERMEDIATE

Let $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{x^{2}+4}$ be given. (α) Find the domain. (β) By solving the equation $y=f(x)$ for $x^{2}$, determine for which $y$ there is a real solution. (γ) Conclude the range of $f$ and explain why the value $1$ is not attained.

B. Monotonicity and one-to-one functions

Monotonicity is proved by comparing values; from it the one-to-one property follows immediately.

8E.4 BASIC

Using the definition of monotonicity show that $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$ is strictly increasing on $[0,+\infty)$ and conclude that it is one-to-one.

Hint: $\displaystyle \sqrt{x_2}-\sqrt{x_1}=\frac{x_2-x_1}{\sqrt{x_2}+\sqrt{x_1}}$ for $x_1,x_2\ge 0$ not both zero.

8E.5 INTERMEDIATE

Let $f(x)=x^{3}-3x$ be given. (α) Find two different points of $\mathbb{R}$ at which $f$ takes the same value and conclude that it is not one-to-one on $\mathbb{R}$. (β) Prove that $f$ is strictly increasing on $[1,+\infty)$.

Hint for (β): $f(x_2)-f(x_1)=(x_2-x_1)\bigl(x_2^{2}+x_1x_2+x_1^{2}-3\bigr)$; estimate the parenthesis for $x_1,x_2\ge 1$.

8E.6 ADVANCED

Let $f$ be strictly increasing and $g$ strictly decreasing, both defined on $\mathbb{R}$. (α) Prove that $f-g$ is strictly increasing. (β) Prove that the composition $f\circ g$ is strictly decreasing. (γ) Give a specific pair $f,g$ for which $f+g$ is not monotone on $\mathbb{R}$.

C. The inverse function

Solve $y=f(x)$ for $x$; the range of $f$ becomes the domain of $f^{-1}$.

8E.7 BASIC

Find the formula and the domain of the inverse: (α) $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{x}+2$ with $x\gt 0$,   (β) $g(x)=(x-3)^{3}+1$ with $x\in\mathbb{R}$.

8E.8 INTERMEDIATE

Let $f(x)=x+\sqrt{x}$ be given with domain $[0,+\infty)$. (α) Show that it is strictly increasing, as a sum of two strictly increasing functions, and hence one-to-one. (β) Find its range. (γ) Setting $t=\sqrt{x}\ge 0$, solve $y=t^{2}+t$ for $t$ and write a closed formula for $f^{-1}$.

8E.9 INTERMEDIATE

Let $f,g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be one-to-one and onto. (α) Prove that $f\circ g$ is one-to-one and onto. (β) Prove that $(f\circ g)^{-1}=g^{-1}\circ f^{-1}$. (γ) Verify the formula for $f(x)=2x+1$ and $g(x)=x^{3}$.

Hint: for (β) it is enough to show that $g^{-1}\circ f^{-1}$ undoes $f\circ g$ on both sides.

D. Composition of functions

First the formula, then the domain: the inner function must give values that are acceptable to the outer one.

8E.10 BASIC

If $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{x-1}$ and $g(x)=x^{2}+1$, compute the formulas and the domains of $(f\circ g)(x)$ and $(g\circ f)(x)$. Do the two compositions coincide?

8E.11 INTERMEDIATE

Let $\displaystyle h(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln x-1}}$ be given. (α) Write $h$ as a composition of three functions $h=f\circ g\circ k$, specifying explicitly $f$, $g$, $k$. (β) Find the domain of $h$, explaining which two different restrictions come into play.

8E.12 ADVANCED

Let $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{x}{x-1}$ with $x\ne 1$. (α) Show that for every $x\ne 1$ we have $f(x)\ne 1$, so that the composition $f\circ f$ is defined. (β) Compute and simplify $(f\circ f)(x)$. (γ) Conclude that $f$ is one-to-one and that $f^{-1}=f$. (δ) Find $(f\circ f\circ f)(x)$.

E. The exponential and the logarithmic function

The properties of powers and of logarithms turn these equations into algebraic ones.

8E.13 BASIC

Solve, checking the restrictions each time: (α) $2^{2x}-5\cdot 2^{x}+4=0$,   (β) $\ln x+\ln(x-3)=\ln 4$,   (γ) $3^{\,x+1}=27^{\,x-1}$.

Hint: in (α) set $t=2^{x}$ with $t\gt 0$.

8E.14 INTERMEDIATE

Solve the equation $x^{\log_{10}x}=100\,x$. (α) What is the domain of the equation? (β) Take the logarithm to base $10$ of both sides and set $t=\log_{10}x$. (γ) Return to the variable $x$ and verify each root.

8E.15 ADVANCED

Let $f(x)=\ln\!\bigl(x+\sqrt{x^{2}+1}\bigr)$ be given. (α) Show that it is defined for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$. (β) Prove that it is odd. (γ) Prove that it is strictly increasing. (δ) Find its inverse and identify which well-known hyperbolic function results.

Hint: the identity $\bigl(\sqrt{x^{2}+1}-x\bigr)\bigl(\sqrt{x^{2}+1}+x\bigr)=1$ is useful; for (δ) set $y=f(x)$ and compute the difference $e^{y}-e^{-y}$.

F. Transformations of graphs

Every change in the formula corresponds to one specific geometric motion of the curve.

8E.16 BASIC

Suppose the graph of a function $y=f(x)$ is known. Describe precisely how the graphs of $y=f(x-3)$, $y=f(x)+2$, $y=-f(x)$, $y=f(-x)$, $y=2f(x)$, $y=f(2x)$ are obtained from it.

8E.17 BASIC

Starting from the basic $\displaystyle y=\frac{1}{x}$, describe the successive transformations that lead to $\displaystyle y=\frac{1}{x-2}+3$, draw its graph and find its domain and its range.

8E.18 INTERMEDIATE

Starting from $y=\sin x$, describe the transformations that give $\displaystyle y=3\sin\!\left(2x-\frac{\pi}{3}\right)+1$ and determine the amplitude, the period, the horizontal shift and the range.

Hint: first write the argument in the form $2\bigl(x-c\bigr)$; the order of the transformations matters.

G. Parametric and polar curves

Elimination of the parameter and the relations $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$: two languages for the same curve.

8E.19 BASIC

Eliminate the parameter, identify the curve and determine exactly which part of it is traced out: (α) $x=1+2t$, $y=3-t$, $t\in\mathbb{R}$; (β) $x=3\cos t$, $y=3\sin t$, $t\in[0,\pi]$; (γ) $\displaystyle x=\frac{1}{\cos t}$, $y=\tan t$, $\displaystyle t\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right)$.

8E.20 INTERMEDIATE

Consider the parametric curve $x=t^{2}-1$, $y=t^{3}-t$, $t\in\mathbb{R}$. (α) Show that the values $t=1$ and $t=-1$ give the same point of the plane. (β) Observing that $y=t\,x$, prove that every point of the curve satisfies the relation $y^{2}=x^{2}(x+1)$. (γ) Complete a table of values for $t=-2,-1,0,1,2$ and sketch the curve qualitatively.

8E.21 ADVANCED

The polar equation $r^{2}=4\cos(2\theta)$ is given. (α) For which values of $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$ is the curve defined? (β) Multiplying both sides by $r^{2}$, prove that in Cartesian coordinates it is written as $\bigl(x^{2}+y^{2}\bigr)^{2}=4\bigl(x^{2}-y^{2}\bigr)$. (γ) Find the maximum value of $r$ and the directions in which it is attained, and sketch qualitatively.

Hint: use $\cos(2\theta)=\cos^{2}\theta-\sin^{2}\theta$ and $r^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}$.

H. Mixed and composite problems

Exercises that combine more than one concept of the chapter.

8E.22 INTERMEDIATE

Let $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{x^{2}-1}{x^{2}+1}$ be given. (α) Show that it is even. (β) Write it in the form $\displaystyle f(x)=1-\frac{2}{x^{2}+1}$ and find its range. (γ) Show that it is strictly increasing on $[0,+\infty)$. (δ) Is it one-to-one on $\mathbb{R}$? Justify your answer.

8E.23 INTERMEDIATE

A function $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is even and periodic with period $T=2$, while for $x\in[0,1]$ we have $f(x)=x^{2}$. (α) Find its formula on $[-1,0]$. (β) Compute $f(3.5)$, $f(-2.25)$ and $f(7)$. (γ) Sketch its graph qualitatively on $[-3,3]$. (δ) Find its range.

Hint: first use evenness to cover $[-1,1]$ and then periodicity.

8E.24 ADVANCED

Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be a function satisfying the relation $f(x)+2f(1-x)=3x$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$. (α) Replacing $x$ by $1-x$, write a second relation. (β) Regarding $f(x)$ and $f(1-x)$ as two unknowns, find the formula of $f$. (γ) Verify that the function you have found does indeed satisfy the original relation.

From the printed list in the chapter

Exercises moved here from the printed list, so that the chapter keeps a core of representative problems. They cover the full range of difficulty and are arranged in the order of the sections.

Exercises for the section

8E.25

Find the domain of each function: (α) $f(x)=\sqrt{9-x^2}+\dfrac{1}{x+2}$, (β) $g(x)=\ln(x^2-x-6)$, (γ) $h(x)=\arccos\left(\dfrac{x}{3}\right)$.

8E.26

Find the range, justifying your answer analytically: (α) $g(x)=2\sin x+1$, (β) $h(x)=3-|x-1|$.

8E.27

Construction of a function: Let $f(x+1)=x^2-1$ be given. (α) Use a substitution (say $u=x+1$) to find a formula for $f(u)$. (β) Write the formula $f(x)$. (γ) Find the domain of $f$.

8E.28

Sketch $f(x)=|x^2-4|$ and find the range.

8E.29

Find the inverse function $f^{-1}(x)$ for each of the following, after checking that it is one-to-one on its natural domain: (α) $f(x)=3x-5$, (β) $f(x)=x^3+1$, (γ) $f(x)=\sqrt{x+2}$.

8E.30

If $f(x)=\dfrac{2x+1}{x-3}$, find $f^{-1}(x)$, its domain, and verify that $f(f^{-1}(x))=x$.

8E.31

Find the inverse function (if it exists), after checking the one-to-one property, together with its domain: (α) $f(x)=2x^2-1$, $x\geq 0$, (β) $g(x)=e^{x-1}+2$.

8E.32

Let $f(x)=e^{2x}-3$ be given. Find $f^{-1}(x)$, as well as the domain and the range of $f^{-1}$.

8E.33

Prove that: (α) the product of two odd functions is even, (β) the product of an even and an odd function is odd, (γ) the sum of two even functions is even.

8E.34

If $f$ is odd and is defined at $0$, prove that $f(0)=0$. Is there a function that is both even and odd? Which one?

8E.35

Is $f(x)=\sin x + \cos x$ periodic? If so, find its period.

8E.36

A function $f$ is periodic with period $T=4$ and is defined on $[0,4)$ by $f(x)=x(4-x)$. Compute $f(5)$, $f(-1)$ and $f(10.5)$.

8E.37

If $f(x)=x^2+1$ and $g(x)=\sqrt{x-1}$, compute and find the domain of: (α) $(f\circ g)(x)$, (β) $(g\circ f)(x)$.

8E.38

If $f(x)=2x-3$ and $g(x)=\dfrac{x+3}{2}$, check whether $f\circ g = g\circ f = \mathrm{id}$. What do you conclude?

8E.39

Let $h(x)=\sin(x^2)$ be given. Find $f$, $g$ such that $h=f\circ g$ and compute the domain.

8E.40

If $f(x)=2x+1$ and $g(x)=x^2-3$, compute: (α) $(f\circ g)(x)$, (β) $(g\circ f)(x)$, (γ) $(f\circ g)(2)$, (δ) $(f\circ f)(x)$.

8E.41

Let $f(x)=\dfrac1x$, $g(x)=x+1$, $h(x)=x^2$ be given. Compute $(f\circ g\circ h)(x)$ and find its domain.

8E.42

Using the definition of monotonicity, show that $f(x)=x^3$ is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$.

8E.43

By completing the square, write $f(x)=x^2-6x+5$ in the form $(x-a)^2+b$. Find its minimum and the intervals of monotonicity without using a derivative.

8E.44

Show that $|\sin x+\cos x|\leq\sqrt2$ for every $x$, by writing $\sin x+\cos x=\sqrt2\,\sin\!\left(x+\tfrac{\pi}{4}\right)$. What is the maximum and what is the minimum of the function?

8E.45

A force $F(t)=5\bigl[H(t-1)-H(t-3)\bigr]$ kN is applied to a structure. Describe in words when and for how long the force acts.

8E.46

Draw the graph of $g(t)=2H(t)-H(t-1)-H(t-3)$ for $t\in[-1,5]$.

8E.47

If $h(t)=t\cdot H(t-2)$, find the values $h(0)$, $h(1)$, $h(2)$, $h(3)$ and sketch it for $t\in[0,4]$.

8E.48

Compute the exact values $\cosh(\ln 2)$, $\tanh(\ln 2)$ and $\sinh(\ln 3)$.

8E.49

Using only the definitions, show that $\sinh$ is odd, $\cosh$ is even and $\tanh$ is odd.

8E.50

Prove the identity $\sinh(2x)=2\sinh x\cosh x$ starting from the exponential definitions.

8E.51

Points of intersection of two exponential functions: $f(x)=e^x$ and $g(x)=e^{2-x}$. (α) Set $e^x = e^{2-x}$ and solve. (β) Find the coordinates of the points of intersection.

8E.52

Describe the shift of the graph $y=|x-2|-1$ relative to $y=|x|$. Find the vertex, the zeros, and sketch it.

8E.53

Parametrize the line segment from $A(1,2)$ to $B(5,-2)$ with $t\in[0,1]$ and write the parametric equations of the line that contains it.

8E.54

Convert to polar coordinates: (α) $x^{2}+y^{2}=9$; (β) $y=x$; (γ) $x^{2}+y^{2}=2x$ (Hint: the result is a shifted circle, which one?).

8E.55

Sketch qualitatively the cardioid $r=1+\cos\theta$ by completing a table of values for $\theta=0,\tfrac{\pi}{3},\tfrac{\pi}{2},\pi,\tfrac{3\pi}{2}$. Where does the radius vanish?

Composite Problems

8E.56

For $f(x)=\dfrac{x-1}{x+1}$: (α) Find the domain. (β) Write it in the form $f(x)=1-\dfrac{2}{x+1}$ and examine the monotonicity separately on $(-\infty,-1)$ and on $(-1,+\infty)$. (γ) Is it one-to-one? If so, find the inverse and its domain.

8E.57

For $x>0$ consider $f(x)=x+\dfrac1x$. (α) Prove that $f(x)\geq 2$ for every $x>0$ without using a derivative (hint: $(\sqrt{x}-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}})^2\geq0$). (β) When does equality hold? (γ) Show that $f$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,1)$ and strictly increasing on $(1,+\infty)$.

8E.58

Let $f(x)=|x|+|x-2|$ be given. (α) Write it piecewise. (β) Draw its graph. (γ) Find the minimum and the range. (δ) Is it even with respect to some vertical axis?

8E.59

Show that $f(x)=\dfrac{x}{1+|x|}$ is one-to-one from $\mathbb{R}$ onto $(-1,1)$ and find its inverse. Sketch the graph qualitatively.

Exercises for Section 8.1.4 – Monotonicity, Bounded Functions and Extrema

8E.65

If $f$, $g$ are strictly increasing, prove that $f+g$ is strictly increasing. Does the same hold for the product $f\cdot g$? Give a counterexample.

Exercises for Section 8.1.5 – The Elementary Functions

8E.66

A cable suspended between two pylons takes the shape of a catenary $y=20\cosh\left(\dfrac{x}{20}\right)$ (in m), for $x\in[-15,15]$. Compute the height at the midpoint ($x=0$) and at the ends, as well as the sag (their difference).

8E.67

For the inverse trigonometric functions $\arcsin$, $\arccos$, $\arctan$: write down the domain and the range and sketch their graphs qualitatively. Which of them are odd?

8E.68

Solving a logarithmic equation: $\log_2(x-1)+\log_2(x+1)=3$. (α) What is the domain? (β) Use the properties of logarithms to simplify. (γ) Solve. (δ) Check that the solution satisfies the restrictions.

8E.69

Exercises for Section 8.2 – Parametric and Polar Curves

8E.70

For the cycloid $x=a(t-\sin t)$, $y=a(1-\cos t)$ find the points at which it meets the $x$ axis, as well as its maximum height. Interpret the result physically, considering that the curve is traced out by a point on the rim of a wheel.

Composite Problems

8E.71

Composition and periodicity: Let $f$ be periodic with period $T$ and let $g$ be any function. (α) Prove that $T$ is a period of $g\circ f$ — not necessarily the fundamental one; give an example where the fundamental period of $g\circ f$ is strictly smaller than $T$. (β) Is $f\circ g$ necessarily periodic? Give an example and a counterexample.
