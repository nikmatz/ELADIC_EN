Introduction to Dynamical Systems

Chapter 7 · Introduction to Dynamical Systems

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by topic and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

Every result checks itself. An eigenpair is verified by a single multiplication ($A\mathbf{v}=\lambda\mathbf{v}$), a closed formula for $\mathbf{x}_k$ is verified by setting $k=0$ and $k=1$, and a stationary distribution is verified by the relations $P\boldsymbol{\pi}=\boldsymbol{\pi}$ and $\sum_i\pi_i=1$. You do not need a solutions manual.

A. Discrete linear systems

The solution is always $\mathbf{x}_k=A^k\mathbf{x}_0$; what is asked is to write it down without raising the matrix to a power.

7E.1 BASIC

For the system $\mathbf{x}_{k+1}=A\mathbf{x}_k$ with $A=\begin{bmatrix}0&2\\1&1\end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{x}_0=\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}$, compute $\mathbf{x}_1$, $\mathbf{x}_2$ and $\mathbf{x}_3$ by successive multiplications. Then find the eigenvalues of $A$ and say whether the system decays, remains bounded or blows up.

7E.2 BASIC

Let $A=\begin{bmatrix}4&1\\1&4\end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{x}_0=\begin{bmatrix}2\\0\end{bmatrix}$ be given. Find the eigenvalues and the eigenvectors of $A$, expand $\mathbf{x}_0$ in the basis of the eigenvectors and write a closed formula for $\mathbf{x}_k$.

Hint: for a symmetric matrix of the form $\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\b&a\end{bmatrix}$ the vectors $(1,1)^\top$ and $(1,-1)^\top$ are always eigenvectors.

7E.3 INTERMEDIATE

Let $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix}$. (α) Show that $A$ has a double eigenvalue but only one linearly independent eigenvector, and therefore is not diagonalizable. (β) Prove by induction that $A^k=\begin{bmatrix}1&k\\0&1\end{bmatrix}$ for every $k\ge 1$. (γ) Find $\mathbf{x}_k$ for $\mathbf{x}_0=(0,1)^\top$ and describe the trajectory.

7E.4 ADVANCED

Consider the recursive sequence $a_{k+2}=a_{k+1}+2a_k$ with $a_0=1$ and $a_1=0$. Setting $\mathbf{x}_k=(a_k,a_{k+1})^\top$, write the recurrence in the form $\mathbf{x}_{k+1}=A\mathbf{x}_k$, find the eigenvalues and the eigenvectors of $A$ and give a closed formula for $a_k$. Verify the formula for $k=0,1,2$.

Hint: the companion matrix has first row $(0\ \ 1)$.

B. The role of the eigenvalues

The fate of the system is read off from the term with the largest $|\lambda|$ — provided its coefficient is not zero.

7E.5 BASIC

The solution of a discrete system is $\displaystyle \mathbf{x}_k=2\,(0.8)^k\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\end{bmatrix}+(0.5)^k\begin{bmatrix}1\\-1\end{bmatrix}$. (α) Compute $\mathbf{x}_0$ and $\mathbf{x}_1$. (β) Find $\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbf{x}_k$. (γ) Which of the two terms dies out faster and what is, consequently, the limiting direction of $\mathbf{x}_k$?

7E.6 INTERMEDIATE

For $A=\begin{bmatrix}6&-2\\2&1\end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{x}_0=\begin{bmatrix}3\\3\end{bmatrix}$, write the solution $\mathbf{x}_k$ in closed form, determine the dominant term and compute $\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{x}_k}{5^k}$.

7E.7 ADVANCED

Let $A=\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\0&-2\end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{x}_0=(1,1)^\top$. (α) Write $\mathbf{x}_k$. (β) Show that the sequence $\mathbf{x}_k/2^k$ does not converge and determine its two accumulation points. (γ) Which hypothesis of the conclusion “the direction of $\mathbf{x}_k$ tends to the dominant eigendirection” is violated here?

Hint: compare the absolute values of the two eigenvalues.

C. Stability

One single number decides everything: the spectral radius $\rho(A)=\max_i|\lambda_i|$ relative to unity.

7E.8 BASIC

For each of the following matrices compute the spectral radius $\rho(A)$ and characterize the system $\mathbf{x}_{k+1}=A\mathbf{x}_k$ as asymptotically stable, bounded or unstable: (α) $\begin{bmatrix}0.4&0\\0&-0.9\end{bmatrix}$, (β) $\begin{bmatrix}1&0.3\\0&0.2\end{bmatrix}$, (γ) $\begin{bmatrix}-1.1&0\\0.5&0.3\end{bmatrix}$.

7E.9 BASIC

For $A=\begin{bmatrix}0&-0.6\\0.6&0\end{bmatrix}$ compute the (complex) eigenvalues and their absolute value, characterize the stability of $\mathbf{x}_{k+1}=A\mathbf{x}_k$ and describe qualitatively the trajectories in the plane.

7E.10 INTERMEDIATE

The system $\mathbf{x}_{k+1}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-b&0\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}_k$ with $b\in\mathbb{R}$ is given. Determine for which values of $b$ the system is asymptotically stable and for which it is bounded. Distinguish the cases $b>0$ (complex eigenvalues) and $b<0$ (real ones).

7E.11 ADVANCED

Let $A=\begin{bmatrix}0.5&a\\0&0.5\end{bmatrix}$ with $a\neq0$. (α) Show that $A$ is not diagonalizable. (β) Writing $A=0.5\,I+N$ with $N^2=O$, find a closed formula for $A^k$. (γ) Prove that $\mathbf{x}_k\to\mathbf{0}$ for every $\mathbf{x}_0$ and for every value of $a$, although the matrix is defective.

Hint: in (γ) you need the behavior of the sequence $k\,r^{k}$ when $0<r<1$.

D. Markov chains

For a column-stochastic matrix the eigenvalue $1$ always exists; the corresponding eigenvector, normalized, is the stationary distribution.

7E.12 BASIC

Let $P=\begin{bmatrix}0.7&0.4\\0.3&0.6\end{bmatrix}$ be given. Verify that it is column-stochastic and find the stationary distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ with $P\boldsymbol{\pi}=\boldsymbol{\pi}$ and $\pi_1+\pi_2=1$.

7E.13 INTERMEDIATE

Application: A production machine is every day in one of two states: in operation or out of operation. A machine in operation breaks down within the day with probability $0.1$, while one out of operation is repaired with probability $0.6$. (α) Write the column-stochastic matrix $P$ of the chain. (β) Find the stationary distribution. (γ) Find the second eigenvalue of $P$ and explain what it determines about the speed of convergence.

Hint: for a $2\times2$ matrix we have $\lambda_1+\lambda_2=\mathrm{tr}(P)$ and you already know one eigenvalue.

7E.14 INTERMEDIATE

The column-stochastic matrix of three states $\displaystyle P=\begin{bmatrix}0.5&0.2&0\\0.5&0.6&0.3\\0&0.2&0.7\end{bmatrix}$ is given. Verify that the columns sum to one and find the stationary distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ as exact fractions.

Hint: solve the homogeneous system $(P-I)\boldsymbol{\pi}=\mathbf{0}$ and normalize at the end; one of the three equations is redundant.

E. Phase portraits

In continuous systems $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=A\mathbf{x}$ the place of the absolute value $|\lambda|$ is taken by the sign of $\operatorname{Re}\lambda$.

7E.15 BASIC

For each of the systems $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=A\mathbf{x}$ find the eigenvalues and classify the equilibrium point $(0,0)$: (α) $A=\begin{bmatrix}-4&2\\1&-3\end{bmatrix}$, (β) $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&-2\\1&3\end{bmatrix}$, (γ) $A=\begin{bmatrix}0&3\\-3&0\end{bmatrix}$.

7E.16 INTERMEDIATE

For $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\4&3\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}$: find the eigenvalues and the eigenvectors, write the general solution, classify the equilibrium point and determine the stable and the unstable eigendirection. Finally give the solution with initial condition $\mathbf{x}(0)=(2,1)^\top$.

7E.17 INTERMEDIATE

For $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=\begin{bmatrix}-1&-2\\2&-1\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}$ compute the eigenvalues, classify the equilibrium point and determine (α) the rate at which the amplitude of the trajectory shrinks and (β) the period of one full revolution about the origin.

Hint: write $\lambda=\alpha\pm i\beta$; $\alpha$ controls the amplitude and $\beta$ the angular velocity.

7E.18 ADVANCED

Let $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=A\mathbf{x}$ with $A=\begin{bmatrix}-2&1\\0&-2\end{bmatrix}$ be given. (α) Show that the eigenvalue $\lambda=-2$ is double with only one independent eigenvector $\mathbf{v}$. (β) Find a generalized eigenvector $\mathbf{w}$ with $(A-\lambda I)\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{v}$ and write the general solution. (γ) Classify the equilibrium point and explain why, despite the term $t\,e^{-2t}$, all the trajectories end at the origin.

F. Applications

The same criterion appears in numerical methods, in populations and in vibrations of structures.

7E.19 BASIC

Application: The error $\mathbf{e}_k$ of an iterative method for solving a linear system satisfies $\mathbf{e}_{k+1}=T\mathbf{e}_k$ with $T=\begin{bmatrix}0&0.4\\0.4&0\end{bmatrix}$. (α) Compute $\rho(T)$ and explain why the method converges. (β) Find the least number of iterations $k$ so that the magnitude of the error has been reduced below $10^{-3}$ of the initial one.

7E.20 INTERMEDIATE

Application: A population of insects is divided into juveniles ($n_k$) and adults ($e_k$), with $n_{k+1}=3e_k$ and $e_{k+1}=0.5\,n_k+0.5\,e_k$. (α) Write the matrix $A$ of the system. (β) Find the eigenvalues and explain whether the total population grows or decreases in the long run, giving the rate of change per time step as a percentage. (γ) Find the limiting ratio of juveniles to adults.

7E.21 INTERMEDIATE

Application: A single-degree-of-freedom system with mass $m=1000$ kg and stiffness $k=100\,000$ N/m is described by $m\ddot{x}+c\dot{x}+kx=0$. (α) Compute the natural frequency $\omega_n=\sqrt{k/m}$ and the critical damping $c_{cr}=2\sqrt{km}$. (β) For which values of $c$ is the response a decaying oscillation? (γ) For $c=12\,000$ N$\cdot$s/m write the first-order system, compute the eigenvalues and the damping ratio $\zeta=c/c_{cr}$ and classify the equilibrium point.

G. Mixed and composite problems

Here the route is not given: first identify whether the system is discrete or continuous and which criterion applies.

7E.22 BASIC

For $A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}$ find the diagonalization $A=PDP^{-1}$ and give a closed formula for $A^k$ as a $2\times2$ matrix with entries in terms of $3^k$. Verify the result for $k=1$ and $k=2$.

7E.23 INTERMEDIATE

Let $A=\begin{bmatrix}0.5&0\\1&-0.9\end{bmatrix}$ be given. (α) Find the eigenvalues. (β) Characterize the stability of the discrete system $\mathbf{x}_{k+1}=A\mathbf{x}_k$. (γ) Characterize the stability of the continuous system $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=A\mathbf{x}$ and classify its equilibrium point. (δ) Explain why the two conclusions differ, referring to the two different criteria.

7E.24 ADVANCED

Two tanks exchange liquid according to $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=A\mathbf{x}$, where $A=\begin{bmatrix}-2&1\\2&-1\end{bmatrix}$. (α) Observe that every column of $A$ sums to zero and show that the quantity $x_1(t)+x_2(t)$ remains constant. (β) Find the eigenvalues and the eigenvectors and explain why the zero eigenvalue was to be expected. (γ) For $\mathbf{x}(0)=(3,0)^\top$ write the solution $\mathbf{x}(t)$ and find $\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbf{x}(t)$, checking that the limit is consistent with (α).

Hint: (α) follows by differentiating the sum and adding the two equations side by side.

7E.29

7E.30

7E.31

7E.32

7E.33

7E.34

7E.35

7E.36

For $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=\begin{bmatrix}-2&0\\0&-3\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}$ classify the equilibrium point, solve with initial condition $\mathbf{x}(0)=(1,2)^\top$ and find $\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbf{x}(t)$.

7E.37

Classify the equilibrium point of $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}$ and sketch the phase portrait qualitatively.

7E.38

For $A=\begin{bmatrix}-3&1\\1&-3\end{bmatrix}$ find the eigenvalues and the eigenvectors and write the solution $\mathbf{x}(t)$ with $\mathbf{x}(0)=(2,-1)^\top$. Classify the equilibrium point.

7E.39

Theoretical: When does a $2\times2$ linear system $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=A\mathbf{x}$ have a center? State the conditions in terms of the trace and the determinant of $A$ and give a numerical example.

7E.40

Application: A seismic damper with $m=500$ kg, $k=2000$ N/m and $b=100$ N$\cdot$s/m. Determine the type of damping and classify the equilibrium point of the phase portrait.

7E.41

Application: For a damping ratio $\zeta=0.05$ (typical for a concrete building) show that the eigenvalues are complex with negative real part and explain why the response is a decaying oscillation.

7E.42

Explain why the phase portrait of a damped oscillator is a stable focus, whereas without damping it is a center. Connect your answer with the real part of the eigenvalues.

Composite Problems
