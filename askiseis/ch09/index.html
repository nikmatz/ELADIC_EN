Limits and Continuity of a Function

Chapter 9 · Limits and Continuity of a Function

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by technique and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

Every indeterminate form is handled using only the tools of the present chapter: factorization, multiplication by the conjugate expression, division by the dominant power, the fundamental limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1$ and the Squeeze Theorem. The derivative and L'Hôpital's rule have not been introduced yet and may not be used. At every step justify which limit law you are applying.

A. Limits — basic computations

When the function is continuous at the point, the limit follows by direct substitution; check that first.

9E.1 BASIC

Evaluate: (α) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-1}\left(x^4-2x^2+3x\right)$,   (β) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^2+3}{x+5}$.

9E.2 BASIC

Evaluate: (α) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to3}\sqrt{2x^2-5}$,   (β) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x^3-2x+5}{x^2+1}$.

Hint: state explicitly which limit law allows interchanging the limit and the root.

9E.3 INTERMEDIATE

Suppose that $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2}f(x)=4$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2}g(x)=-2$. Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2}\frac{3f(x)-\bigl(g(x)\bigr)^2}{f(x)+g(x)}$, justifying each step by the corresponding limit law.

Which condition must be checked before the quotient law is applied?

B. Indeterminate forms and roots

Form $\tfrac{0}{0}$: cancel the common factor by factoring, by the conjugate expression or by a suitable substitution.

9E.4 BASIC

Evaluate: (α) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^2+x-2}{x^2+2x-3}$,   (β) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^3-3x^2+2x}{x^2-4}$.

9E.5 INTERMEDIATE

Evaluate: (α) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sqrt{4+x}-\sqrt{4-x}}{x}$,   (β) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2}\frac{\sqrt{x+2}-2}{x-2}$.

Hint: multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the numerator.

9E.6 ADVANCED

Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to1}\frac{\sqrt[3]{x}-1}{\sqrt{x}-1}$  $(x>0)$.

Hint: set $x=t^6$ with $t>0$; as $x\to1$ we have $t\to1$. Both roots then become polynomials in $t$.

C. One-sided limits

Absolute values, piecewise formulas and zeros of the denominator require a separate study from the left and from the right.

9E.7 BASIC

For $\displaystyle h(x)=\frac{x^2-x}{|x-1|}$ evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to1^-}h(x)$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to1^+}h(x)$ and decide whether $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to1}h(x)$ exists.

9E.8 INTERMEDIATE

Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2^-}\frac{x-3}{x^2-4}$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2^+}\frac{x-3}{x^2-4}$.

Hint: study separately the sign of the numerator and the sign of the denominator on either side of $2$.

9E.9 ADVANCED

Let $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{1+2^{1/x}}$, $x\neq0$. Evaluate the one-sided limits of $f$ at $x=0$ and examine whether $f$ extends to a continuous function at $x=0$.

Hint: examine first the behavior of the exponent $1/x$ and then of the power $2^{1/x}$ on either side of zero.

D. Limits at infinity and asymptotes

Divide by the dominant power of $x$ and watch the sign when $x\to-\infty$.

9E.10 BASIC

Evaluate: (α) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{4x^3-2x+1}{x^3+5x^2}$,   (β) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{2x+1}{x^2-3}$.

9E.11 INTERMEDIATE

Let $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{3x^2-12}{x^2-x-6}$. Determine its domain, all of its vertical and horizontal asymptotes, and examine its behavior at the points where the denominator vanishes.

Hint: factor numerator and denominator before drawing a conclusion; not every root of the denominator gives a vertical asymptote.

9E.12 INTERMEDIATE

Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\left(\sqrt{x^2+x+1}-\sqrt{x^2-x}\right)$.

Hint: this is an indeterminate form $\infty-\infty$; multiply and divide by the sum of the two roots.

9E.13 ADVANCED

Let $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{x^3}{x^2+1}$. Show that $f$ has neither vertical nor horizontal asymptotes and find its oblique asymptote as $x\to+\infty$ and as $x\to-\infty$.

Hint: carry out the polynomial division and study the limit of the difference $f(x)-(\lambda x+\beta)$.

E. Trigonometric limits and the squeeze theorem

Reduce everything to the fundamental limit $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1$; for bounded factors use the squeeze theorem.

9E.14 BASIC

Evaluate: (α) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin\frac{x}{2}}{x}$,   (β) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x}{\sin 4x}$.

9E.15 INTERMEDIATE

Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos x}{x\sin x}$.

Hint: multiply numerator and denominator by $1+\cos x$ and use the identity $1-\cos^2 x=\sin^2 x$.

9E.16 INTERMEDIATE

Let $f$ be a function defined on a neighborhood of $1$ for which $\bigl|f(x)-3\bigr|\le 5(x-1)^2$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$. Using the Squeeze Theorem, show that $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to1}f(x)$ exists, and determine it.

9E.17 ADVANCED

Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin(\sin x)}{x}$.

Hint: write the quotient as the product $\dfrac{\sin(\sin x)}{\sin x}\cdot\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ and set $u=\sin x$, explaining why $u\neq0$ for $x$ near $0$ with $x\neq0$.

F. Continuity and points of discontinuity

Continuity at $x_0$ means three things at once: the function is defined, the limit exists, and the two are equal.

9E.18 BASIC

Examine whether the function $f(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{x^2-5x+6}{x-2}, & x\neq2\\[4pt] -1, & x=2\end{cases}$ is continuous at $x=2$, checking each of the three conditions for continuity separately.

9E.19 BASIC

(α) Determine the set of points at which $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{\sqrt{x+1}}{x^2-4}$ is continuous, justifying your answer with the theorems on continuity of operations and composition. (β) For $\displaystyle g(x)=\frac{x^2+x-6}{x^2+5x+6}$ locate all points of discontinuity and classify each one (removable or essential — here, infinite).

Hint: in (β) factor numerator and denominator first and study the two one-sided limits separately at each suspect point.

9E.20 INTERMEDIATE

Find $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}$ so that $f(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\sqrt{x+\beta}-3}{x}, & x>0\\[4pt] \alpha, & x\le0\end{cases}$ is continuous at $x=0$.

Hint: for which value of $\beta$ can the right-hand limit be a real number? Answer that first.

G. Bolzano's theorem and the intermediate value theorem

Construct a suitable auxiliary function, check continuity on a closed interval and opposite signs at the endpoints.

9E.21 BASIC

Show that the equation $\cos x=x$ has at least one solution in the interval $\left(0,\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$.

Hint: consider the function $g(x)=\cos x-x$ and check the hypotheses of the theorem.

9E.22 INTERMEDIATE

Let $f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ be a continuous function. Prove that there exists $\xi\in[0,1]$ with $f(\xi)=\xi$ (a fixed point).

Hint: apply the theorem to $g(x)=f(x)-x$ and treat the cases $f(0)=0$ or $f(1)=1$ separately.

H. Mixed — composite problems

Here limits, parameters and continuity are combined; first identify the structure of the problem.

9E.23 INTERMEDIATE

Find $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}$ such that $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^2+\alpha x+\beta}{x^2-1}=2$.

Hint: which necessary condition must the numerator satisfy at $x=1$ so that the limit does not become infinite?

9E.24 ADVANCED

Let $f$ be continuous on $[0,2]$ with $f(0)=f(2)$. Prove that there exists $c\in[0,1]$ with $f(c)=f(c+1)$.

Hint: consider $g(x)=f(x+1)-f(x)$ on $[0,1]$ and compare $g(0)$ and $g(1)$.

Exercises for the section

9E.25

Prove from the ($\varepsilon$-$\delta$) definition that $\lim_{x\to1}\dfrac{1}{x}=1$. Find an explicit $\delta(\varepsilon)$.

9E.26

Let $f(x)=\begin{cases}x^2, & x<1\\ 2x-1, & x\geq1\end{cases}$. Evaluate $\lim_{x\to1^-}f(x)$, $\lim_{x\to1^+}f(x)$ and decide whether $\lim_{x\to1}f(x)$ exists.

9E.27

Examine whether $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{1}{x^2}$ exists, by examining $x\to0^+$ and $x\to0^-$.

9E.28

Find $a\in\mathbb{R}$ so that $f(x)=\begin{cases}ax+3,&x<2\\x^2-1,&x\geq2\end{cases}$ has a limit at $x=2$.

9E.29

Find the following limits, if they exist: (α) $\lim_{x\to1}\dfrac{x^2-1}{x-1}$, (β) $\lim_{x\to4}\dfrac{\sqrt{x}-2}{x-4}$.

9E.30

Use the limit laws to evaluate: (α) $\lim_{x\to3}(x^2-5x+6)$, (β) $\lim_{x\to2}\dfrac{x^3-8}{x-2}$, (γ) $\lim_{x\to+\infty}\dfrac{3x^2-5}{2x^2+7}$.

9E.31

Evaluate: (α) $\lim_{x\to-2}\dfrac{x^2+5x+6}{x+2}$, (β) $\lim_{x\to3}\dfrac{x^3-27}{x-3}$, (γ) $\lim_{x\to3}\dfrac{x^2-9}{x^2-2x-3}$.

9E.32

If $3-x^2\leq f(x)\leq 3+x^2$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$, find $\lim_{x\to0}f(x)$ using the Squeeze Theorem.

9E.33

Evaluate: (α) $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin 3x}{x}$, (β) $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin(3x^2)}{x^2}$.

9E.34

Evaluate: (α) $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin 7x}{\sin 2x}$, (β) $\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{1-\cos 2x}{x^2}$.

9E.35

Using the Squeeze Theorem, show that $\lim_{x\to+\infty}\dfrac{\cos x}{x}=0$ and that $\lim_{x\to0}x^2\sin\dfrac{1}{x^2}=0$.

9E.36

Evaluate: (α) $\lim_{x\to3^+}\dfrac{1}{x-3}$, (β) $\lim_{x\to3^-}\dfrac{1}{x-3}$, (γ) $\lim_{x\to3}\dfrac{1}{(x-3)^2}$.

9E.37

Show that $\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{5x^3-2x}{3x^3+x^2}=\dfrac{5}{3}$ by dividing numerator and denominator by $x^3$.

9E.38

Check whether there are vertical asymptotes for $h(x)=\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2-3x+2}$ and evaluate the corresponding one-sided infinite limits. What happens at $x=1$?

9E.39

Find all asymptotes (vertical, horizontal, oblique) of $f(x)=\dfrac{2x^2-3x+1}{x+2}$. Hint: polynomial division.

9E.40

Removable discontinuity: for which $c$ is the function $f(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{x^2-9}{x-3}&x\neq3\\c&x=3\end{cases}$ continuous at $x=3$?

9E.41

Find parameters $a,b$ so that $f(x)=\begin{cases}ax+b&x<1\\2&x=1\\bx^2+a&x>1\end{cases}$ is continuous everywhere and, in addition, $f(2)=5$. (Hint: set the one-sided limits at $x=1$ equal to $f(1)$.)

9E.42

Continuity of a piecewise function: find $a,b$ so that $f(x)=\begin{cases}ax+b,&x<0\\x^2+1,&0\leq x\leq 1\\ ax-b,&x>1\end{cases}$ is continuous everywhere. (α) Examine continuity at $x=0$ and $x=1$. (β) Set the one-sided limits equal. (γ) Solve the system.

9E.43

Examine the continuity of $g(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\sin 3x}{x},&x<0\\ 3,&x=0\\ 3\cos x,&x>0\end{cases}$ at $x=0$.

9E.44

Bolzano's theorem: show that $f(x)=x^3-x-1$ has at least one root in $(1,2)$. (α) Check the hypotheses. (β) Compute $f(1)$ and $f(2)$.

9E.45

Apply Bolzano's theorem to show that the equation $x^5+x-3=0$ has at least one root in $[1,2]$, and prove that this root is unique (monotonicity).

9E.46

Use the Intermediate Value Theorem to show that the equation $e^x=3-x$ has a solution in $(0,2)$.

Composite Problems

9E.47

Show that $g(x)=x\sin(1/x)$, $x\neq0$, extends continuously to $x=0$ with $g(0)=0$. Why does $\sin(1/x)$ not extend?

9E.48

Asymptotes and modeling. The cost of producing $x$ units is $K(x)=5000+20x$ (in euros). The average cost per unit is $\bar{K}(x)=K(x)/x$. (α) Find $\lim_{x\to0^+}\bar{K}(x)$ and $\lim_{x\to+\infty}\bar{K}(x)$. (β) Which asymptotes does $\bar{K}$have? (γ) Interpret the horizontal asymptote in economic terms.

9E.51

(For engineers) The temperature along a rod of length $1$ m is given by the continuous function $T(x)$, with $T(0)=15\,^{\circ}$C and $T(1)=45\,^{\circ}$C. Prove that there is a point of the rod whose temperature is exactly $30\,^{\circ}$C. Which theorem do you use?

Composite Problems

9E.52

(For engineers) Terminal velocity. A body falling with air resistance has velocity $v(t)=v_{\infty}\left(1-e^{-kt}\right)$, $k>0$. (α) Evaluate $\lim_{t\to+\infty}v(t)$ and interpret it physically. (β) Evaluate $\lim_{t\to0^+}\dfrac{v(t)}{t}$ and show that it equals $v_{\infty}k$ (the initial acceleration).
