The Derivative of a Function

Chapter 10 · The Derivative of a Function

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by topic and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

Before every computation identify the structure of the function: sum, product, quotient or composition. The result is usually checked numerically, by comparing the value of the derivative at a point with the difference quotient $\dfrac{f(x_0+h)-f(x_0)}{h}$ for small $h$.

A. The definition and differentiability

Here you work exclusively with the limit of the difference quotient, without any differentiation rules.

10E.1 BASIC

Using the definition $f'(x)=\displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$ find the derivative: (α) $f(x)=4x-x^2$,   (β) $g(x)=\dfrac{2}{3x-1}$,   (γ) $h(x)=\sqrt{x+5}$.

10E.2 INTERMEDIATE

The function $f(x)=x\,|x|$ is given. (α) Examine by the definition whether $f$ is differentiable at $x=0$. (β) Find a formula for $f'(x)$ on all of $\mathbb{R}$. (γ) Examine whether $f'$ is in turn differentiable at $x=0$.

Hint: write $f$ piecewise and compute the two one-sided limits separately.

10E.3 ADVANCED

Let $f$ be differentiable at the point $x_0$ and let $a,b$ be constants. Show, starting from the definition, that $$\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x_0+ah)-f(x_0+bh)}{h}=(a-b)\,f'(x_0).$$ Then explain why the existence of the above limit does not by itself guarantee the differentiability of $f$ at $x_0$, by giving a suitable counterexample for $a=1$, $b=-1$.

Hint: add and subtract the term $f(x_0)$ in the numerator. For the counterexample think of an even function.

B. The differentiation rules

Product, quotient and linearity — before you compute, check whether a simplification shortens the process.

10E.4 BASIC

Compute the derivative: (α) $f(x)=(2x^3-5)(x^2+x)$,   (β) $g(x)=\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2+1}$,   (γ) $h(x)=\sqrt{x}\,(x+3)$, $x>0$.

10E.5 BASIC

Compute the derivative and simplify the result as much as possible: (α) $f(x)=x\,\tan x$,   (β) $g(x)=\dfrac{\sin x}{1+\cos x}$,   (γ) $h(x)=\dfrac{\mathrm{e}^{x}}{x^2}$, $x\neq0$.

10E.6 INTERMEDIATE

The functions $u,v$ are differentiable and $u(1)=2$, $u'(1)=-1$, $v(1)=3$, $v'(1)=4$. Compute at $x=1$ the derivative of the functions: (α) $u\,v$,   (β) $\dfrac{u}{v}$,   (γ) $u^2v$,   (δ) $\dfrac{3u-2v}{5}$.

You do not need to know the formulas of $u,v$: the differentiation rules suffice.

C. The chain rule

First identify the outer and the inner function, and only then differentiate.

10E.7 BASIC

Find the derivative: (α) $f(x)=(3x^2-2x+1)^5$,   (β) $g(x)=\mathrm{e}^{\cos x}$,   (γ) $h(x)=\ln(1+x^2)$.

10E.8 INTERMEDIATE

Find the derivative and determine its domain: (α) $f(x)=\sqrt{1+\sqrt{x}}$,   (β) $g(x)=\tan^{2}(3x)$,   (γ) $h(x)=\arcsin\!\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)$.

In (α) the rule is applied twice in succession.

10E.9 INTERMEDIATE

The function $f$ is differentiable with $f(2)=5$ and $f'(2)=-3$. We set $F(x)=f(x^2+1)$ and $G(x)=\bigl(f(x)\bigr)^{3}$. Compute $F'(1)$ and $G'(2)$.

D. The tangent and the normal line

The derivative gives the slope· the normal line has the negative reciprocal slope.

10E.10 BASIC

For the curve $y=\dfrac{1}{x}$ write the equation of the tangent and of the normal line at the point $\left(2,\dfrac12\right)$.

10E.11 INTERMEDIATE

Find the equations of all the tangent lines to the curve $y=x^2-3x$ that pass through the point $(0,-4)$.

Hint: call $a$ the abscissa of the point of tangency, write the tangent at $a$ and require that it be satisfied by $(0,-4)$. Observe that the point does not lie on the curve.

10E.12 ADVANCED

Let $a>0$. The tangent to the hyperbola $y=\dfrac{1}{x}$ at the point $\left(a,\dfrac1a\right)$ meets the $x'x$ axis at $A$ and the $y'y$ axis at $B$. (α) Find the coordinates of $A$ and $B$. (β) Show that the area of the triangle $OAB$ is independent of $a$. (γ) Show that the point of tangency is the midpoint of the segment $AB$.

E. Implicit and logarithmic differentiation

You differentiate both sides with respect to $x$, regarding $y$ as a function of $x$.

10E.13 BASIC

For the curve $x^3+y^3=6xy$ (folium of Descartes) find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ and compute the slope of the tangent at the point $(3,3)$.

10E.14 INTERMEDIATE

By logarithmic differentiation find $f'(x)$ for $\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{2x}\sqrt{x^2+1}}{(x+3)^5}$, $x>-3$, and compute the ratio $\dfrac{f'(0)}{f(0)}$.

Take logarithms first and use the properties of the logarithm, so that the differentiation becomes a sum of terms.

10E.15 ADVANCED

The curve $x^2-xy+y^2=3$ is given. (α) Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ by implicit differentiation. (β) Determine all the points of the curve at which the tangent is horizontal. (γ) Determine all the points at which the tangent is vertical.

In (β) the condition is that the numerator vanishes· in (γ) the denominator. In each case combine the condition with the equation of the curve.

F. The derivative of a parametric and of a polar curve

The slope arises as a quotient of rates with respect to the parameter, without eliminating the parameter.

10E.16 BASIC

For the parametric curve $x=2t+1$, $y=t^2-3t$ find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ as a function of $t$, the slope at $t=2$ and the value of $t$ for which the tangent is horizontal.

10E.17 INTERMEDIATE

The polar curve $r=\theta$ (spiral of Archimedes), $\theta\ge 0$, is given. Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for $\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and write the equation of the tangent at the corresponding point.

Hint: first pass to parametric form with $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$.

10E.18 ADVANCED

The parametric curve $x=t-\dfrac{1}{t}$, $y=t+\dfrac{1}{t}$, with $t>0$, is given. (α) Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ as a function of $t$. (β) Find $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ as a function of $t$. (γ) Show that the points of the curve satisfy the relation $y^2-x^2=4$ and verify the result of (α) by implicit differentiation of this relation.

In (β) recall that $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\dfrac{\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)}{\frac{dx}{dt}}$.

G. Mean value theorems

First you check the hypotheses, then you apply the conclusion — never the other way round.

10E.19 BASIC

For the function $f(x)=x^3-4x$ on the interval $[-2,2]$ verify the hypotheses of Rolle’s theorem and find all the values $c\in(-2,2)$ that satisfy its conclusion.

10E.20 BASIC

For the function $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$ on the interval $[0,4]$ check the hypotheses of the mean value theorem, find the $c\in(0,4)$ that satisfies it and interpret it geometrically.

10E.21 INTERMEDIATE

By applying the mean value theorem to the function $f(t)=\ln(1+t)$ on the interval $[0,x]$, prove that $$\frac{x}{1+x}<\ln(1+x)<x\qquad \text{for every } x>0 .$$ Then use the inequality to bound the number $\ln(1.2)$ between two rational numbers.

Hint: the $c$ given by the theorem satisfies $0<c<x$· bound the quantity $\dfrac{1}{1+c}$ from above and from below.

H. Related rates of change

First write the relation that connects the quantities and then differentiate with respect to time.

10E.22 BASIC

The edge of a cube increases at a rate of $0.5$ cm/s. At what rate do (α) the volume and (β) the total surface area of the cube increase, at the moment when the edge is $10$ cm?

10E.23 INTERMEDIATE

A ladder of length $5$ m leans against a vertical wall. Its base moves away from the wall at a constant speed of $0.6$ m/s. At what rate does the top of the ladder come down at the moment when the base is $3$ m from the wall? What sign does this rate have and why?

Hint: the Pythagorean theorem gives the relation between the two distances· the length of the ladder is constant.

10E.24 ADVANCED

A person of height $1.8$ m moves away at a speed of $1.5$ m/s from the base of a lamppost of height $5.4$ m. (α) At what rate does the length of their shadow increase? (β) At what rate does the tip of the shadow move along the ground? (γ) Explain why the two rates do not depend on the distance of the person from the lamppost.

Hint: call $x$ the distance from the lamppost and $s$ the length of the shadow· the two similar triangles give a linear relation between $x$ and $s$.

10E.30

$f(x)=x^3-3x$: find the points where $f'(x_0)=0$ and write the corresponding (horizontal) tangent lines.

10E.31

Find the equation of the tangent and of the normal at the indicated point: (α) $y=\ln x$ at $(1,0)$, (β) $y=\mathrm{e}^{-x}$ at $(0,1)$.

10E.32

For $f(x)=x^3-3x^2$ find: (α) the points with a horizontal tangent, (β) the points at which the tangent is parallel to the line $y=9x+1$.

10E.34

10E.35

10E.36

Find the derivative: (α) $f(x)=\sin^3(2x)$, (β) $g(x)=e^{-x^2}$, (γ) $h(x)=\ln\bigl(x+\sqrt{x^2+1}\bigr)$ (simplify; what do you recognize?).

10E.37

Find $dy/dx$ if $x^2+3y^2=12$ and compute the slope of the tangent at the point $(3,1)$.

10E.38

Find the tangent to the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{4}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$ at the point $\left(1,\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$.

10E.39

Logarithmic differentiation: $f(x)=\dfrac{(x^2+1)^3\sqrt{x+1}}{(x-1)^2}$.

10E.40

Differentiate by logarithmic differentiation: (α) $y=x^{\ln x}$ ($x>0$), (β) $y=\dfrac{x^2\,\sqrt[3]{x+1}}{(2x-3)^4}$, (γ) $f(x)=(\sin x)^{x}$, $0<x<\pi$.

10E.41

Higher-order derivatives: for $f(x)=x^3\ln x$ ($x>0$) find $f'''(x)$.

10E.42

Find a general formula for $f^{(n)}(x)$ when $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{1-x}$ and when $g(x)=\ln(1+x)$.

10E.43

Electric power. The power dissipated in a resistor is $W=RI^2$ (in W), where $R$ is the resistance (in $\Omega$) and $I$ the current (in A). For $R=10\,\Omega$ constant, the current increases from $3$ A to $3.1$ A. Estimate the change in the power by means of the differential and compare it with the exact change.

10E.44

For the parametric curve $x=\mathrm{e}^{t}$, $y=t\,\mathrm{e}^{t}$: (α) find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ as a function of $t$, (β) write the equation of the tangent at $t=0$.

10E.45

Find the points of horizontal and of vertical tangency of the curve $x=t^3-3t$, $y=t^2$.

10E.46

Polar curve $r=1+\sin\theta$: find the points of horizontal tangency for $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$.

10E.47

Rolle’s theorem. Verify the hypotheses and find $c$: (α) $f(x)=x^2-4$ on $[-2,2]$, (β) $f(x)=\sin x$ on $[0,\pi]$.

10E.48

A right triangle: one leg increases at $2$ cm/s, the other decreases at $1$ cm/s. At the moment when $a=6$ cm and $b=8$ cm, at what rate does the hypotenuse change?

10E.49

Related rate: the area of a circle increases at $2\ \mathrm{cm^2/s}$; how fast does the radius increase when $r=5$ cm?

10E.50

A circle has a radius that increases at a rate of $0.5$ cm/s. At what rate do the circumference and the area increase when $r=4$ cm?

10E.51

A square has a side that increases at a rate of $3$ cm/s. At what rate does its diagonal increase when the side is $5$ cm?

10E.52

A point moves along the curve $y=\sqrt{x}$ in such a way that $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=4$ cm/s. At what rate does the ordinate $y$ change when $x=4$ cm?

Composite Problems

10E.53

The population of a bacterial culture is given by $N(t)=100\,\mathrm{e}^{0.5t}$ ($t$ in hours). (α) Find the rate of growth $N'(t)$. (β) Show that $N'(t)=0.5\,N(t)$ and interpret the relation. (γ) When does the rate become $100$ bacteria per hour?

10E.54

By combining Bolzano’s theorem and Rolle’s theorem, prove that the equation $x^3+x-1=0$ has exactly one real root.

Composite Problems

10E.55

Rate of change and a model: The temperature of a cooling body is given by $T(t)=T_{\pi}+\left(T_0-T_{\pi}\right)e^{-kt}$, where $T_{\pi}$ is the ambient temperature. (α) Find $T'(t)$. (β) Show that $T'(t)=-k\bigl(T(t)-T_{\pi}\bigr)$ (Newton’s law of cooling). (γ) What does the sign of $T'$ mean geometrically for the graph of $T$?
