Techniques of Integration

Chapter 16 · Techniques of Integration

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by technique and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

In every indefinite integral the constant $+C$ is understood. The result of every exercise is always checked by differentiating: if the derivative of your antiderivative gives the integrand, the solution is correct. You do not need an answer key.

A. Substitution

Look for the pair “function – its derivative” inside the integral.

16E.1 BASIC

$\displaystyle\int x\,e^{x^2}\,dx$

16E.2 BASIC

$\displaystyle\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}\,dx$

16E.3 INTERMEDIATE

$\displaystyle\int \tan x\,dx$

Hint: write $\tan x=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$.

16E.4 INTERMEDIATE

$\displaystyle\int \frac{\ln x}{x}\,dx$

B. Integration by parts

Choose as $u$ the factor that simplifies under differentiation (the LIATE rule).

16E.5 BASIC

$\displaystyle\int x\cos x\,dx$

16E.6 INTERMEDIATE

$\displaystyle\int x^2 e^{x}\,dx$

How many successive applications are needed, and why?

16E.7 INTERMEDIATE

$\displaystyle\int \arctan x\,dx$

Hint: write $\arctan x=\arctan x\cdot 1$.

16E.8 ADVANCED

$\displaystyle\int e^{x}\cos x\,dx$

After two applications the original integral reappears. Treat it as an equation.

C. Trigonometric integrals

Odd exponent → substitution. Even exponents → reduction identities.

16E.9 BASIC

$\displaystyle\int \sin^3 x\,dx$

16E.10 INTERMEDIATE

$\displaystyle\int \sin^2 x\cos^3 x\,dx$

16E.11 BASIC

$\displaystyle\int \cos^2 x\,dx$

D. Trigonometric substitution & completing the square

A root of the form $\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$, $\sqrt{a^2+x^2}$ or $\sqrt{x^2-a^2}$: a right triangle resolves the root.

16E.12 BASIC

$\displaystyle\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}$

16E.13 ADVANCED

$\displaystyle\int \sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx$

After the substitution an even power appears; a reduction step is also needed.

16E.14 INTERMEDIATE

$\displaystyle\int \frac{dx}{x^2+2x+5}$

First complete the square in the denominator.

E. Partial fractions

Factor the denominator first; an irreducible quadratic factor gives a logarithm and an arctangent.

16E.15 BASIC

$\displaystyle\int \frac{dx}{x^2-1}$

16E.16 INTERMEDIATE

$\displaystyle\int \frac{3x+1}{(x-1)(x+2)}\,dx$

16E.17 ADVANCED

$\displaystyle\int \frac{dx}{x(x^2+1)}$

The factor $x^2+1$ is irreducible: what form must the corresponding numerator have?

F. Mixed — choosing a method

The technique is not given here. First identify the structure, then evaluate.

16E.18 BASIC

$\displaystyle\int \frac{x}{x+1}\,dx$

Careful: partial fractions are not needed.

16E.19 BASIC

$\displaystyle\int x\ln x\,dx$

16E.20 ADVANCED

$\displaystyle\int e^{\sqrt{x}}\,dx$

Two techniques are needed, in this order.

16E.21 INTERMEDIATE

$\displaystyle\int \frac{dx}{1+e^{x}}$

Hint: $\dfrac{1}{1+e^x}=1-\dfrac{e^x}{1+e^x}$.

16E.22 ADVANCED

$\displaystyle\int \sec^3 x\,dx$

The classic example of circular integration by parts.
