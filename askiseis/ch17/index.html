Applications of the Integral

Chapter 17 · Applications of the Integral

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by application and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

You always draw the region first and mark the axis of revolution: that is what decides the choice between disks, washers and shells. In the physical applications you work in the International System of Units, with $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ for water and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Every numerical result you check by an order-of-magnitude estimate before you accept it.

A. Numerical integration (the trapezoidal rule)

The trapezoidal rule replaces the curve by chords; the error is controlled by the second derivative.

17E.1 BASIC

Apply the trapezoidal rule with $n=4$ to $\displaystyle\int_0^2 x^3\,dx$ and compare the estimate with the exact value of the integral.

Hint: the step is $h=\dfrac{b-a}{n}$ and the interior nodes are weighted by a factor $2$.

17E.2 INTERMEDIATE

The velocity of a vehicle moving in a straight line was recorded every $5\,\mathrm{s}$:

$t\,(\mathrm{s}):\ 0,\ 5,\ 10,\ 15,\ 20,\ 25,\ 30$

$v\,(\mathrm{m/s}):\ 0.0;\ 7.5;\ 13.0;\ 17.5;\ 20.0;\ 19.5;\ 16.0$

Estimate by the trapezoidal rule ($n=6$) the distance traveled in the $30\,\mathrm{s}$ and compute the average velocity of the vehicle.

Hint: the distance is the integral of the velocity with respect to time; you do not have a formula for $v(t)$, only values at equally spaced nodes.

17E.3 INTERMEDIATE

Using the bound $|E_T|\le\dfrac{(b-a)^3}{12n^2}M_2$, with $M_2=\max_{[a,b]}|f''|$, find the least number of subintervals $n$ for which the trapezoidal rule computes $\displaystyle\int_0^1 e^{x}\,dx$ with an error (α) $|E_T|<10^{-3}$ and (β) $|E_T|<10^{-5}$. Comment on how $n$ changes when you require a hundred times better accuracy.

Hint: first locate at which endpoint of $[0,1]$ the quantity $|f''|$ is maximized.

17E.4 ADVANCED

Let $f$ be twice differentiable and convex on $[a,b]$, that is $f''(x)\ge 0$ for every $x\in[a,b]$. Prove that for every $n$ we have $\displaystyle T_n\ \ge\ \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$, that is, the trapezoidal rule always overestimates the integral. Interpret the conclusion geometrically and state the corresponding result for a concave $f$.

Hint: it is enough to show that on each subinterval $[x_{i-1},x_i]$ the chord joining the endpoints of the graph lies above the graph.

B. Simpson's rule and error estimation

Simpson's rule passes a parabola through three successive points and requires an even number of subintervals.

17E.5 BASIC

Apply Simpson's rule with $n=4$ to $\displaystyle\int_0^2 \frac{dx}{1+x}$ and compare with the exact value $\ln 3$. Give the absolute error to four decimal places.

Hint: the weights of the interior nodes alternate, $4$ at the odd and $2$ at the even indices.

17E.6 INTERMEDIATE

On the basis of the bound $|E_S|\le\dfrac{(b-a)^5}{180n^4}M_4$, where $M_4=\max_{[a,b]}|f^{(4)}|$, find the least even $n$ for which Simpson's rule computes $\displaystyle\int_0^1 e^{-x}\,dx$ with an error $|E_S|<10^{-5}$. Compare the $n$ you have found with the corresponding one for the trapezoidal rule for the same accuracy requirement.

Hint: for the exponential function the fourth derivative is computed immediately; note that $n$ must be even.

17E.7 ADVANCED

The integral $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \sqrt{1+x^3}\,dx$ cannot be expressed in terms of elementary functions. (α) Estimate it by Simpson's rule for $n=4$, giving four decimal places. (β) Repeat the estimate with the trapezoidal rule for the same $n$. (γ) Which of the two estimates do you expect to be more accurate, and by what argument, given that the exact value is not available?

Hint: keep at least six decimal places in the intermediate values $f(x_i)$, so that the rounding does not corrupt the fourth decimal.

C. Volumes by disks and washers

Every strip perpendicular to the axis of revolution generates a disk or, when the region has a hole, a washer.

17E.8 BASIC

The curve $y=e^{x}$, $x\in[0,1]$, is revolved about the $x$ axis. Find the volume of the solid by the method of disks.

17E.9 BASIC

The region bounded by the curve $x=y^{2}$, the $y$ axis and the lines $y=0$, $y=2$ is revolved about the $y$ axis. Find the volume by integrating with respect to $y$.

Hint: here the disks are horizontal and their radius is $x=y^2$.

17E.10 INTERMEDIATE

Method of washers: the region enclosed by the parabola $y=4-x^{2}$ and the line $y=x+2$ is revolved about the $x$ axis. Compute the volume of the solid.

Hint: first find the points of intersection of the two curves and determine which of them gives the outer radius over the whole interval of integration.

D. Volumes by cylindrical shells

A vertical strip at distance $x$ from the axis unrolls into a rectangle of area $2\pi x\,f(x)\,dx$.

17E.11 BASIC

The region under the curve $y=x^{2}+1$, $x\in[0,2]$, is revolved about the $y$ axis. Find the volume by the method of cylindrical shells.

17E.12 INTERMEDIATE

The region between $y=\sin x$ and the $x$ axis, for $x\in[0,\pi]$, is revolved about the $y$ axis. Compute the volume by shells and explain why the method of disks would here be clearly more difficult.

Hint: the integral $\displaystyle\int x\sin x\,dx$ is handled by integration by parts.

17E.13 INTERMEDIATE

The region enclosed by the curves $y=x$ and $y=x^{3}$ for $x\in[0,1]$ is revolved about the $y$ axis. Find the volume by the method of shells.

Hint: the height of the shell at position $x$ is the difference of the two functions; check which one is larger on $[0,1]$.

E. Arc length

The root $\sqrt{1+[f'(x)]^{2}}$ becomes manageable only when the quantity under the root is a perfect square.

17E.14 BASIC

Find the length of the arc of the curve $y=\dfrac{2}{3}(x-1)^{3/2}$ from $x=1$ to $x=4$.

Hint: after squaring, the quantity $1+[f'(x)]^{2}$ simplifies to a monomial.

17E.15 INTERMEDIATE

Find the length of the arc of the curve $y=\dfrac{x^{3}}{3}+\dfrac{1}{4x}$ from $x=1$ to $x=2$.

Hint: $1+[f'(x)]^{2}$ is a perfect square; write it as $\bigl(A(x)+B(x)\bigr)^{2}$ before taking the root.

17E.16 ADVANCED

The astroid is given in parametric form by $x=a\cos^{3}t$, $y=a\sin^{3}t$, with $a>0$ and $t\in[0,2\pi]$. Find its total length.

Hint: use the symmetry and compute only the part $t\in[0,\pi/2]$. Note that $\sqrt{u^{2}}=|u|$ in simplifying the root.

F. The area of a surface of revolution

Every elementary arc $ds$ generates a thin band of area $2\pi f(x)\,ds$.

17E.17 BASIC

The line segment $y=\dfrac{x}{3}+1$, $x\in[0,3]$, is revolved about the $x$ axis. Find the area of the resulting surface and verify it with the geometric formula for the lateral surface of a frustum of a cone, $S=\pi(r_{1}+r_{2})\ell$, where $\ell$ is the slant height.

17E.18 INTERMEDIATE

A zone of a sphere. The curve $y=\sqrt{R^{2}-x^{2}}$ is revolved about the $x$ axis for $x\in[a,b]$, where $-R<a<b<R$. Show that the area of the resulting zone is $S=2\pi R\,(b-a)$, that is, it depends only on the height of the zone and not on its position. Check that for $a\to -R$ and $b\to R$ the formula $S=4\pi R^{2}$ is recovered.

Hint: first compute the product $f(x)\sqrt{1+[f'(x)]^{2}}$ and observe what simplifies.

G. Center of mass and moments

The center of mass is a ratio of moment to mass, while the moment of inertia weights each elementary mass by the square of its distance from the axis.

17E.19 BASIC

A thin rod occupies the interval $[0,2]$ (in $\mathrm{m}$) and has linear density $\rho(x)=3-x$ (in $\mathrm{kg/m}$). Find (α) the total mass, in $\mathrm{kg}$, and (β) the position $\overline{x}$ of the center of mass.

Hint: before computing, explain why $\overline{x}$ must be less than $1\,\mathrm{m}$.

17E.20 INTERMEDIATE

Find the center of mass $(\overline{x},\overline{y})$ of the homogeneous plane region enclosed by $y=\sin x$ and the $x$ axis, for $x\in[0,\pi]$.

Hint: $\overline{x}$ follows at once from the symmetry about the line $x=\pi/2$; for $\overline{y}$ you need $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi}\sin^{2}x\,dx$.

17E.21 ADVANCED

A homogeneous circular disk of radius $R$ has constant surface density $\rho$ and total mass $M=\rho\pi R^{2}$. Using the formula $I_x=\dfrac{\rho}{3}\displaystyle\int_a^b\bigl([f(x)]^{3}-[g(x)]^{3}\bigr)\,dx$, prove by integration that the moment of inertia of the disk about one of its diameters is $I=\dfrac{1}{4}MR^{2}$. Then find the radius of gyration $k=\sqrt{I/M}$ and comment on its position relative to $R$.

Hint: you will arrive at $\displaystyle\int_{-R}^{R}\bigl(R^{2}-x^{2}\bigr)^{3/2}\,dx$; handle it by a trigonometric substitution and a power-reduction identity.

H. Work and hydrostatic pressure

The work of a variable force: $W=\displaystyle\int_a^b F(x)\,dx$; the hydrostatic force on a vertical wall: $F=\displaystyle\int_0^h \rho g\,y\,w(y)\,dy$.

17E.22 BASIC

A force of magnitude $F(x)=\dfrac{10}{(1+x)^{2}}$ (in $\mathrm{N}$) acts along the direction of motion of a body. Compute its work for a displacement from $x=0$ to $x=3\,\mathrm{m}$.

Hint: a simple substitution suffices; give the result in $\mathrm{J}$.

17E.23 INTERMEDIATE

A conical tank with its vertex pointing downwards has height $4\,\mathrm{m}$ and top radius $2\,\mathrm{m}$, and is full of water ($\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^{3}}$, $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^{2}}$). Compute the work required to pump all the water up to the rim of the tank.

Hint: if $y$ is the height above the vertex, the elementary layer of thickness $dy$ has radius $y/2$ and must be raised by $4-y$.

17E.24 ADVANCED

A vertical dam wall has the shape of a trapezoid: its width is $8\,\mathrm{m}$ at the free surface of the water, $4\,\mathrm{m}$ at the bottom, and the depth of the water is $6\,\mathrm{m}$. With $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^{3}}$ and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^{2}}$, find (α) the total hydrostatic force on the wall and (β) the depth $\overline{y}$ of the center of pressure. Check whether $\overline{y}$ coincides with the depth of the centroid of the trapezoid and comment.

Hint: first express the width $w(y)$ as a linear function of the depth $y$ and use $\overline{y}=\dfrac{\int_0^h y^{2}w(y)\,dy}{\int_0^h y\,w(y)\,dy}$.
