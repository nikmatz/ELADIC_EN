Applications of the Derivative and Curve Sketching

Chapter 12 · Applications of the Derivative and Curve Sketching

How to use them. The exercises are arranged by topic and, within each group, in increasing order of difficulty: BASIC INTERMEDIATE ADVANCED.

Before every application of L'Hôpital's rule you confirm that it is indeed an indeterminate form $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$; otherwise the rule leads to an error. In the study of functions every finding is checked by another: the sign of $f'$ must agree with the shape of the graph, the asymptotes with the limits at the ends of the domain and the inflection points with the change of convexity. In Newton's method, the stabilization of the decimal digits from one iteration to the next is itself the verification. You do not need a solutions manual.

A. L'Hôpital's rule

First you identify the form, then you differentiate numerator and denominator separately.

12E.1 BASIC

Compute the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin 5x}{\sin 2x}$.

Hint: first confirm that it is a form $\frac{0}{0}$.

12E.2 BASIC

Compute the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2}\frac{x^{3}-8}{x^{2}-4}$ by L'Hôpital's rule and verify the result by factoring the numerator and the denominator.

12E.3 INTERMEDIATE

Compute the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\ln\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{x}\right)}{x}$.

Hint: the form is $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$. After the first application, examine whether the new expression is still indeterminate or whether the limit follows directly.

B. The indeterminate forms $0\cdot\infty$, $\infty-\infty$, $1^{\infty}$

None of these is handled directly: first you reduce them to $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$.

12E.4 BASIC

Compute the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty} x\,\sin\frac{3}{x}$.

Hint: form $\infty\cdot 0$. Move the factor $x$ to the denominator by writing it as $\frac{1}{x}$.

12E.5 INTERMEDIATE

Compute the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 1}\left(\frac{1}{\ln x}-\frac{1}{x-1}\right)$.

Hint: form $\infty-\infty$. Bring the two fractions to a common denominator; the rule will have to be applied twice.

12E.6 ADVANCED

Compute the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\left(\cos x\right)^{1/x^{2}}$.

Hint: form $1^{\infty}$. Set $y=(\cos x)^{1/x^{2}}$, study $\ln y$ and return to the required limit by using the continuity of the exponential function.

C. The tangent and the normal line

The tangent has slope $f'(x_{0})$ and the normal slope $-\dfrac{1}{f'(x_{0})}$, when $f'(x_{0})\neq 0$.

12E.7 BASIC

The function $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$ is given. Write the equations of the tangent and of the normal line at the point with $x_{0}=4$ and find the point at which the tangent meets the $x$ axis.

12E.8 BASIC

The function $f(x)=x^{3}-3x^{2}+2$ is given. Write the equations of the tangent and of the normal line at the point with $x_{0}=1$.

12E.9 INTERMEDIATE

Find the equations of all the tangent lines to the parabola $y=x^{2}$ that pass through the point $(0,-1)$.

Hint: $(0,-1)$ does not lie on the parabola, so it is not a point of tangency. Call $a$ the abscissa of the point of tangency, write the tangent there and require that it be satisfied by the coordinates of $(0,-1)$.

D. Newton's method

At each step you replace the curve by its tangent line: $x_{n+1}=x_{n}-\dfrac{f(x_{n})}{f'(x_{n})}$.

12E.10 BASIC

Approximate the number $\sqrt[3]{7}$ by Newton's method. (α) Choose a suitable function $f$ that has $\sqrt[3]{7}$ as a root. (β) Write the iterative formula and simplify it. (γ) Carry out two iterations starting from $x_{0}=2$ and compare with the value given by a calculator.

12E.11 INTERMEDIATE

Let $a>0$. Apply Newton's method to the function $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{x}-a$. (α) Show that the iterative formula is written as $x_{n+1}=x_{n}\left(2-a\,x_{n}\right)$. (β) Explain why this formula computes $\dfrac{1}{a}$ without any division being carried out. (γ) With $a=3$ and $x_{0}=0.3$ carry out three iterations.

Hint: this algorithm is used in processors precisely because it requires only multiplications and subtractions. Observe how many correct digits are added at each step.

12E.12 INTERMEDIATE

The equation $x^{3}+2x-5=0$ is given. (α) Show, by studying the monotonicity, that it has exactly one real root and locate it in an interval of length $1$. (β) Carry out two iterations of Newton's method with $x_{0}=1.5$, keeping four decimal digits. (γ) Estimate the error by the criterion $\left|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right|$.

E. Asymptotes

Vertical ones from the infinite limits, horizontal and oblique ones from the behavior at $\pm\infty$.

12E.13 BASIC

Find the domain and all the asymptotes of $f(x)=\dfrac{2x-5}{x+3}$.

12E.14 INTERMEDIATE

The function $f(x)=\dfrac{x^{2}+x-2}{x^{2}-4}$ is given. (α) Find the domain. (β) Factor and simplify the fraction and explain why only one of the two roots of the denominator gives a vertical asymptote; what happens at the other one? (γ) Find the horizontal asymptote.

12E.15 INTERMEDIATE

The function $f(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}+4x}$ is given. (α) Find the domain. (β) Show that the graph has two different oblique asymptotes, one as $x\to+\infty$ and one as $x\to-\infty$.

Hint: write $\sqrt{x^{2}+4x}=|x|\sqrt{1+\frac{4}{x}}$ and note that $|x|=-x$ when $x<0$. For the constant $\beta$ of each asymptote use the conjugate expression.

F. Complete study and sketching of a graph

You always follow the same order: domain, symmetries, intercepts, asymptotes, $f'$, $f''$, table, figure.

12E.16 BASIC

Study completely and sketch the graph of $f(x)=x^{3}-6x^{2}+9x$: domain, points of intersection with the axes, monotonicity, local extrema, convexity and inflection point.

12E.17 INTERMEDIATE

Study completely and sketch the graph of $f(x)=\dfrac{x^{2}}{x^{2}+3}$: symmetry, sign, asymptotes, monotonicity, extrema, convexity and inflection points.

Hint: first write $f(x)=1-\dfrac{3}{x^{2}+3}$; the differentiations then become considerably shorter.

12E.18 ADVANCED

Study completely and sketch the graph of $f(x)=x\,\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/2}$: symmetry, limits at $\pm\infty$ and asymptotes, monotonicity, local extrema, convexity and inflection points.

Hint: both derivatives have the common factor $\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/2}>0$, so the signs are determined exclusively by the polynomial factors that remain.

G. Applications in engineering

You model first, restrict the domain to physically acceptable values and then optimize.

12E.19 BASIC

An open tank with a square base and without a lid has volume $32\ \mathrm{m^{3}}$. (α) Express the total surface area $S$ as a function of the side $x$ of the base, stating the domain. (β) Find the dimensions that minimize the surface area. (γ) Verify by the second derivative test.

12E.20 INTERMEDIATE

A source with electromotive force $E$ and internal resistance $r$ feeds a load resistance $R>0$. The power dissipated in the load is $\displaystyle P(R)=\frac{E^{2}R}{(R+r)^{2}}$. (α) Find the value of $R$ that maximizes $P$. (β) Compute the maximum power in terms of $E$ and $r$. (γ) Compute $\displaystyle\lim_{R\to+\infty}P(R)$ and interpret it physically.

Hint: $E$ and $r$ are constants; the only variable is $R$.

12E.21 ADVANCED

From a cylindrical log of diameter $d$ a beam of rectangular cross-section of width $w$ and height $h$ is cut. The bending strength of the beam is proportional to the product $w\,h^{2}$. (α) Express the strength as a function of $w$ alone, with $0<w<d$. (β) Find the dimensions of the cross-section of maximum strength. (γ) Show that the ratio $h/w$ of the optimal cross-section is constant, independent of the diameter $d$.

Hint: the rectangular cross-section is inscribed in the circle of diameter $d$, hence $w^{2}+h^{2}=d^{2}$.

H. Mixed and composite problems

Here several tools of the chapter are combined; first identify what is being asked.

12E.22 INTERMEDIATE

Compute the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln(\sin x)}{\ln x}$.

Hint: this is a form $\frac{-\infty}{-\infty}$. After applying the rule and a simplification you will recognize a basic limit of Chapter 9.

12E.23 ADVANCED

The function $f(x)=\dfrac{x^{2}+ax+b}{x-1}$ is given, with $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$. Determine $a$ and $b$ so that the graph of $f$ has the line $y=x+3$ as an oblique asymptote and a local extremum at the point with $x=3$. For the values you find, determine also the type of the extremum.

Hint: first carry out the polynomial division, so that $f$ is written as the sum of a linear polynomial and a fraction that tends to zero.

12E.24 ADVANCED

The function $f(x)=x^{4}-4x+1$ is given. (α) Study the monotonicity of $f$ and show that the equation $f(x)=0$ has exactly two real roots. (β) Locate each of them in an interval of length $1$ with the help of Bolzano's theorem. (γ) Approximate the larger root by two iterations of Newton's method, starting from $x_{0}=1.5$.

Hint: in (α) it is enough to see how many critical points $f$ has and what the sign of its minimum value is.

12E.40

12E.43

12E.44

12E.45

12E.46

12E.47

12E.48

12E.49

A divided fence. A rectangular region is fenced and divided into two equal rectangles by an additional fence parallel to one side. The total available length of fencing is $200$ m. (α) Express the area as a function of one dimension. (β) Find the dimensions of maximum area. (γ) Verify by the second derivative test.

12E.50

An inscribed triangle: A right triangle is inscribed in a circle of radius $R$ (the hypotenuse is a diameter). (α) Express its area in terms of a suitable angle. (β) Find the maximum area.

12E.51

A cylindrical container: For a given volume of $V=1$ liter, find the dimensions (radius $r$ and height $h$) that minimize the total surface area. (Hint: $V = \pi r^2 h$, $S = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh$.)

12E.52

A cylindrical container open at the top, of capacity $V=500\pi\ \mathrm{cm^3}$: find the diameter and the height that minimize the surface area of the material.

12E.53

A box open at the top is made from a square sheet of cardboard of side $a$, by cutting small squares of side $x$ from the corners. Show that the maximum volume is attained for $x=a/6$.

Composite Problems

12E.54

The Lagrange remainder. For $f(x)=\cos x$ about $x_0=0$:

  1. Write the Taylor polynomial $P_4(x)$.
  2. Estimate the error $|f(x)-P_4(x)|$ for $x\in[0;\,0.5]$ with $|R_4(x)|\le\frac{M_5}{5!}|x|^5$.
  3. How many terms are enough so that the error is $<10^{-6}$ on $x=0.5$?

12E.55

Find the third-order Maclaurin polynomial of $f(x)=\sqrt[3]{1+x}$ and use it to approximate $\sqrt[3]{1.03}$. Estimate the error.

12E.56

The minimum length of a beam. A horizontal beam must be carried from a corridor of width $a=2$ m into a perpendicular corridor of width $b=2$ m. (α) Express the length $L(\theta)$ of the part that touches the corner as a function of the angle $\theta$. (β) By minimizing $L(\theta)$, find the maximum length of beam that can turn the corner. (γ) Show that the minimum is attained for $\theta=\pi/4$.

12E.57

Optimization with a numerical solution. Let $f(x)=x^2+\mathrm{e}^{-x}$ be given. (α) Show that $f$ has a unique global minimum. (β) The condition $f'(x)=0$ gives $2x=\mathrm{e}^{-x}$, which cannot be solved analytically: carry out three steps of Newton's method with $x_0=0.5$ to approximate the solution. (γ) Compute the minimum value.
