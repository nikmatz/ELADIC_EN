Taylor polynomials and the radius of convergence

The Taylor polynomial of degree n of f about 0 is Tₙ(x) = Σₖ₌₀ⁿ f⁽ₖ⁾(0)·xₖ/k!. Each increase of n adds one more term and improves the approximation near the center.

f(x) − Tₙ(x) = Rₙ(x) = f⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾(c)·xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1)!  (Lagrange remainder)

For sin, cos and eˣ the series converges for every x (R = ∞): as n increases, the curve Tₙ “sticks” to f over an ever wider interval. For ln(1+x) and 1/(1−x) the radius is R = 1: outside (−1, 1) no increase of n helps — the error blows up. This is the difference between “the series converges” and “the series converges to the function”.
