Geometric Interpretation of Eigenvectors

Chapter 5: Eigenvectors - Convergence toward the dominant direction

Learning objective: We repeatedly apply a linear transformation to many random vectors. Most vectors gradually align with the dominant eigenvector (the one with the largest eigenvalue). This reveals the geometric meaning of eigenvectors.
Repeated application: A^n·v → λ₁^n·v₁ as n → ∞ (for |λ₁| > |λ₂|)
Geometric interpretation: Every vector (dot) is a mixture of the eigenvectors v₁ and v₂. Under repeated application of A, the component with the largest eigenvalue |λ₁| dominates, and the vectors converge toward the direction of v₁.

Geometric Interpretation of Eigenvectors

The eigenvectors are the directions left unchanged by the transformation (only scaled).

Ax = λx: x keeps its direction. If λ>1 it is stretched, if 0<λ<1 it is contracted, if λ<0 it is reflected.

Eigenvectors belonging to distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent. A matrix with n distinct eigenvalues is diagonalizable.
