Learning objective: A function f is continuous at x=a if: (1) f(a) is defined, (2) lim(x→a) f(x) exists, (3) lim(x→a) f(x) = f(a). There are three types of discontinuity: removable, jump and essential (the infinite one shown here is a case of an essential discontinuity).
Continuous: No problem at all. The limit = the value of the function.

Continuity of a Function

f is continuous at a if: f(a) is defined, lim f(x) exists, and lim f(x) = f(a).

Discontinuities: removable (the limit exists, but f(a) is missing or differs), jump (the one-sided limits exist, are finite and differ), essential (one of the one-sided limits does not exist as a real number — e.g. an infinite discontinuity).

Bolzano's theorem (the special case k=0 of the Intermediate Value Theorem): if f is continuous on [a,b] and f(a)·f(b)<0, then ∃c∈(a,b): f(c)=0. The general IVT states that f attains every value k between f(a) and f(b). This is the basis of numerical root-finding methods.
