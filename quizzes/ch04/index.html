Linear Transformations

Chapter 4 · Linear Maps

Self-Assessment

10 questions on the definition, kernel, image, representation matrix and change of basis.

Question 1 / 10

Every linear transformation maps the zero vector to the zero vector.

✓ Answer: Correct
$T(0)=T(0\cdot v)=0\cdot T(v)=0$. A basic property.
Question 2 / 10

The kernel of $T: V\to W$ is:

✓ Answer: b) the set $\{v\in V: T(v)=0\}$
$\ker T=\{v\in V: T(v)=0_W\}$. The image is $\{T(v): v\in V\}$.
Question 3 / 10

$T$ is one-to-one (injective) if and only if:

✓ Answer: b) $\ker T=\{0\}$
$T$ is one-to-one $\Leftrightarrow$ $\ker T=\{0\}$ (trivial kernel).
Question 4 / 10

The nullity plus the rank of a linear transformation $T:V\to W$ equals $\dim V$.

✓ Answer: Correct
Rank–nullity theorem: $\dim(\ker T)+\dim(\mathrm{Im}T)=\dim V$.
Question 5 / 10

If $T: \mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^2$ and $\mathrm{rank}(T)=2$, then $\dim(\ker T)=$

✓ Answer: b) 1
$\dim(\ker T)=\dim(\mathbb{R}^3)-\mathrm{rank}(T)=3-2=1$.
Question 6 / 10

The representation matrix of $T$ depends on:

✓ Answer: b) the bases of $V$ and $W$
The representation matrix changes according to the bases chosen in the domain and the codomain.
Question 7 / 10

A linear transformation $T:V\to V$ with $\ker T=\{0\}$ is an isomorphism if $\dim V<\infty$.

✓ Answer: Correct
In finite dimension, one-to-one $\Leftrightarrow$ onto $\Leftrightarrow$ isomorphism.
Question 8 / 10

The change of basis from $\mathcal{B}$ to $\mathcal{B}'$ is carried out by means of:

✓ Answer: b) the change-of-basis matrix $P$
The change-of-basis matrix $P$ transforms the coordinates: $[v]_{\mathcal{B}'}=P^{-1}[v]_{\mathcal{B}}$.
Question 9 / 10

If $T: \mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}^2$ is a rotation by $90°$, then its representation matrix with respect to the standard basis is:

✓ Answer: a) $\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$
Rotation by $\theta=90°$: $R=\begin{pmatrix}\cos90°&-\sin90°\\\sin90°&\cos90°\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$.
Question 10 / 10

The zero transformation $T(v)=0$ is linear.

✓ Answer: Correct
$T(\lambda u+\mu v)=0=\lambda\cdot0+\mu\cdot0=\lambda T(u)+\mu T(v)$. It satisfies the linearity properties.
