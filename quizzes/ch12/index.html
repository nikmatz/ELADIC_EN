10 questions on L’Hôpital's rule, Newton's method, asymptotes and curve sketching.

Question 1 / 10

L'Hôpital's rule applies when $\lim_{x\to a}\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ has the form:

✓ Answer: b) $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$
L'Hôpital's rule applies to the indeterminate forms $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$. The forms $0\cdot\infty$ and $\infty-\infty$ must first be rewritten as quotients.
Question 2 / 10

$\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{e^x-1}{x}=$

✓ Answer: c) 1
$\frac{0}{0}$: L'Hôpital: $\lim\frac{e^x}{1}=e^0=1$.
Question 3 / 10

Newton's method for roots uses the iterative formula $x_{n+1}=x_n-\dfrac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$.

✓ Answer: Correct
This is the Newton–Raphson formula for finding a root numerically.
Question 4 / 10

A horizontal asymptote $y=L$ exists if:

✓ Answer: b) $\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}f(x)=L$
Horizontal asymptote: $\lim_{x\to+\infty}f(x)=L$ or $\lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x)=L$.
Question 5 / 10

A vertical asymptote $x=a$ exists if:

✓ Answer: b) $\lim_{x\to a}|f(x)|=+\infty$
Vertical asymptote at $a$: $\lim_{x\to a^+}f(x)=\pm\infty$ or $\lim_{x\to a^-}f(x)=\pm\infty$.
Question 6 / 10

$\lim_{x\to0^+}x\ln x = 0$.

✓ Answer: Correct
$\lim_{x\to0^+}x\ln x=\lim\frac{\ln x}{1/x}\overset{L'H}{=}\lim\frac{1/x}{-1/x^2}=\lim(-x)=0$.
Question 7 / 10

An oblique asymptote $y=mx+b$ has $m=\lim_{x\to\infty}$:

✓ Answer: b) $f(x)/x$
$m=\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{f(x)}{x}$ and $b=\lim_{x\to\infty}(f(x)-mx)$.
Question 8 / 10

$\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\sin(3x)}{x}=$

✓ Answer: b) 3
$\lim\frac{\sin(3x)}{x}=3\lim\frac{\sin(3x)}{3x}=3\cdot1=3$.
Question 9 / 10

Newton's method may fail to converge if the initial guess is far from the root.

✓ Answer: Correct
The convergence of Newton's method depends on the initial guess. A poor starting point $\Rightarrow$ divergence, or convergence to a different root.
Question 10 / 10

If $f(x)=x+\dfrac{1}{x}$, what is the vertical asymptote?

✓ Answer: c) $x=0$
The denominator vanishes at $x=0$: $\lim_{x\to0}|f(x)|=\infty$, so $x=0$ is a vertical asymptote.
