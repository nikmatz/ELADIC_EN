Chapter 5 · Eigenvalues

10 questions on the characteristic polynomial, eigenvalues, eigenvectors and eigenspaces.

Question 1 / 10

$\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$ if and only if:

✓ Answer: b) $\det(A-\lambda I)=0$
$\lambda$ is an eigenvalue $\Leftrightarrow$ $Av=\lambda v$ for some $v\neq0$ $\Leftrightarrow$ $\det(A-\lambda I)=0$.
Question 2 / 10

The zero vector can be an eigenvector.

✓ Answer: Incorrect
By definition, an eigenvector is a vector $v\neq0$ such that $Av=\lambda v$.
Question 3 / 10

If $A$ is $3\times3$, what is the degree of its characteristic polynomial?

✓ Answer: c) 3
$\det(A-\lambda I)$ for an $n\times n$ matrix is a polynomial of degree $n$. For $3\times3$: degree 3.
Question 4 / 10

If $A=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&5\end{pmatrix}$, what are its eigenvalues?

✓ Answer: a) 2 and 5
For a diagonal matrix the eigenvalues are the diagonal entries: $\lambda_1=2, \lambda_2=5$.
Question 5 / 10

Two eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent.

✓ Answer: Correct
Eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues are always linearly independent.
Question 6 / 10

The eigenspace $E_\lambda$ of an eigenvalue $\lambda$ is:

✓ Answer: b) $\ker(A-\lambda I)$
$E_\lambda=\ker(A-\lambda I)=\{v: Av=\lambda v\}$.
Question 7 / 10

The sum of the eigenvalues of an $n\times n$ matrix $A$ equals:

✓ Answer: c) $\mathrm{tr}(A)$ (the trace)
$\sum\lambda_i=\mathrm{tr}(A)$ (the sum of the diagonal entries). Also $\prod\lambda_i=\det A$.
Question 8 / 10

An $n\times n$ matrix always has $n$ distinct eigenvalues.

✓ Answer: Incorrect
A matrix may have repeated eigenvalues (multiplicity >1) or complex eigenvalues.
Question 9 / 10

If $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$, then $\lambda^2$ is an eigenvalue of:

✓ Answer: c) $A^2$
If $Av=\lambda v$, then $A^2v=A(Av)=A(\lambda v)=\lambda Av=\lambda^2 v$.
Question 10 / 10

At least how many eigenvalues in ℝ does every real $3\times3$ matrix have?

✓ Answer: b) 1
A characteristic polynomial of degree 3 (odd degree) has at least one real root.
