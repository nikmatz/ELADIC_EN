Linear Vector Spaces

Chapter 1 · Vector Spaces

Self-Assessment

10 questions on the definition of a vector space, subspaces, linear independence, basis and dimension.

Question 1 / 10

The zero vector belongs to every subspace.

✓ Answer: Correct
Every subspace V must contain 0 (closure under vector addition: 0=v+(−v) ∈ V).
Question 2 / 10

Which of the following sets is NOT a subspace of ℝ²?

✓ Answer: c) $\{(x,y): x+y=1\}$
The set $\{(x,y): x+y=1\}$ does not contain (0,0), so it is not a subspace.
Question 3 / 10

Which set is linearly dependent?

✓ Answer: b) $\{(1,2),(2,4)\}$
$(2,4)=2\cdot(1,2)$, so the vectors are linearly dependent.
Question 4 / 10

What is the dimension of the space $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ (square $2\times2$ matrices)?

✓ Answer: c) 4
A basis is $\{E_{11},E_{12},E_{21},E_{22}\}$, with 4 elements. Hence $\dim=4$.
Question 5 / 10

Any basis of ℝⁿ has exactly n vectors.

✓ Answer: Correct
By definition, the dimension of a space is the number of vectors in any of its bases. For ℝⁿ, dim=n.
Question 6 / 10

If $\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}$ is a basis of $V$, then every $v\in V$:

✓ Answer: a) is expressed in a unique way as a linear combination of the $v_i$
A basis provides a unique representation of every element of the space as a linear combination.
Question 7 / 10

The vectors $(1,0,-1),(2,1,0),(0,1,2)$ in ℝ³ are:

✓ Answer: a) linearly dependent
The matrix having them as rows has $\det=0$ (rank 2), so they are linearly dependent: $(0,1,2)=-2(1,0,-1)+(2,1,0)$. Therefore they do NOT form a basis.
Question 8 / 10

The solution space of a homogeneous system $Ax=0$ is a subspace.

✓ Answer: Correct
If $Ax=0$ and $Ay=0$, then $A(x+y)=0$ and $A(\lambda x)=0$, so the solution space is closed under the operations.
Question 9 / 10

If $V$ has dimension $n$, then every set of $n+1$ vectors in $V$ is:

✓ Answer: c) linearly dependent
In a space of dimension $n$, any set of more than $n$ vectors is linearly dependent.
Question 10 / 10

Which is NOT a property required of a vector space?

✓ Answer: c) $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{u}$
An inner product is not required for a vector space. The axioms concern addition and scalar multiplication.
