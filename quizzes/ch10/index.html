Question 1 / 10

The derivative of $f(x)=x^5$ is:

✓ Answer: a) $5x^4$
Power rule: $(x^n)'=nx^{n-1}$. Hence $(x^5)'=5x^4$.
Question 2 / 10

If $f$ is differentiable at $a$, then $f'(a)=\lim_{h\to0}\dfrac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$.

✓ Answer: Correct
This is precisely the definition of the derivative.
Question 3 / 10

$(\sin x)'=$

✓ Answer: b) $\cos x$
$(\sin x)'=\cos x$. $(\cos x)'=-\sin x$.
Question 4 / 10

Chain rule: if $h(x)=f(g(x))$, then $h'(x)=$

✓ Answer: b) $f'(g(x))\cdot g'(x)$
Chain rule: $[f(g(x)))'=f'(g(x))\cdot g'(x)$.
Question 5 / 10

$(e^{3x})'=$

✓ Answer: b) $3e^{3x}$
Chain rule: $(e^{3x})'=e^{3x}\cdot 3=3e^{3x}$.
Question 6 / 10

The derivative of a constant function equals zero.

✓ Answer: Correct
If $f(x)=c$ (constant), then $f'(x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{c-c}{h}=0$.
Question 7 / 10

$(\ln x)'=$

✓ Answer: b) $1/x$
$(\ln x)'=1/x$ for $x>0$.
Question 8 / 10

Product rule: $(f\cdot g)'=$

✓ Answer: c) $f'g+fg'$
$(fg)'=f'g+fg'$ (the Leibniz/product rule).
Question 9 / 10

If $f'$ is continuous at $a$ and $f'(a)>0$, then $f$ is strictly increasing on some neighborhood of $a$.

✓ Answer: Correct
By the continuity of $f'$ at $a$: $f'>0$ on a small interval around $a$ $\Rightarrow$ $f$ is strictly increasing there. Without the continuity of $f'$ the statement is false: $f(x)=\frac{x}{2}+x^2\sin\frac{1}{x}$ (with $f(0)=0$) has $f'(0)=\frac{1}{2}>0$, yet it is not monotone on any neighborhood of $0$.
Question 10 / 10

Which of the following has derivative 0 everywhere?

✓ Answer: c) $f(x)=5$
The constant function $f(x)=5$ has $f'(x)=0$ for every $x$.
