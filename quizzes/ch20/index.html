10 questions on the radius and interval of convergence, Taylor/Maclaurin series and error estimation.

Question 1 / 10

The radius of convergence of $\sum_{n=0}^\infty \dfrac{x^n}{n!}$ is:

✓ Answer: c) $\infty$
$R=\lim\left|\frac{c_n}{c_{n+1}}\right|=\lim(n+1)=\infty$: the series (which equals $e^x$) converges for every $x$.
Question 2 / 10

The Maclaurin expansion of $e^x$ is $\sum_{n=0}^\infty \dfrac{x^n}{n!}$.

✓ Answer: Correct
Since $f^{(k)}(0)=1$ for every $k$, the coefficient of $x^k$ is $1/k!$.
Question 3 / 10

The radius of convergence of the geometric power series $\sum_{n=0}^\infty x^n$ is:

✓ Answer: b) 1
It converges $\Leftrightarrow |x|<1$, with sum $\frac{1}{1-x}$.
Question 4 / 10

In the Taylor polynomial of $f$ about $x_0$, the coefficient of $(x-x_0)^k$ is:

✓ Answer: b) $\dfrac{f^{(k)}(x_0)}{k!}$
$f(x)=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{f^{(k)}(x_0)}{k!}(x-x_0)^k$.
Question 5 / 10

It holds that $\sin x = x - \dfrac{x^3}{3!} + \dfrac{x^5}{5!} - \cdots$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$.

✓ Answer: Correct
The Maclaurin series of $\sin x$ has radius of convergence $\infty$ and the remainder tends to 0 everywhere.
Question 6 / 10

The Lagrange remainder $R_n(x)$ of a Taylor expansion expresses:

✓ Answer: a) the approximation error
$f(x)=P_n(x)+R_n(x)$, with $R_n(x)=\dfrac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}(x-x_0)^{n+1}$.
Question 7 / 10

Inside the interval of convergence, a power series can be differentiated and integrated term by term.

✓ Answer: Correct
The new series has the same radius of convergence $R$.
Question 8 / 10

The series $\arctan x = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \dfrac{(-1)^n x^{2n+1}}{2n+1}$ has radius of convergence:

✓ Answer: a) 1
It arises by integrating $\frac{1}{1+x^2}=\sum(-1)^n x^{2n}$ (radius 1). At $x=1$ it gives the Gregory-Leibniz series for $\pi/4$.
Question 9 / 10

The Maclaurin expansion of $\dfrac{1}{1-x}$ is:

✓ Answer: a) $\sum_{n=0}^\infty x^n$
A geometric series with ratio $x$; valid for $|x|<1$.
Question 10 / 10

For computing $\pi$, Machin's formula converges:

✓ Answer: b) faster
The values $\arctan\frac15,\arctan\frac1{239}$ have a very small argument, so the series converges extremely fast compared with the very slow Gregory-Leibniz series.
