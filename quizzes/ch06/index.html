Diagonalization of Matrices

Chapter 6 · Diagonalization

Self-Assessment

10 questions on diagonalization, symmetric matrices, the SVD and applications.

Question 1 / 10

An $n\times n$ matrix $A$ is diagonalizable if:

✓ Answer: b) it has $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors
$A$ is diagonalizable $\Leftrightarrow$ there exist $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors (equivalently: an invertible $P$ with $P^{-1}AP=D$).
Question 2 / 10

Every real symmetric matrix is orthogonally diagonalizable.

✓ Answer: Correct
Spectral theorem: every real symmetric matrix is orthogonally diagonalizable (its eigenvectors are orthogonal).
Question 3 / 10

If $A=PDP^{-1}$, then $A^5=$

✓ Answer: b) $PD^5P^{-1}$
$A^k=PD^kP^{-1}$ (easy to compute since $D$ is diagonal: $D^k=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1^k,\ldots)$).
Question 4 / 10

Every matrix with eigenvalues 0 and 1 (without repetitions) is diagonalizable.

✓ Answer: Correct
If it has $n$ distinct eigenvalues, then it has $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors, so it is diagonalizable.
Question 5 / 10

In the SVD $A=U\Sigma V^T$, the singular values are:

✓ Answer: b) the square roots of the eigenvalues of $A^TA$
The singular values are $\sigma_i=\sqrt{\lambda_i(A^TA)}\geq 0$. They differ from the eigenvalues (except for a symmetric $A\geq0$).
Question 6 / 10

A $2\times2$ matrix with a single (double) eigenvalue $\lambda$ is diagonalizable if:

✓ Answer: d) if and only if rank$(A-\lambda I)=0$
The matrix is diagonalizable only when the geometric multiplicity of $\lambda$ is $2$, i.e. $\dim\ker(A-\lambda I)=2$, i.e. rank$(A-\lambda I)=0$ and therefore $A=\lambda I$. Option (c) does not hold: $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ has $\det=1\neq0$ and is not diagonalizable, while the zero matrix has $\det=0$ and is already diagonal.
Question 7 / 10

The singular values in the SVD are always non-negative.

✓ Answer: Correct
$\sigma_i=\sqrt{\lambda_i(A^TA)}\geq 0$, since $A^TA$ is positive semidefinite.
Question 8 / 10

For a symmetric $A$, which statement is true?

✓ Answer: a) Eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal
Spectral theorem: for a real symmetric matrix, eigenvectors belonging to distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal.
Question 9 / 10

If $A$ is diagonalizable with eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$, then $\det A=$

✓ Answer: b) $\prod_i\lambda_i$
$\det A=\det(PDP^{-1})=\det D=\prod_i\lambda_i$.
Question 10 / 10

Every orthogonal matrix ($A^TA=I$) has $|\det A|=1$.

✓ Answer: Correct
$1=\det(A^TA)=(\det A)^2$, so $\det A=\pm 1$, i.e. $|\det A|=1$.
