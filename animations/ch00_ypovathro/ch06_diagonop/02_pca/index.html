Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

Chapter 6 · Diagonalization · Eigenvectors of the covariance matrix

Idea: PCA finds the directions of maximum variance of a cloud of points. They are the eigenvectors of the covariance matrix $\Sigma$, and the variance along them is given by the eigenvalues $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2$.
data 1st principal component (PC1) 2nd principal component (PC2)

PCA & the Covariance Matrix

We center the data ($X_c$) and compute the symmetric covariance matrix $\Sigma=\frac{1}{n-1}X_c^\top X_c$.

$\Sigma\,v_i=\lambda_i\,v_i$  ·  PC1 = the eigenvector of the largest $\lambda$

The eigenvectors are orthogonal (since $\Sigma$ is symmetric). The proportion of the variance explained by PC1 is $\lambda_1/(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)$. This is exactly an orthogonal diagonalization $\Sigma=Q\Lambda Q^\top$.
