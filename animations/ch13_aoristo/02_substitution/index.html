Integration by Substitution

Chapter 13 · The Indefinite Integral · u-substitution

Example: $\int_0^b 2x\cos(x^2)\,dx$ with the substitution $u=x^2,\ du=2x\,dx$ becomes $\int_0^{b^2}\cos u\,du$. The two shaded regions are equal.
x-space: $2x\cos(x^2)$
u-space: $\cos u$

Integration by Substitution

The chain rule in reverse:

$\int_a^b f(g(x))\,g'(x)\,dx=\int_{g(a)}^{g(b)} f(u)\,du$

With $u=x^2$ the limits change: $x:0\to b$ becomes $u:0\to b^2$. Both integrals equal $\sin(b^2)$ — the substitution preserves the value (the signed area).
