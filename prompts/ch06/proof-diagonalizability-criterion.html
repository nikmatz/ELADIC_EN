Criteria for Diagonalizability

Proof with the help of a Large Language Model (AI)

StatementLet $A$ be an $(n\times n)$ matrix with entries in a field $\mathbb{F}$ ($\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$). (C1) Fundamental characterization: $A$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb{F}$ if and only if $\mathbb{F}^n$ has a basis consisting of eigenvectors of $A$, that is, if and only if $A$ possesses $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors. (C2) Sufficient condition: if $A$ has $n$ distinct eigenvalues in $\mathbb{F}$, then it is diagonalizable; the condition is sufficient, not necessary (the identity $I_n$ has a single eigenvalue and is already diagonal). (C3) Multiplicity criterion, for repeated eigenvalues: $A$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb{F}$ if and only if $\sum_{\lambda}m_g(\lambda)=n$, where the sum extends over the distinct eigenvalues of $A$ that lie in $\mathbb{F}$; equivalently, if and only if (a) the characteristic polynomial factors completely into linear factors within $\mathbb{F}$ and (b) $m_g(\lambda)=m_a(\lambda)$ for every eigenvalue $\lambda$. The field matters: the matrix of rotation by $90^{\circ}$ is not diagonalizable over $\mathbb{R}$, but it is over $\mathbb{C}$.
