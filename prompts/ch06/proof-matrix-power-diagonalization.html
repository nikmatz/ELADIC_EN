1. What is proved: the statement in the book and in Lean

  • $(PDP^{-1})^{k}=PD^{k}P^{-1}$ for every $k$, when $P$ is invertible
    theorem conj_pow (P D : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ) (hP : P.det ≠ 0) (k : ℕ) :
    (P * D * P⁻¹) ^ k = P * D ^ k * P⁻¹
  • If $A=PDP^{-1}$ with $D$${}=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)$, then $A^{k}$${}=P\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1^{k},\dots,\lambda_n^{k})P^{-1}$
    theorem pow_diagonal (A P : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ) (d : Fin n → ℝ) (hP : P.det ≠ 0)
    (hA : A = P * diagonal d * P⁻¹) (k : ℕ) :
    A ^ k = P * diagonal (fun i => d i ^ k) * P⁻¹

$P$ is invertible (hypothesis hP : P.det ≠ 0), while in the first theorem $D$ can be any matrix. The diagonal matrix $\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)$ is written diagonal d, where d i is the $i$-th diagonal entry. The equality $D^{k}$${}=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1^{k},\dots,\lambda_n^{k})$ is the Mathlib theorem diagonal_pow.

2. Step by step: $(PDP^{-1})^{k}=PD^{k}P^{-1}$, by induction on $k$

The idea: in the product $(PD^{k}P^{-1})(PDP^{-1})$ the $P^{-1}P$ in the middle becomes $I$, and what remains is $PD^{k}DP^{-1}=PD^{k+1}P^{-1}$. We start from the goal:

The goal
n : ℕ
P D : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ
hP : P.det ≠ 0
k : ℕ
⊢ (P * D * P⁻¹) ^ k = P * D ^ k * P⁻¹

Above ⊢ is what we know; after ⊢ is what we have to show. After each step we show only what changed.

  1. induction k with
| zero =>

    Induction on $k$. First the case $k=0$.

    What remains to be proved
    ⊢ (P * D * P⁻¹) ^ 0 = P * D ^ 0 * P⁻¹
  2. rw [pow_zero, pow_zero, mul_one, mul_nonsing_inv P hP.isUnit]

    Both sides are equal to $I$: on the left $(PDP^{-1})^{0}=I$, on the right $PD^{0}P^{-1}=PIP^{-1}=PP^{-1}=I$.

    ✓ This part is complete.
  3. | succ k ih =>

    Inductive step: we assume that $(PDP^{-1})^{k}=PD^{k}P^{-1}$ (hypothesis ih).

    What remains to be proved
    n : ℕ
P D : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ
hP : P.det ≠ 0
k : ℕ
ih : (P * D * P⁻¹) ^ k = P * D ^ k * P⁻¹
⊢ (P * D * P⁻¹) ^ (k + 1) = P * D ^ (k + 1) * P⁻¹
  4. rw [pow_succ, ih]

    $(PDP^{-1})^{k+1}$${}=(PDP^{-1})^{k}(PDP^{-1})$${}=(PD^{k}P^{-1})(PDP^{-1})$.

    What remains to be proved
    ⊢ P * D ^ k * P⁻¹ * (P * D * P⁻¹) = P * D ^ (k + 1) * P⁻¹
  5. simp only [Matrix.mul_assoc]

    We change the parentheses, grouping from the right: $P(D^{k}(P^{-1}(P(DP^{-1}))))$.

    What remains to be proved
    ⊢ P * (D ^ k * (P⁻¹ * (P * (D * P⁻¹)))) = P * (D ^ (k + 1) * P⁻¹)
  6. rw [nonsing_inv_mul_cancel_left P _ hP.isUnit]

    Since $P$ is invertible, $P^{-1}(PX)=X$, and what remains is $P(D^{k}(DP^{-1}))$.

    What remains to be proved
    ⊢ P * (D ^ k * (D * P⁻¹)) = P * (D ^ (k + 1) * P⁻¹)
  7. rw [← Matrix.mul_assoc (D ^ k), ← pow_succ]

    $D^{k}(DP^{-1})$${}=(D^{k}D)P^{-1}$${}=D^{k+1}P^{-1}$, and the two sides become identical.

    ✓ No goals: the proof is complete.

4. In Mathlib

We used the following theorems:

  • Matrix.diagonal_pow: $\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)^{k}$${}=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1^{k},\dots,\lambda_n^{k})$.
  • Matrix.nonsing_inv_mul_cancel_left: $P^{-1}(PX)=X$ for invertible $P$.
  • Matrix.mul_nonsing_inv: $PP^{-1}=I$ for invertible $P$.
  • Matrix.mul_assoc: $(AB)C=A(BC)$.

import Mathlib

namespace Ch6.DynamiPinaka
open Matrix

variable {n : ℕ}

theorem conj_pow (P D : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ) (hP : P.det ≠ 0) (k : ℕ) :
    (P * D * P⁻¹) ^ k = P * D ^ k * P⁻¹ := by
  induction k with
  | zero =>
    rw [pow_zero, pow_zero, mul_one, mul_nonsing_inv P hP.isUnit]
  | succ k ih =>
    rw [pow_succ, ih]
    simp only [Matrix.mul_assoc]
    rw [nonsing_inv_mul_cancel_left P _ hP.isUnit]
    rw [← Matrix.mul_assoc (D ^ k), ← pow_succ]

theorem pow_diagonal (A P : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ) (d : Fin n → ℝ) (hP : P.det ≠ 0)
    (hA : A = P * diagonal d * P⁻¹) (k : ℕ) :
    A ^ k = P * diagonal (fun i => d i ^ k) * P⁻¹ := by
  rw [hA, conj_pow P (diagonal d) hP k, diagonal_pow, Pi.pow_def]

end Ch6.DynamiPinaka
