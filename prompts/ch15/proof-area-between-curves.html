1. What is proved: the statement in the book and in Lean

  • If $f,g$ are continuous on $[\alpha,\beta]$ and $g\le f$, the area of the region between their graphs and the lines $x=\alpha$, $x=\beta$ is $E$${}=\int_\alpha^\beta\bigl[f(x)-g(x)\bigr]\,dx$
    theorem area_between (f g : ℝ → ℝ) (α β : ℝ) (hαβ : α ≤ β)
    (hf : ContinuousOn f (Set.Icc α β)) (hg : ContinuousOn g (Set.Icc α β))
    (hgf : ∀ x ∈ Set.Icc α β, g x ≤ f x) :
    volume (regionBetween g f (Set.Icc α β)) = ENNReal.ofReal (∫ x in α..β, (f x - g x))

In Mathlib, the area of a plane region is its Lebesgue measure, volume. The area is a nonnegative number or $+\infty$ (type ENNReal); that is why the integral is written inside ENNReal.ofReal, which converts it into such a number. The region is the set regionBetween g f (Set.Icc α β) of the points $(x,y)$ with $x\in[\alpha,\beta]$ and $g(x)\lt y\lt f(x)$. Including the boundary curves as well does not change the area. Mathlib gives the area as an integral over the set $[\alpha,\beta]$, and the proof converts it into an integral from $\alpha$ to $\beta$.

2. Where it is in Mathlib

We used the following theorems:

  • volume_regionBetween_eq_integral: the area of the region between two graphs.
  • MeasureTheory.Measure.volume_eq_prod: area in the plane is the product of the length measures on the two axes.
  • ContinuousOn.integrableOn_Icc: a function continuous on a closed interval is integrable there.
  • intervalIntegral.integral_of_le: for $a\le b$, $\int_a^b$ is an integral over $(a,b]$.
  • MeasureTheory.integral_Icc_eq_integral_Ioc: the integrals over $[a,b]$ and $(a,b]$ are equal.
