1. What is proved: the statement in the book and in Lean

  • $(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)^n$${}=\cos(n\theta)+i\sin(n\theta)$, for $n\in\mathbb{N}$
    theorem de_moivre (θ : ℝ) (n : ℕ) :
    (cos θ + sin θ * I) ^ n = cos (n * θ) + sin (n * θ) * I
  • The same for every $n\in\mathbb{Z}$
    theorem de_moivre_int (θ : ℝ) (n : ℤ) :
    (cos θ + sin θ * I) ^ n = cos (n * θ) + sin (n * θ) * I
  • Equivalently: $(e^{i\theta})^n=e^{in\theta}$
    theorem exp_form (θ : ℝ) (n : ℕ) : exp (θ * I) ^ n = exp (n * θ * I)

Here I is the imaginary unit $i$, and exp is the complex exponential (Lean also displays it as cexp). The number $\theta$ is real; when it appears in a complex expression, Lean writes it as ↑θ: the same number, viewed as a complex number.

2. Step by step: induction on $n$

In the book: the statement holds for $n=0$, and if it holds for $k$, then $(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)^{k+1}$${}=(\cos k\theta+i\sin k\theta)(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)$, and the sum formulas give $\cos(k+1)\theta+i\sin(k+1)\theta$. In Lean:

The goal
θ : ℝ
n : ℕ
⊢ (cos ↑θ + sin ↑θ * I) ^ n = cos (↑n * ↑θ) + sin (↑n * ↑θ) * I

Above ⊢ is what we know; after ⊢ is what we have to show. After each step we show only what changed.

  1. induction n with
| zero =>

    The command induction n with starts an induction on $n$. First the base case, $n=0$.

    What remains to be proved
    ⊢ (cos ↑θ + sin ↑θ * I) ^ 0 = cos (↑0 * ↑θ) + sin (↑0 * ↑θ) * I
  2. simp

    Both sides equal $1$: $x^0=1$, $\cos 0=1$, $\sin 0=0$. The tactic simp carries out these simplifications.

    ✓ This part is complete.
  3. | succ k ih =>

    Inductive step. We assume that the statement holds for $k$ (the hypothesis ih) and we want to show that it holds for $k+1$.

    What remains to be proved
    θ : ℝ
k : ℕ
ih : (cos ↑θ + sin ↑θ * I) ^ k = cos (↑k * ↑θ) + sin (↑k * ↑θ) * I
⊢ (cos ↑θ + sin ↑θ * I) ^ (k + 1) = cos (↑(k + 1) * ↑θ) + sin (↑(k + 1) * ↑θ) * I
  4. rw [pow_succ, ih]

    The theorem pow_succ writes $x^{k+1}=x^k\cdot x$, and the hypothesis ih replaces $x^k$ with $\cos k\theta+i\sin k\theta$.

    What remains to be proved
    ⊢ (cos (↑k * ↑θ) + sin (↑k * ↑θ) * I) * (cos ↑θ + sin ↑θ * I) = cos (↑(k + 1) * ↑θ) + sin (↑(k + 1) * ↑θ) * I
  5. have h : ((k + 1 : ℕ) : ℂ) * θ = k * θ + θ := by push_cast; ring

    A small lemma: $(k+1)\theta=k\theta+\theta$. The tactic push_cast takes care of converting the natural number $k+1$ to a complex number.

    What remains to be proved
    h : ↑(k + 1) * ↑θ = ↑k * ↑θ + ↑θ
⊢ (cos (↑k * ↑θ) + sin (↑k * ↑θ) * I) * (cos ↑θ + sin ↑θ * I) = cos (↑(k + 1) * ↑θ) + sin (↑(k + 1) * ↑θ) * I
  6. rw [h, cos_add, sin_add]

    We substitute $k\theta+\theta$ and expand using the sum formulas for cosine and sine.

    What remains to be proved
    ⊢ (cos (↑k * ↑θ) + sin (↑k * ↑θ) * I) * (cos ↑θ + sin ↑θ * I) =
  cos (↑k * ↑θ) * cos ↑θ - sin (↑k * ↑θ) * sin ↑θ + (sin (↑k * ↑θ) * cos ↑θ + cos (↑k * ↑θ) * sin ↑θ) * I
  7. linear_combination (sin (k * θ) * sin θ) * I_sq

    If we carry out the multiplication on the left, the two sides differ only by $\sin k\theta\,\sin\theta\,(i^2+1)$, which is $0$ since $i^2=-1$ (I_sq). The tactic linear_combination checks exactly this.

    ✓ No goals: the proof is complete.

Hint

The theorem exp_natCast_mul says that $e^{nx}=(e^x)^n$. Use it from right to left: rw [← exp_natCast_mul]. What remains is the rearrangement $n(\theta i)=(n\theta)i$, that is, mul_assoc.

Solution
theorem exp_form (θ : ℝ) (n : ℕ) : exp (θ * I) ^ n = exp (n * θ * I) := by
  rw [← exp_natCast_mul, mul_assoc]

import Mathlib

namespace Ch0.DeMoivre
open Complex

theorem de_moivre (θ : ℝ) (n : ℕ) :
    (cos θ + sin θ * I) ^ n = cos (n * θ) + sin (n * θ) * I := by
  induction n with
  | zero =>
    simp
  | succ k ih =>
    rw [pow_succ, ih]
    have h : ((k + 1 : ℕ) : ℂ) * θ = k * θ + θ := by push_cast; ring
    rw [h, cos_add, sin_add]
    linear_combination (sin (k * θ) * sin θ) * I_sq

theorem de_moivre_int (θ : ℝ) (n : ℤ) :
    (cos θ + sin θ * I) ^ n = cos (n * θ) + sin (n * θ) * I := by
  rw [← exp_mul_I, ← exp_mul_I, ← exp_intCast_mul, mul_assoc]

theorem exp_form (θ : ℝ) (n : ℕ) : exp (θ * I) ^ n = exp (n * θ * I) := by
  rw [← exp_natCast_mul, mul_assoc]

end Ch0.DeMoivre
