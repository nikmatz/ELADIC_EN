1. What is proved: the statement in the book and in Lean

  • The vectors $w_1,\dots,w_k$ produced by the process are pairwise orthogonal
    theorem orthogonal {k : ℕ} (v : Fin k → V) {i j : Fin k} (h : i ≠ j) :
    ⟪gramSchmidt ℝ v i, gramSchmidt ℝ v j⟫_ℝ = 0
  • They span the same subspace as $v_1,\dots,v_k$
    theorem same_span {k : ℕ} (v : Fin k → V) :
    span ℝ (Set.range (gramSchmidt ℝ v)) = span ℝ (Set.range v)
  • For linearly independent $v_1,\dots,v_k$, normalization yields an orthonormal family
    theorem orthonormal {k : ℕ} (v : Fin k → V) (h : LinearIndependent ℝ v) :
    Orthonormal ℝ (gramSchmidtNormed ℝ v)

The Gram–Schmidt process is defined in Mathlib: gramSchmidt ℝ v gives the orthogonal vectors $w_1,\dots,w_k$ and gramSchmidtNormed ℝ v the normalized ones. The definition is the same as in the book: from each $v_j$ we subtract its projections onto $w_1,\dots,w_{j-1}$. span ℝ (Set.range v) is the subspace spanned by $v_1,\dots,v_k$. Together, the three theorems say that we obtain an orthonormal basis of the same subspace.

2. Where it is in Mathlib

We used the following theorems:

  • InnerProductSpace.gramSchmidt: the definition of the process.
  • InnerProductSpace.gramSchmidt_orthogonal: the vectors are pairwise orthogonal.
  • InnerProductSpace.span_gramSchmidt: they span the same subspace.
  • InnerProductSpace.gramSchmidtNormed_orthonormal: after normalization they are orthonormal.

variable {V : Type*} [NormedAddCommGroup V] [InnerProductSpace ℝ V]

theorem orthogonal {k : ℕ} (v : Fin k → V) {i j : Fin k} (h : i ≠ j) :
    ⟪gramSchmidt ℝ v i, gramSchmidt ℝ v j⟫_ℝ = 0 :=
  gramSchmidt_orthogonal ℝ v h

theorem same_span {k : ℕ} (v : Fin k → V) :
    span ℝ (Set.range (gramSchmidt ℝ v)) = span ℝ (Set.range v) :=
  span_gramSchmidt ℝ v

theorem orthonormal {k : ℕ} (v : Fin k → V) (h : LinearIndependent ℝ v) :
    Orthonormal ℝ (gramSchmidtNormed ℝ v) :=
  gramSchmidtNormed_orthonormal h
